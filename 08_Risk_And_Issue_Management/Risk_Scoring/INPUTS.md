# Inputs

## Scoring framework

- Approved organizational risk-scoring methodology.
- Probability scale and definitions.
- Impact scale and definitions.
- Risk-rating calculation method.
- Risk matrix and rating bands.
- Risk appetite and tolerance thresholds.
- Time-horizon definitions.
- Inherent-risk and residual-risk requirements.
- Control-effectiveness scale.
- Escalation thresholds.
- Required review and approval authority.
- Rules for handling multiple impact categories.
- Previous scoring methodology, when changed.

## Risk definition

For each risk:

- Risk ID and title.
- Cause–event–impact statement.
- Initiative, program, strategic objective, deliverable, system, or operation affected.
- Risk category.
- Date identified.
- Risk owner.
- Source and date last reviewed.
- Current status and trend.
- Time horizon.
- Early-warning indicators and triggers.
- Related risks, issues, assumptions, and dependencies.

## Probability evidence

- Relevant historical events.
- Frequency of comparable events.
- Current operating conditions.
- Known vulnerabilities or exposure.
- Dependency reliability.
- Vendor or partner performance.
- Control failures or near misses.
- Expert assessments.
- Scenario assumptions.
- Time period over which probability is being assessed.
- Confidence and limitations of the probability evidence.

## Impact evidence

Assess applicable dimensions separately:

- Strategic objectives.
- Operations and service delivery.
- Financial performance.
- Schedule and delivery.
- Clinical services or patient safety.
- Workforce and labor.
- Technology, architecture, integration, and availability.
- Cybersecurity, privacy, data, and records management.
- Legal, regulatory, policy, and compliance.
- Reputation and stakeholder trust.
- Business continuity and recovery.
- Environmental or sustainability impact.

For each applicable dimension:

- Best-case consequence.
- Expected consequence.
- Reasonable worst-case consequence.
- Duration.
- Geographic or organizational extent.
- Reversibility.
- Number or type of stakeholders affected.
- Supporting evidence and confidence level.

## Risk characteristics

- Velocity: how quickly impact could occur.
- Detectability: likelihood the risk would be detected before impact.
- Persistence: how long the consequences may continue.
- Proximity: when the event might occur.
- Interdependency: connection with other risks or systems.
- Concentration: common exposure across initiatives or operations.
- Recoverability.
- Uncertainty and quality of evidence.

## Existing controls

For each control:

- Control description.
- Control type.
- Control owner.
- Implementation status.
- Scope and coverage.
- Frequency.
- Evidence of operation.
- Last test or review date.
- Effectiveness rating.
- Known gaps or limitations.
- Assurance, audit, or validation findings.

## Planned mitigation

- Mitigation action.
- Intended effect on probability or impact.
- Owner.
- Due date.
- Implementation status.
- Evidence of completion.
- Expected effectiveness.
- Dependencies.
- Resource and approval requirements.

Planned mitigation must be recorded separately from existing controls.

## Previous assessment

- Previous probability score.
- Previous impact score.
- Previous inherent rating.
- Previous residual rating.
- Previous control-effectiveness assessment.
- Previous trend.
- Date and assessor.
- Reason for any prior change.
- Previous acceptance or escalation decision.
