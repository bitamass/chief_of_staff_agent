# Skill: Risk Scoring

## Purpose

This skill applies an approved risk-scoring method consistently to documented risks and explains the evidence behind each score.

It helps leadership understand:

- The likelihood that a risk event may occur.
- The magnitude of potential consequences.
- The exposure before controls.
- Whether controls are operating effectively.
- The exposure remaining after controls.
- Whether residual risk exceeds approved tolerance.
- How the assessment has changed.
- How confident decision-makers should be in the score.

The skill supports consistent assessment. It does not create organizational risk appetite, approve risk acceptance, certify control effectiveness, or replace specialized professional judgment.

## Core concepts

### Probability

Probability represents the likelihood that the risk event will occur within a defined time horizon.

Probability must be based on:

- The approved scale.
- A stated assessment period.
- Available historical, operational, technical, or expert evidence.
- Documented assumptions and limitations.

Avoid vague terms such as “likely” unless they correspond to an approved definition.

### Impact

Impact represents the consequence if the risk event occurs.

Assess relevant dimensions separately. Do not allow a low score in one category to dilute a catastrophic or mandatory concern in another category.

Use the organization’s approved aggregation rule. If none exists, present the impact dimensions separately and flag the need for human determination.

### Inherent risk

Exposure before considering controls or mitigation.

Inherent scoring should reflect the environment in which the risk exists without credit for current controls.

### Control effectiveness

The degree to which an implemented control reduces probability or impact.

A control may be assessed as:

- Not implemented.
- Partially implemented.
- Implemented but untested.
- Operating with limitations.
- Operating effectively.
- Ineffective or failed.

Use approved definitions when available.

### Residual risk

Exposure remaining after considering implemented and operating controls.

Do not credit:

- Planned controls.
- Draft procedures.
- Unfunded mitigations.
- Controls without evidence of operation.
- Actions that have not yet been implemented.

### Target risk

The expected future exposure after planned mitigation is implemented and validated.

Target risk is a forecast, not the current residual risk.

### Velocity

How quickly significant impact could occur after the event begins.

### Detectability

The likelihood that the risk would be identified early enough for effective intervention.

### Confidence

The reliability of the evidence supporting the assessment.

A high score with weak evidence and a high score with strong evidence may require different management responses. Confidence must therefore be visible.

## Core activities

### 1. Validate the risk statement

Confirm that:

- The risk describes an uncertain future event.
- The cause, event, and impact are distinguishable.
- The affected objective is identified.
- The assessment horizon is defined.
- The risk owner and source are known.
- The item is not actually a current issue, dependency, action, or assumption.

If the event has occurred, route it to issue or incident management.

### 2. Select the approved methodology

- Use the organization’s approved probability and impact scales.
- Use the approved calculation and matrix.
- Use approved tolerance thresholds.
- Record the framework version.
- Do not combine scores from different frameworks.
- Do not silently invent institutional thresholds.

For demonstration data without an approved institutional method, label the method as provisional or illustrative.

### 3. Score inherent probability

Evaluate conditions before controls:

- Exposure.
- Frequency.
- Vulnerability.
- Historical events.
- Dependencies.
- Time horizon.
- Scenario assumptions.

Document the evidence and rationale. Do not rely on a number without explanation.

### 4. Score inherent impact

Assess each relevant impact dimension.

For each dimension:

- Identify the consequence.
- Identify who or what is affected.
- Estimate extent and duration.
- Consider reversibility.
- Support the score with evidence.
- State uncertainty.

Apply the approved rule for determining overall impact. If the method uses the highest material dimension, state which dimension determined the score.
