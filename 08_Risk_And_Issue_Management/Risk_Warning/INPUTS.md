# Inputs

## Monitoring scope

- Initiative, program, portfolio, system, service, operation, or strategic objective being monitored.
- Monitoring purpose and intended audience.
- Reporting and warning period.
- Approved risk categories.
- Risk appetite and tolerance thresholds.
- Warning levels and escalation criteria.
- Required notification recipients.
- Required response times.
- Relevant governance, incident-response, and specialized review processes.
- Data cutoff date and monitoring cadence.

## Active risk information

For each monitored risk:

- Risk ID and title.
- Cause–event–impact statement.
- Risk owner.
- Affected objective or operation.
- Inherent and residual risk ratings.
- Current trend.
- Velocity, proximity, and detectability.
- Time horizon.
- Existing controls.
- Mitigation actions.
- Trigger conditions and thresholds.
- Last review date.
- Source and confidence level.

## Early-warning indicators

For each indicator:

- Indicator name and description.
- Risk or objective connected to the indicator.
- Indicator owner.
- Data source.
- Measurement frequency.
- Current value.
- Baseline value.
- Target or expected range.
- Watch threshold.
- Warning threshold.
- Critical threshold.
- Direction of concern.
- Rate of change.
- Duration or persistence required before triggering.
- Data-quality and timeliness requirements.
- Known seasonal or operational variation.

## Delivery and performance signals

- Missed or threatened milestones.
- Schedule variance.
- Budget variance.
- Resource or capacity shortfalls.
- Quality failures.
- Repeated defects or rework.
- Overdue actions or decisions.
- Changes in scope.
- Declining adoption or stakeholder participation.
- Vendor or dependency delays.
- Control failures.
- Service availability or performance degradation.
- Backlog growth.
- Forecast changes.
- Increased exception volume.

## Meeting and communication signals

- New concerns raised in meetings.
- Repeated unresolved questions.
- Decisions deferred.
- Conflicting leadership direction.
- Unfulfilled commitments.
- Changes in stakeholder support.
- Escalations or complaints.
- Emerging themes in status reports, meeting notes, or stakeholder communications.
- Gaps between reported status and supporting evidence.

## External and contextual signals

When approved for use:

- Regulatory or policy developments.
- Vendor announcements.
- Market or financial changes.
- Technology developments.
- Cybersecurity advisories.
- Environmental or operational events.
- Legal or compliance developments.
- Partner or supply-chain changes.
- Relevant public or industry information.

## Control and mitigation signals

- Control-test results.
- Control exceptions.
- Failed or missed control activities.
- Mitigation progress.
- Overdue mitigation actions.
- Evidence that controls are not operating as designed.
- Changes in residual exposure.
- New dependencies created by mitigation.
- Contingency readiness.

## Historical and comparative data

- Previous indicator values.
- Historical warning events.
- Prior risk realizations.
- Near misses.
- Normal operating ranges.
- Seasonal patterns.
- Previous false positives and false negatives.
- Lessons learned.
- Prior warning responses and outcomes.

## Data-quality information

- Source owner.
- Collection date and time.
- Completeness.
- Accuracy.
- Timeliness.
- Known gaps or outages.
- Conflicting sources.
- Confidence level.
- Items requiring human validation.
