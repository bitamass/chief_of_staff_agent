# Skill: Issue and Risk Dashboard

## Purpose

This skill converts approved risk, issue, control, mitigation, decision, and action data into an executive-level dashboard.

It helps leadership understand:

- What might happen and what has already happened.
- Which exposures are most significant.
- Whether risks remain within approved tolerance.
- Which issues require urgent resolution.
- Whether controls and mitigations are working.
- Where ownership, action, or decision-making is missing.
- How the risk and issue profile has changed.
- Which matters require executive, governance, or specialized review.

The dashboard is a decision-support view. It does not replace formal risk management, incident response, audit, compliance, clinical, privacy, security, legal, or regulatory processes.

## Core definitions

### Risk

A risk is an uncertain future event or condition that may affect an objective.

Write risks in cause–event–impact form:

- **Cause:** the circumstance creating exposure.
- **Event:** what may occur.
- **Impact:** the consequence if it occurs.

Example structure:

> Because of [cause], there is a possibility that [event], resulting in [impact].

### Issue

An issue is a condition or event that has already occurred and requires management or resolution.

An issue statement should describe:

- What happened.
- What is currently affected.
- What containment or resolution is underway.
- What further consequence may occur if unresolved.

### Inherent risk

Risk exposure before considering controls or mitigation.

### Residual risk

Risk exposure remaining after considering existing controls and planned or completed mitigation.

### Severity

The magnitude of an issue’s current or potential consequence.

### Urgency

How quickly a response or decision is required.

Risk, issue, severity, urgency, and priority must not be used interchangeably.

## Core activities

### 1. Validate and classify records

For every record:

- Determine whether it is a risk, issue, dependency, assumption, action, or decision.
- Confirm the owner.
- Confirm the affected objective or operation.
- Confirm dates and current status.
- Identify the supporting source.
- Flag missing or contradictory information.
- Detect possible duplicates.

A future uncertainty should not be recorded as an issue. A condition that has already occurred should not remain classified only as a risk.

### 2. Standardize risk statements

For each risk:

- Identify the cause.
- State the uncertain event.
- Describe the potential impact.
- Identify the affected objectives.
- Separate the risk statement from its mitigation.
- Avoid vague labels such as “security risk,” “resource risk,” or “schedule risk” without explaining the exposure.

### 3. Standardize issue statements

For each issue:

- Describe the current condition.
- State the observed impact.
- Identify containment already in place.
- Identify the resolution plan.
- Record the owner and target date.
- Distinguish a confirmed root cause from a suspected cause.

### 4. Apply the approved scoring method

Use the organization’s defined probability, impact, severity, and risk-rating scales.

When no approved method is provided:

- Do not silently invent organizational thresholds.
- Use clearly labeled demonstration or provisional ratings.
- Explain the method applied.
- Flag the rating for human confirmation.

Do not calculate an overall exposure score by combining incomparable categories without an approved methodology.

### 5. Evaluate controls and mitigation

For each material risk:

- Identify existing controls.
- Determine whether evidence of control operation exists.
- Record control owner and effectiveness.
- Identify mitigation actions and due dates.
- Compare inherent and residual exposure.
- Verify whether completed mitigation justifies a lower rating.

A mitigation plan does not reduce risk merely because it exists. Reduction should reflect implementation and evidence of effectiveness.

### 6. Evaluate issue response

For each material issue:

- Confirm containment.
- Identify the resolution owner.
- Assess the resolution plan.
- Identify dependencies.
- Determine whether target dates are credible.
- Identify escalation or decision requirements.
- Track whether the issue is improving, stable, or worsening.

### 7. Analyze trends

Compare with the previous reporting period:

- New risks and issues.
- Closed or resolved items.
- Rating changes.
- Trend changes.
- Risks exceeding tolerance.
- Issues aging beyond target dates.
- Mitigation actions becoming overdue.
- Risks converting into issues.
- Repeated control failures.
- Recurring causes or systemic patterns.

Explain the evidence behind reported trends.

### 8. Prioritize executive attention

Elevate matters requiring:

- Risk acceptance.
- Resource or funding decisions.
- Priority resolution.
- Cross-organizational intervention.
- Governance review.
- Legal, regulatory, security, privacy, clinical, financial, or audit action.
- Incident or crisis response.
- Approval of a recovery or mitigation plan.

Each executive-attention item should specify the requested action, authorized decision-maker, and required date.

### 9. Review closure

A risk may be closed when:

- The exposure no longer exists.
- The relevant objective or activity ended.
- The risk was transferred or formally accepted under an authorized process.
- Residual exposure is handled through another approved mechanism.

An issue may be closed when:

- The condition has been resolved.
- Required corrective actions are complete.
- The authorized owner validates resolution.
- Residual risks are documented.
- Required evidence exists.

Do not close an item merely because it disappeared from a current report.

## Decision rules

- Distinguish future risks from current issues.
- Distinguish impact from probability.
- Distinguish severity from urgency.
- Preserve inherent and residual ratings separately.
- Apply approved scoring and tolerance definitions.
- Do not lower a rating because mitigation is merely planned.
- Do not treat a control as effective without evidence.
- Do not report an item closed without validation.
- Do not average risk scores across unrelated categories.
- Do not interpret a large number of low risks as equivalent to one critical exposure.
- Identify correlated or concentrated risks when evidence supports the relationship.
- Separate facts, owner assessments, and analytical recommendations.
- Clearly label provisional ratings and assumptions.

## Quality standards

A strong issue and risk dashboard must be:

- Accurate: records are correctly classified.
- Current: status is tied to a defined cutoff date.
- Comparable: trends use consistent definitions.
- Evidence-based: ratings, controls, and closures connect to sources.
- Tolerance-aware: exposure is compared with approved thresholds.
- Action-oriented: required decisions and interventions are explicit.
- Concise: executives can quickly identify material exposure.
- Transparent: data gaps and disagreements are visible.
- Traceable: changes in status, ratings, ownership, and closure are documented.
- Governed: specialized processes and approval rights are respected.

## Safeguards and limitations

- Do not fabricate risks, issues, ratings, owners, controls, impacts, or mitigations.
- Do not provide definitive legal, regulatory, clinical, financial, privacy, security, or compliance determinations.
- Do not suppress, downgrade, or close material items without evidence and authorization.
- Do not expose sensitive risk or incident information to unauthorized audiences.
- Do not automatically accept risk, approve mitigation, escalate incidents, or notify stakeholders.
- Do not replace required incident-response or regulatory-reporting processes.
- Flag stale, incomplete, conflicting, or unverified data.
- Require human review before the dashboard is treated as an official risk record.

## Human role

The risk owner, issue owner, program lead, control owner, governance body, or authorized executive must:

- Validate classifications and descriptions.
- Confirm ratings and trends.
- Verify control and mitigation effectiveness.
- Determine risk tolerance and acceptance.
- Approve escalation.
- Confirm issue resolution and closure.
- Authorize distribution.
- Ensure required specialized processes are followed.
