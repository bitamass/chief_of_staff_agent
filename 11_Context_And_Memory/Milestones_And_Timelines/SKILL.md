# Skill: Milestones and Timelines

## Purpose

This skill creates and maintains a source-linked chronological record of important meetings, decisions, approvals, actions, commitments, deliverables, milestones, risks, issues, and organizational changes.

It helps leadership understand:

- What happened.
- When it happened.
- What decisions and commitments shaped the current state.
- How milestones and forecasts changed.
- What is happening now.
- What must happen next.
- Which dates, decisions, dependencies, or approvals require attention.
- Where records conflict or lack sufficient evidence.

This skill provides historical and forward-looking context. It does not alter schedules, approve milestones, make decisions, or certify official records.

## Core concepts

### Event

A dated occurrence relevant to the initiative, decision, organization, or outcome.

### Milestone

A significant checkpoint, decision, approval, completion point, or outcome with defined criteria.

### Baseline date

The originally approved planned date.

### Approved revised date

A new baseline authorized through an approved change process.

### Forecast date

The current estimate of when the event or milestone will occur.

### Actual date

The verified date on which the event or milestone occurred.

### Effective date

The date on which a decision, policy, approval, contract, or organizational change takes effect.

These date types must remain distinct.

## Core activities

### 1. Define the timeline scope

Confirm:

- Subject.
- Start and end dates.
- Intended audience.
- Required level of detail.
- Reporting cutoff date.
- Authoritative sources.
- Whether historical, current, future, or complete lifecycle context is required.

### 2. Extract dated events

Identify material events from:

- Calendars.
- Meeting records.
- Decision logs.
- Action and commitment trackers.
- Charters and roadmaps.
- Status reports.
- Governance records.
- Risk and issue registers.
- Contracts and policies.
- Communications.
- Approved external sources.

For every event:

- Record the date.
- Record the event type.
- Identify the source.
- Identify participants or owners.
- Identify the outcome.
- Link related records.
- Assign a confidence level.

### 3. Normalize dates

Classify every date as:

- Actual.
- Baseline.
- Approved revised baseline.
- Current forecast.
- Estimated.
- Required-by.
- Effective.
- Expiration.
- Unknown.

Do not present forecast or estimated dates as confirmed actual dates.

### 4. Sequence events

Order events chronologically while preserving:

- Predecessor relationships.
- Successor relationships.
- Decision dependencies.
- Approval requirements.
- Milestone criteria.
- Lead and lag time.
- Effective dates.

If the documented sequence is inconsistent, flag it rather than silently correcting history.

### 5. Connect history to current context

For the current state, identify:

- Prior decisions still in effect.
- Open commitments.
- Unresolved actions.
- Historical risks or issues still relevant.
- Approved scope or strategy changes.
- Milestones that moved.
- Conditions attached to prior approvals.

Avoid providing a chronological list without explaining which events still matter.

### 6. Prepare the forward view

Identify:

- Upcoming meetings.
- Decisions.
- Approvals.
- Actions.
- Deliverables.
- Milestones.
- Governance reviews.
- External deadlines.
- Risk triggers.
- Contingency dates.

For each event:

- Identify owner.
- Identify required preparation.
- Identify dependencies.
- Identify consequence of delay.
- Identify confidence.

### 7. Preserve change history

When dates or plans change:

- Preserve the original.
- Record the revised date.
- Identify who authorized the change.
- Record the reason.
- Identify affected downstream events.
- Link the supporting decision.

Do not overwrite history.

### 8. Validate milestones

A milestone should be marked achieved only when:

- Completion criteria are satisfied.
- Required evidence exists.
- Required review or approval occurred.
- Authorized validation exists.

Do not infer achievement from a meeting, draft submission, or owner statement alone.

### 9. Detect timeline inconsistencies

Identify:

- Conflicting dates.
- Missing predecessor events.
- Decisions recorded after dependent work began.
- Approvals missing before implementation.
- Impossible or unlikely sequencing.
- Milestones lacking criteria.
- Forecasts inconsistent with dependencies.
- Duplicate records.
- Time-zone errors.
- Missing sources.

### 10. Construct historical narrative

When requested, explain:

- Initial purpose.
- Key turning points.
- Decisions.
- Changes in direction.
- Consequences.
- Current unresolved matters.
- Upcoming decision points.

The narrative must remain source-linked and distinguish fact from inference.

### 11. Maintain temporal relevance

Evaluate whether historical context is:

- Still valid.
- Superseded.
- Expired.
- Replaced by a later decision.
- Relevant only to a prior phase.
- Material to a current or future decision.

Do not treat all historical information as equally relevant.

## Decision rules

- Preserve actual, planned, forecast, effective, and estimated dates separately.
- Use a defined reporting cutoff date.
- Maintain original baselines.
- Do not overwrite historical records.
- Do not infer completion or approval.
- Link events to sources.
- Distinguish current decisions from superseded decisions.
- Distinguish open commitments from completed or cancelled commitments.
- Identify sequencing conflicts.
- Mark uncertain dates clearly.
- Include only events material to the stated scope.
- Separate verified facts from analytical inference.

## Quality standards

A strong milestone and timeline output must be:

- Chronological.
- Source-linked.
- Date-type aware.
- Historically accurate.
- Current.
- Forward-looking.
- Dependency-aware.
- Change-aware.
- Confidence-aware.
- Decision-relevant.

## Safeguards and limitations

- Do not fabricate dates, events, decisions, milestones, owners, or sources.
- Do not change schedules, forecasts, or official records.
- Do not represent estimated dates as confirmed.
- Do not treat generated history as an official institutional record without validation.
- Do not expose confidential, privileged, personnel, clinical, legal, security, privacy, financial, research, or procurement information to unauthorized audiences.
- Flag incomplete, conflicting, stale, or low-confidence information.
- Require human confirmation for consequential historical or future claims.

## Human role

The initiative owner, program manager, record owner, decision owner, governance partner, executive sponsor, or authorized historian must:

- Confirm timeline scope.
- Validate authoritative sources.
- Resolve date conflicts.
- Approve baseline changes.
- Confirm decision and milestone history.
- Validate future forecasts.
- Approve official use or distribution.
