# Skill: Strategic Priorities

## Purpose

This skill identifies, organizes, preserves, and explains the organization’s approved strategic priorities and connects them to objectives, measures, initiatives, resources, decisions, outcomes, and benefits.

It helps leadership understand:

- What the organization has formally prioritized.
- Why each priority matters.
- Who owns it.
- How success is measured.
- Which initiatives and investments support it.
- Whether resources match stated priorities.
- Where priorities conflict or overlap.
- What changed over time.
- Which priorities require intervention, clarification, or reconsideration.

The skill maintains strategic context and institutional memory. It does not establish, approve, rank, revise, or retire strategic priorities automatically.

## Core distinctions

### Mission

The organization’s enduring purpose.

### Vision

The desired future state.

### Strategic theme

A broad area of strategic focus.

### Strategic priority

An approved outcome or direction requiring organizational focus and investment.

### Objective

A specific outcome that supports a strategic priority.

### Key result or measure

A measurable indicator of progress or achievement.

### Initiative

A program, project, or body of work intended to influence an objective or key result.

### Deliverable

A defined output produced by an initiative.

### Outcome

A measurable change produced by the work.

### Benefit

The strategic, operational, financial, clinical, workforce, research, stakeholder, risk-reduction, or capability value produced by an outcome.

These concepts should remain distinct.

## Core activities

### 1. Identify authoritative priorities

Retrieve priorities from approved:

- Strategic plans.
- Board decisions.
- Executive decisions.
- Annual operating plans.
- Governance records.
- Public commitments.
- Budget decisions.
- Policies.
- Regulatory or contractual obligations.

For every priority:

- Confirm wording.
- Confirm approval.
- Confirm owner.
- Confirm planning period.
- Confirm source and version.
- Confirm current status.

Do not treat recurring executive comments or informal preferences as approved strategic priorities without evidence.

### 2. Normalize the priority record

For each priority:

- Preserve the approved wording.
- Create a concise description.
- Identify intended outcome.
- Identify sponsor and owner.
- Identify timeframe.
- Identify measures.
- Identify supporting initiatives.
- Identify resources.
- Identify risks and dependencies.
- Identify review cadence.

### 3. Build the strategic hierarchy

Map:

Mission  
→ Strategic theme  
→ Priority  
→ Objective  
→ Key result  
→ Initiative  
→ Deliverable  
→ Outcome  
→ Benefit

Identify:

- Missing connections.
- Weak links.
- Unsupported alignment claims.
- Priorities without delivery mechanisms.
- Initiatives without strategic purpose.

### 4. Validate outcome measures

For each priority:

- Identify baseline.
- Identify target.
- Identify data source.
- Identify owner.
- Identify reporting period.
- Assess whether measures represent outcomes.
- Distinguish activity, output, outcome, and benefit.
- Identify evidence gaps.

A priority should not be considered measurable merely because associated activities are counted.

### 5. Assess initiative support

For each initiative:

- Identify the priority supported.
- Describe expected contribution.
- Evaluate strength of connection.
- Review current evidence.
- Identify overlap or conflict.
- Identify resources consumed.
- Identify expected and realized benefit.

Do not infer alignment from similar terminology alone.

### 6. Assess resource alignment

Compare stated priorities with:

- Funding.
- Staffing.
- Technology investment.
- Leadership attention.
- Governance capacity.
- Change capacity.
- Vendor and partner commitments.

Identify when resource allocation contradicts stated strategic priorities.

### 7. Identify priority conflicts

Detect:

- Competing resource demands.
- Conflicting outcomes.
- Contradictory measures.
- Timing conflicts.
- Duplicate or overlapping priorities.
- Mandatory obligations competing with discretionary work.
- Short-term and long-term trade-offs.

Present options and trade-offs. Do not silently resolve the conflict.

### 8. Monitor progress and confidence

For every priority:

- Review current measures.
- Review supporting initiative status.
- Review risks and dependencies.
- Review resource sufficiency.
- Review forecast.
- Assess confidence.
- Compare with the previous review.

Progress, status, and confidence should remain distinct.

### 9. Track strategic change

When priorities change:

- Preserve previous wording.
- Record revised wording.
- Record approving authority.
- Record effective date.
- Explain rationale.
- Identify affected initiatives and resources.
- Identify measures requiring revision.
- Identify communication and implementation needs.

Do not overwrite strategic history.

### 10. Assess continuing relevance

At each review, consider whether the priority:

- Remains aligned with mission.
- Remains approved.
- Remains sufficiently important.
- Has achieved its intended outcome.
- Has been superseded.
- Requires revision because of changed conditions.
- Requires continued investment.
- Should inform the next planning cycle.

The skill may recommend review but cannot revise or retire the priority.

### 11. Prepare leadership attention

Elevate:

- Priority conflicts.
- Resource misalignment.
- Missing ownership.
- Weak or missing measures.
- Strategic commitments at risk.
- Initiatives disconnected from strategy.
- External developments requiring review.
- Decisions required.

For every item, identify the decision-maker and deadline.

## Decision rules

- Use current approved strategy.
- Preserve exact approved priority wording.
- Distinguish approved priorities from informal preferences.
- Connect priorities to outcomes and measures.
- Distinguish activity, output, outcome, and benefit.
- Do not infer alignment from shared terminology.
- Compare resource allocation with stated priorities.
- Identify mandatory obligations separately.
- Do not rank or revise priorities without authorization.
- Preserve strategic history.
- Label assumptions, conflicts, and weak evidence.
- Separate analysis and recommendation from approved strategy.

## Quality standards

A strong strategic-priority output must be:

- Authoritative.
- Current.
- Hierarchical.
- Outcome-oriented.
- Measurable.
- Initiative-linked.
- Resource-aware.
- Conflict-aware.
- Traceable.
- Decision-relevant.

## Safeguards and limitations

- Do not fabricate strategic priorities, ownership, measures, alignment, outcomes, or executive direction.
- Do not approve, rank, revise, or retire priorities.
- Do not present informal statements as approved strategy.
- Do not expose confidential strategy, personnel, financial, legal, clinical, security, privacy, procurement, or vendor information to unauthorized audiences.
- Do not automatically reallocate funding, staff, or initiatives.
- Flag stale, conflicting, incomplete, or low-confidence information.
- Require authorized leadership and governance review.

## Human role

The board or governing body, executive leadership, strategy lead, priority owner, portfolio lead, finance and resource partners, initiative owners, data owners, and authorized governance partners must:

- Confirm approved priorities.
- Validate ownership and measures.
- Review initiative alignment.
- Resolve priority conflicts.
- Approve resource allocation.
- Authorize strategic changes.
- Confirm progress and continuing relevance.
