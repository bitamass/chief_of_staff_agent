# Skill: Prior Decisions

## Purpose

This skill retrieves, organizes, validates, and explains prior decisions relevant to a current executive question, initiative, policy, risk, approval, or organizational action.

It helps leadership understand:

- What was previously decided.
- Who possessed the authority to decide.
- Why the decision was made.
- Which options and trade-offs were considered.
- What conditions or restrictions applied.
- Whether the decision was implemented.
- What outcome resulted.
- Whether the decision remains valid.
- How changes in context affect its relevance.
- Whether the prior decision should inform, constrain, or be reconsidered in the current situation.

The skill provides institutional decision memory. It does not treat every prior decision as binding precedent or make new decisions automatically.

## Core distinctions

### Discussion

An exchange of views without a documented final decision.

### Recommendation

Advice or a proposed course of action submitted to an authorized decision-maker.

### Decision

A documented selection, direction, approval, rejection, deferral, or other determination made by an authorized person or body.

### Approval

Permission granted within a defined scope and under specified conditions.

### Conditional approval

Approval subject to documented requirements that remain enforceable.

### Precedent

A prior decision that may inform a current decision because of meaningful similarity.

### Controlling decision

The most current valid decision governing the matter.

These categories must not be used interchangeably.

## Core activities

### 1. Define the current decision question

Confirm:

- What decision or issue requires historical context.
- Scope.
- Decision-maker.
- Required date.
- Strategic and operational context.
- Relevant time period.
- Source repositories.
- Confidentiality restrictions.

Do not retrieve prior decisions without a defined current purpose.

### 2. Identify potential prior decisions

Search approved:

- Decision logs.
- Meeting minutes.
- Resolutions.
- Governance records.
- Approval records.
- Policies.
- Executive communications.
- Contracts.
- Initiative charters.
- Status reports.
- Action and commitment records.

Treat keywords and topic similarity as discovery aids—not proof of relevance.

### 3. Validate that a decision occurred

Confirm:

- Clear decision statement.
- Decision date.
- Authorized decision-maker.
- Source.
- Status.
- Scope.
- Conditions.
- Approval or record status.

Do not treat discussion, recommendation, silence, meeting attendance, or draft language as a decision.

### 4. Validate decision authority

Determine:

- Who possessed authority at the time.
- Source of authority.
- Approval thresholds.
- Quorum or voting requirements.
- Required consultation.
- Delegation.
- Effective dates.

If authority is unclear, label the record for governance or specialized review rather than declaring it invalid.

### 5. Capture rationale and evidence

Record:

- Problem or opportunity.
- Options considered.
- Evaluation criteria.
- Evidence.
- Recommendation.
- Rationale.
- Trade-offs.
- Risks.
- Assumptions.
- Stakeholder considerations.

Do not invent rationale when the source records only the final decision.

### 6. Preserve conditions and scope

Identify:

- Approved scope.
- Restrictions.
- Safeguards.
- Required monitoring.
- Expiration.
- Renewal.
- Conditions.
- Material-change triggers.

Do not describe a limited or conditional approval as unrestricted.

### 7. Trace implementation and outcomes

Determine:

- Actions assigned.
- Ownership.
- Deliverables.
- Milestones.
- Implementation status.
- Outcome evidence.
- Benefits.
- Issues and unintended consequences.
- Lessons learned.

A decision may have been valid but poorly implemented. Keep decision quality and implementation performance distinct.

### 8. Determine current validity

Classify the decision as:

- Current.
- Current with conditions.
- Partially applicable.
- Expired.
- Superseded.
- Reversed.
- Under review.
- No longer relevant.
- Insufficient evidence.

Consider:

- Subsequent decisions.
- Policy changes.
- Organizational changes.
- Technology or data changes.
- Legal or regulatory changes.
- Changed risks.
- Changed stakeholders.
- Changed scope.

### 9. Assess precedent relevance

Compare the prior and current situations across:

- Objective.
- Scope.
- Decision authority.
- Stakeholders.
- Data.
- Technology.
- Risk.
- Policy.
- Timing.
- Resources.
- Expected outcome.

A prior decision should not be applied merely because the topic or terminology is similar.

### 10. Detect conflicting decisions

Identify:

- Decisions that appear inconsistent.
- Different decisions for similar cases.
- Superseding decisions not implemented.
- Multiple bodies claiming authority.
- Decisions with incompatible conditions.
- Missing rationale for changed direction.

Recommend clarification by the authorized owner or body.

### 11. Construct the decision narrative

Explain:

- What was originally decided.
- Why.
- What happened after the decision.
- What changed.
- Which later decision controls.
- What remains relevant.
- What the current decision-maker must reconsider.

Keep verified facts separate from analytical inference.

### 12. Preserve decision memory

Maintain:

- Original decision.
- Conditions.
- Sources.
- Implementation actions.
- Outcomes.
- Changes.
- Superseding decisions.
- Current validity.

Do not overwrite earlier records when decisions change.

## Decision rules

- Require an explicit decision statement.
- Confirm authority and source.
- Distinguish discussion, recommendation, decision, and approval.
- Preserve conditions, scope, and expiration.
- Identify superseding decisions.
- Do not assume that a past decision remains current.
- Do not treat precedent as automatically binding.
- Compare the factual context.
- Do not invent rationale.
- Distinguish decision quality from implementation results.
- Preserve contradictory evidence.
- Clearly label inference and uncertainty.
- Separate historical analysis from new decision recommendations.

## Quality standards

A strong prior-decision output must be:

- Authoritative.
- Source-linked.
- Chronological.
- Authority-aware.
- Condition-aware.
- Context-sensitive.
- Outcome-aware.
- Current-validity aware.
- Confidence-aware.
- Decision-relevant.

## Safeguards and limitations

- Do not fabricate decisions, rationale, authority, conditions, outcomes, or precedent.
- Do not make legal, policy, regulatory, clinical, security, privacy, financial, or governance determinations.
- Do not treat generated summaries as official decision records without validation.
- Do not expose confidential, privileged, personnel, clinical, legal, research, financial, security, privacy, or procurement information to unauthorized audiences.
- Do not automatically apply a prior decision to a current matter.
- Flag missing, conflicting, draft, stale, or low-confidence evidence.
- Require authorized review before consequential reliance.

## Human role

The current decision-maker, prior decision owner, governance lead, policy owner, records owner, legal or specialized partner, initiative owner, and executive sponsor must:

- Confirm decision records.
- Validate authority.
- Interpret conditions and current applicability.
- Resolve conflicts.
- Determine precedent relevance.
- Make the current decision.
- Approve official use or distribution.
