# Inputs

## Weekly delivery context

- Week start and end dates, time zone, and working-day assumptions.
- Executive, team, initiative, program, or portfolio in scope.
- Approved weekly priorities and expected outcomes.
- Current strategic objectives, operational responsibilities, and stakeholder commitments.
- Planned leave, holidays, travel, reduced availability, and protected focus periods.
- Review audience and person authorized to confirm the weekly delivery plan.

## Candidate deliverables

For each deliverable:

- Unique identifier and concise name.
- Description of the required work product or result.
- Related strategic goal, weekly priority, initiative, program, decision, meeting, or commitment.
- Business or operational purpose.
- Sponsor, accountable owner, responsible lead, and contributors.
- Original source and source date.
- Current status.
- Planned start date and due date.
- Estimated effort and complexity.
- Required format, quality standard, and delivery location.
- Consequence of delay, incomplete delivery, or rejection.

## Deliverable completion criteria

- Observable conditions required for completion.
- Required content, functionality, or work product.
- Required evidence of completion.
- Review and acceptance criteria.
- Required reviewer or approving authority.
- Quality, performance, or compliance expectations.
- Conditions that would make the deliverable incomplete.
- Definition of draft, ready for review, accepted, and complete.

## Daily availability and capacity

For each of the five working days:

- Available executive and team working hours.
- Existing meeting and operational commitments.
- Preparation, travel, transition, and follow-up time.
- Available focus-work periods.
- Participant and specialist availability.
- Expected routine workload.
- Contingency capacity for urgent work.
- Known scheduling constraints.

## Milestones and sequencing

- Interim milestones required for each deliverable.
- Work that must occur on a specific day.
- Required sequence of activities.
- Predecessor and successor deliverables.
- Review, feedback, revision, and approval periods.
- Handoff requirements.
- Dependencies spanning more than one day.
- Work required this week for a deliverable due later.

## Decisions and approvals

- Decisions or approvals required to begin, advance, or complete a deliverable.
- Decision or approval owner.
- Required response date.
- Available options and recommendation.
- Required evidence or specialist review.
- Current readiness.
- Consequence of delay.
- Meeting or governance process where the decision will occur.

## Resources and ownership

- Responsible owner for each deliverable.
- Accountable person.
- Contributors, reviewers, approvers, and people to consult or inform.
- Required expertise, tools, systems, data, budget, or vendor support.
- Owner capacity and competing priorities.
- Required access or permissions.
- Backup owner or continuity arrangement.
- Cross-team coordination needs.

## Dependencies and prerequisites

- Required source documents or data.
- Predecessor tasks, decisions, approvals, and milestones.
- Required participation from another person or team.
- System, vendor, procurement, or external dependencies.
- Assumptions supporting the delivery plan.
- Downstream meetings, decisions, tasks, or deliverables affected.
- Dependencies that could affect multiple deliverables.

## Risks, issues, and blockers

- Risks or issues affecting delivery.
- Owner, status, severity, and trend.
- Current mitigation, containment, or resolution activity.
- Blockers preventing progress.
- Required authority, information, resource, or decision.
- Escalation threshold and response deadline.
- Effect on schedule, quality, scope, cost, or acceptance.
- Contingency or recovery options.

## Status and prior progress

- Work completed before the start of the week.
- Deliverables carried forward from the previous week.
- Evidence supporting current status.
- Prior missed dates or unresolved review comments.
- Remaining work and estimated effort.
- Variance from the earlier plan.
- Lessons or constraints affecting the new five-day schedule.

## Prioritization and scheduling criteria

- Weekly priority rank.
- Fixed and flexible deadlines.
- Strategic and operational value.
- Consequence of delay.
- Dependency and unblocking effect.
- Required executive involvement.
- Deliverable readiness.
- Available capacity and effort.
- Ability to delegate or reduce scope.
- Required weighting or scoring method, if applicable.

## Source and data quality

- Source and date for each deliverable, owner, deadline, and status.
- Distinction between confirmed commitments, proposed work, estimates, and assumptions.
- Missing, outdated, conflicting, or unverified information.
- Confidence in effort, readiness, and completion estimates.
- Items requiring owner, reviewer, approver, or specialist validation.
