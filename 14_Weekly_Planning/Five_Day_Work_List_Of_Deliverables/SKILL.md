# Skill: Five Day Work List Of Deliverables

## Purpose

This skill converts approved weekly priorities, commitments, milestones, decisions, and available capacity into a sequenced five-day work list of concrete deliverables. It shows what must be produced, who owns it, when the work should occur, what dependencies and reviews are required, and how completion will be verified.

The skill prepares a recommended delivery plan. It does not assign work, change deadlines, approve deliverables, modify project records, or communicate commitments without authorized human review.

## Core concepts

### Deliverable versus activity

- An **activity** describes work performed.
- A **deliverable** is a defined output, result, or accepted work product.
- The five-day list should focus on what will exist or be achieved, not merely what people will work on.

Example:

- Activity: Review security requirements.
- Deliverable: Documented security-readiness assessment with identified gaps, owners, and required approvals.

### Deliverable versus milestone

- A **deliverable** is an output or result.
- A **milestone** is a meaningful checkpoint or event.
- A milestone may indicate that a deliverable is drafted, reviewed, approved, or completed.

### Completion versus acceptance

- **Completion** means the responsible owner believes the work satisfies its criteria.
- **Acceptance** means an authorized reviewer or approver confirms the result.
- A deliverable requiring acceptance should not be marked complete solely because it was submitted.

### Weekly completion versus weekly progress

Some deliverables cannot be completed within five days. For those items, the weekly plan should define a specific progress outcome, such as:

- Draft completed.
- Evidence assembled.
- Review completed.
- Decision package ready.
- Required approval obtained.
- Blocker resolved.

### Deliverable readiness

- **Ready:** inputs, authority, people, and capacity are available.
- **Partially ready:** work can proceed, but material gaps remain.
- **Not ready:** essential prerequisites prevent meaningful work.
- **Awaiting information:** progress depends on a named input, decision, or person.

## Core activities

### 1. Establish the five-day planning window

- Confirm the week’s dates and working-day assumptions.
- Identify availability, holidays, leave, travel, and operating constraints.
- Review the approved weekly priorities.
- Identify deliverables carried forward from the prior week.
- Identify work due shortly after the week that requires advance preparation.

### 2. Identify candidate deliverables

Review:

- Weekly priority outcomes.
- Initiative and program plans.
- Decisions and approvals.
- Meeting commitments and action items.
- Milestones and deadlines.
- Risks, issues, and mitigation actions.
- Stakeholder commitments.
- Work carried over from earlier periods.

Exclude routine activity that does not produce a distinct result unless it materially consumes capacity.

### 3. Define each deliverable

For each candidate:

- State the required output or result.
- Explain its purpose.
- Identify the related priority or commitment.
- Define completion and acceptance criteria.
- Identify the owner, reviewers, and approvers.
- Confirm the deadline and consequence of delay.
- Define the specific outcome expected by the end of the week.

### 4. Assess readiness

Confirm whether the deliverable has:

- A clear outcome.
- A responsible owner.
- Required source materials.
- Required expertise and contributors.
- Appropriate authority and access.
- Defined completion criteria.
- Available reviewers and approvers.
- Sufficient time and capacity.
- Resolved or manageable dependencies.

Classify readiness and identify actions needed before work can proceed.

### 5. Decompose deliverables into milestones

Create only the milestones needed to manage delivery, such as:

- Inputs confirmed.
- Draft prepared.
- Initial review completed.
- Feedback incorporated.
- Final quality check completed.
- Approval or acceptance received.

Avoid turning every minor activity into a separately tracked milestone.

### 6. Sequence work across five days

- Place prerequisites before dependent work.
- Schedule reviews early enough to allow revision.
- Account for reviewer and participant availability.
- Avoid placing all acceptance activity on the final day.
- Protect focus periods for substantive production.
- Include decision, communication, and follow-up time.
- Preserve contingency capacity.

### 7. Balance workload and capacity

- Estimate effort for each deliverable and milestone.
- Compare effort with owner availability.
- Include meetings and routine operating work.
- Identify people or days with excessive demand.
- Avoid assigning the same person to simultaneous critical work.
- Make work that does not fit visible.

### 8. Identify dependencies and risks

- Connect each deliverable to required information, decisions, approvals, people, systems, vendors, and predecessor work.
- Identify shared dependencies across deliverables.
- Surface schedule, quality, scope, capacity, and acceptance risks.
- Identify response owners and deadlines.
- Recommend early escalation when a dependency threatens the weekly outcome.

### 9. Recommend trade-offs

When the delivery plan exceeds capacity:

- Retain the highest-value and highest-consequence deliverables.
- Recommend delegation where authority, expertise, access, and capacity support it.
- Reduce scope while preserving a useful weekly outcome.
- Defer work with manageable consequences.
- Separate oversized deliverables into independent outcomes.
- Recommend stopping work that no longer supports current priorities.

State the consequence of every material trade-off.

### 10. Define acceptance and closure

For every deliverable:

- Identify the authorized reviewer or approver.
- Specify evidence required.
- Schedule review and revision.
- Record acceptance conditions.
- Identify related tasks, risks, decisions, or plans requiring updates.
- Preserve unresolved exceptions.

### 11. Prepare end-of-week continuity

- Record completed and accepted deliverables.
- Distinguish submitted work from accepted work.
- Explain variance from the plan.
- Identify incomplete work and the reason.
- Carry work forward with a revised owner, date, status, and next action.
- Connect carryover work to the next weekly planning cycle.

## Decision rules

- Do not describe an activity as a deliverable unless it produces a defined result.
- Do not mark a submitted deliverable as accepted without authorized confirmation.
- Do not schedule dependent work before its prerequisites.
- Do not schedule review so late that reasonable revision is impossible.
- Do not treat an unsupported due date as confirmed.
- Do not assign deliverables to people without considering authority, capacity, access, and acknowledgment.
- Do not hide overload by reducing effort estimates without evidence.
- Define a smaller weekly progress outcome when full delivery is unrealistic.
- Preserve completion criteria when reducing scope, or explicitly revise them through authorized change control.
- Make deferred, blocked, and carryover work visible.
- Do not automatically change official owners, dates, or statuses.

## Quality standards

A high-quality five-day delivery plan must be:

- **Deliverable-based:** organized around defined outputs and results.
- **Prioritized:** supports approved weekly outcomes.
- **Sequenced:** respects dependencies and review timing.
- **Realistic:** fits available capacity.
- **Owned:** responsibility and accountability are explicit.
- **Measurable:** completion and acceptance criteria are clear.
- **Risk-aware:** blockers and recovery actions are visible.
- **Traceable:** deliverables connect to their sources and commitments.
- **Reviewable:** progress can be assessed throughout the week.
- **Continuous:** incomplete work transitions cleanly into the next plan.

## Safeguards and limitations

- Do not independently assign deliverables or change owners.
- Do not approve, accept, close, defer, cancel, or reschedule work.
- Do not fabricate deadlines, status, effort, completion evidence, authority, or acceptance.
- Do not present a proposed plan as an approved commitment.
- Do not hide incomplete, rejected, or blocked deliverables.
- Do not expose sensitive financial, personnel, clinical, security, privacy, legal, procurement, or governance information to unauthorized audiences.
- Do not make specialist determinations requiring qualified review.
- Clearly distinguish confirmed work from recommendations and assumptions.

## Human role

The executive, delivery owner, or authorized manager must:

- Confirm the deliverables included in the five-day plan.
- Approve owners, priorities, scope, and deadlines.
- Validate capacity and resource assumptions.
- Approve delegation, scope reduction, deferral, or cancellation.
- Resolve priority and dependency conflicts.
- Review and accept completed deliverables.
- Authorize updates to official tracking systems.
- Determine what carries forward into the next week.
