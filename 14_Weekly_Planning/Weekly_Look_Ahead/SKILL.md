# Skill: Weekly Look Ahead

## Purpose

This skill creates a forward-looking executive view of the upcoming week by integrating priorities, calendar events, decisions, deliverables, commitments, risks, issues, opportunities, stakeholder engagements, dependencies, and available capacity. It helps leadership anticipate what is coming, prepare before critical events, and identify conflicts or gaps early enough to act.

The skill prepares an outlook and recommendations. It does not modify calendars, establish priorities, assign work, approve decisions, communicate commitments, or initiate escalations without authorized human approval.

## Core concepts

### Look-ahead versus weekly plan

- A **weekly plan** defines intended work and time allocation.
- A **weekly look-ahead** identifies what is approaching, what may change, and what requires preparation or attention.
- The look-ahead may reveal that the existing plan needs human revision.

### Forecast versus commitment

- A **commitment** has been confirmed by an authorized person.
- A **forecast** represents an expected future condition.
- Tentative meetings, anticipated decisions, projected milestones, and emerging risks must not be represented as confirmed commitments.

### Executive attention point

An item warrants executive attention when it may require:

- Decision or approval authority.
- Leadership judgment.
- Sponsorship or alignment.
- Material risk response.
- Resolution of a cross-functional conflict.
- Sensitive stakeholder communication.
- Resource allocation.
- Preparation for a high-consequence event.

### Readiness and lead time

The skill should identify not only when an event occurs, but also when preparation must begin. Readiness includes:

- Information.
- Evidence.
- Required participants.
- Authority.
- Review.
- Materials.
- Time to revise or respond.

### Cross-week continuity

A useful outlook includes:

- Work carried from the previous week.
- Events and outcomes expected this week.
- Preparation required now for the following week.
- Dependencies and risks spanning multiple planning periods.

## Core activities

### 1. Establish the outlook horizon

- Confirm the week’s dates, audience, and scope.
- Review executive and team availability.
- Identify travel, leave, holidays, and operating constraints.
- Review strategic and operational conditions.
- Include a short forward view into the following week.

### 2. Carry forward unresolved context

- Review unfinished priorities, decisions, actions, and deliverables.
- Preserve their original owners, sources, deadlines, and history.
- Identify why they remain unresolved.
- Determine whether their importance, urgency, or risk has changed.
- Connect carryover items to upcoming meetings or milestones.

### 3. Gather forward-looking events and obligations

Review:

- Calendar events.
- Weekly priorities.
- Decisions and approvals.
- Deliverables and milestones.
- Actions and commitments.
- Risks, issues, and blockers.
- Stakeholder engagements.
- Relevant external developments.
- Early requirements for the following week.

### 4. Identify material attention points

For each candidate item, assess:

- Strategic and operational importance.
- Deadline and consequence.
- Executive-specific authority or contribution.
- Risk or opportunity exposure.
- Stakeholder sensitivity.
- Dependency and unblocking effect.
- Readiness.
- Time required for preparation.

Highlight only items that materially affect leadership outcomes.

### 5. Build day-by-day continuity

- Sequence meetings, decisions, deliverables, and preparation.
- Show how one day’s activity affects later days.
- Schedule preparation before the event it supports.
- Identify follow-up and action-capture requirements.
- Show decisions or dependencies that may change the remainder of the week.
- Preserve reasonable contingency time.

### 6. Assess decision and meeting readiness

Check whether:

- The objective and desired outcome are clear.
- Required participants and authority are available.
- Supporting materials are current.
- Options, trade-offs, and risks are visible.
- Prior decisions and commitments are accurately represented.
- Required preparation can be completed in time.
- Missing evidence or specialist review could prevent the intended outcome.

### 7. Assess deliverable and milestone outlook

- Confirm ownership, status, deadlines, and acceptance criteria.
- Compare reported progress with evidence.
- Identify deliverables likely to complete, slip, or require intervention.
- Connect milestone changes to downstream work.
- Flag work with insufficient review or acceptance time.

### 8. Monitor risks, issues, and opportunities

- Identify material changes in exposure or timing.
- Distinguish risks from realized issues.
- Identify emerging opportunities with limited response windows.
- Connect each item to affected work.
- Recommend preparation, mitigation, escalation, or evaluation.
- Label uncertain or speculative developments clearly.

### 9. Map dependencies

- Identify prerequisite decisions, evidence, approvals, people, systems, and resources.
- Surface dependencies shared across priorities.
- Detect work scheduled before prerequisites.
- Identify cascading effects.
- Recommend coordination owners and response dates.

### 10. Evaluate capacity and conflicts

- Compare planned workload with availability.
- Include meetings, preparation, focus work, transitions, and follow-up.
- Identify overallocated days or participants.
- Surface competing deadlines and insufficient preparation time.
- Recommend explicit trade-offs or additional support.
- Do not conceal overload through unsupported effort reductions.

### 11. Prepare the following week

- Identify early-next-week decisions, meetings, and deliverables.
- Determine what preparation must begin now.
- Assign or propose owners for preparation.
- Identify dependencies requiring early resolution.
- Ensure continuity between weekly planning cycles.

## Decision rules

- Do not list every calendar event as an executive attention point.
- Do not represent forecasts, assumptions, or tentative events as confirmed.
- Do not infer approval from anticipated discussion.
- Do not schedule preparation after the event it supports.
- Do not treat a recurring event as low importance without reviewing its current purpose.
- Give higher visibility to items requiring unique executive authority or judgment.
- Include the consequence of delay when highlighting urgency.
- Distinguish risks, realized issues, and opportunities.
- Do not recommend delegation without considering authority, expertise, access, and capacity.
- Preserve unresolved items and their history across weeks.
- Surface overload and sequencing conflicts explicitly.

## Quality standards

A high-quality weekly outlook must be:

- **Forward-looking:** identifies what is approaching and what must begin early.
- **Selective:** emphasizes material leadership attention points.
- **Integrated:** connects events, decisions, deliverables, stakeholders, and risks.
- **Sequenced:** shows preparation and dependencies in the correct order.
- **Realistic:** reflects available capacity and operating constraints.
- **Traceable:** material claims connect to current sources.
- **Transparent:** forecasts, assumptions, and uncertainty are labeled.
- **Continuous:** links prior-week context, the current week, and the following week.
- **Actionable:** each attention point has a recommended next step.
- **Human-reviewed:** recommendations do not become commitments automatically.

## Safeguards and limitations

- Do not independently create, move, accept, decline, or cancel calendar events.
- Do not assign, delegate, defer, or cancel work.
- Do not approve, reject, or communicate decisions.
- Do not fabricate priorities, forecasts, deadlines, owners, stakeholder positions, or external developments.
- Do not treat emerging external information as verified without appropriate sourcing.
- Do not expose sensitive financial, personnel, clinical, legal, security, privacy, procurement, or governance information to unauthorized audiences.
- Do not make specialist determinations requiring qualified review.
- Do not suppress uncertainty, dissent, missing evidence, or conflicting sources.
- Clearly distinguish confirmed commitments from tentative plans and recommendations.

## Human role

The executive or authorized reviewer must:

- Confirm the material priorities and attention points.
- Validate forecasts, deadlines, and consequences.
- Approve changes to calendars, work, or commitments.
- Confirm stakeholder and organizational sensitivities.
- Make or authorize required decisions.
- Resolve capacity and sequencing conflicts.
- Authorize communication, delegation, and escalation.
- Determine what preparation begins for the following week.
