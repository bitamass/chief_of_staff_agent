# Skill: Weekly Key Decisions Or Approvals

## Purpose

This skill identifies, evaluates, prioritizes, and sequences the decisions and approvals that require leadership or governance action during the upcoming week. It connects each item to the correct authority, deadline, options, evidence, risks, dependencies, stakeholder input, readiness gaps, and downstream consequences.

The skill prepares a weekly decision agenda and supporting briefs. It does not make, approve, reject, authorize, endorse, defer, or communicate decisions without authorized human action.

## Core concepts

### Decision versus approval

- A **decision** selects a direction or resolves a defined question.
- An **approval** authorizes a proposed action, expenditure, commitment, exception, deliverable, or implementation step.
- Some matters require a substantive decision followed by one or more formal approvals.

### Key weekly decision

A decision is key for the week when it:

- Has a fixed or consequential deadline.
- Requires unique executive or governance authority.
- Unblocks important work.
- Affects material risk, resources, strategy, operations, or stakeholders.
- Must occur before an upcoming milestone or commitment.
- Has become increasingly consequential through delay.

### Recommendation versus decision

- A **recommendation** proposes a preferred option.
- A **decision** is the authorized selection.
- A recommendation, consensus view, or endorsement must not be represented as approval.

### Decision readiness

- **Ready:** the decision statement, authority, options, evidence, risks, dependencies, and reviews are sufficient.
- **Partially ready:** material gaps remain, but direction or conditional action may be possible.
- **Not ready:** the decision cannot responsibly be made with available evidence or authority.
- **Awaiting information:** action depends on a named input, participant, or review.

### Conditional approval

A conditional approval must specify:

- The authorized action.
- Conditions that must be satisfied.
- Condition owners and deadlines.
- Required evidence.
- Limits on activity before conditions are met.
- Person authorized to confirm satisfaction.

### Decision dependency

Decisions may form a sequence. The skill must show when:

- One approval is required before another.
- A decision unlocks a deliverable or milestone.
- Several decisions depend on the same evidence or specialist.
- A delayed decision creates cascading effects.

## Core activities

### 1. Establish the weekly decision horizon

- Confirm the week’s dates, review forums, and decision-maker availability.
- Identify major weekly priorities, milestones, and commitments.
- Review unresolved decisions from the prior week.
- Identify decisions due shortly after the week that require advance preparation.
- Confirm applicable governance and authority requirements.

### 2. Build the candidate decision inventory

Review:

- Explicit decision and approval requests.
- Recommendations awaiting authorization.
- Deliverables awaiting acceptance.
- Exception and risk-acceptance requests.
- Milestone or contract approvals.
- Decisions generated by meetings, issues, risks, or initiative work.
- Items carried forward from the prior week.

Exclude items already completed, withdrawn, canceled, or superseded unless related follow-through remains open.

### 3. Clarify each decision statement

- State exactly what must be decided or approved.
- Identify the action requested.
- Explain why action is required this week.
- Define the scope and affected parties.
- Identify the consequence of approval, rejection, delay, and no action.
- Separate independent decisions when combining them would obscure authority or trade-offs.

### 4. Confirm authority and process

- Identify the authorized decision-maker or approving body.
- Verify approval thresholds and delegated limits.
- Identify required reviewers, endorsers, co-approvers, or advisors.
- Confirm quorum, voting, conflict-of-interest, and formal-meeting requirements.
- Identify specialist or governance reviews.
- Flag items lacking a valid authority pathway.

Do not infer authority solely from title or meeting participation.

### 5. Gather options and evidence

- Identify all reasonable options, including maintaining the current state when relevant.
- Summarize costs, benefits, risks, timing, resources, and implementation implications.
- Identify the documented recommendation and recommending party.
- Retrieve specialist reviews and stakeholder input.
- Separate facts, estimates, assumptions, and interpretations.
- Preserve dissenting or alternative views.

### 6. Evaluate readiness

Assess whether:

- The decision statement is clear.
- The decision-maker has appropriate authority.
- Options and evaluation criteria are sufficiently defined.
- Evidence is current and material.
- Risks and dependencies are visible.
- Required specialists and stakeholders have participated.
- Implementation ownership is known.
- The deadline and consequence of delay are supported.

Classify readiness and identify material gaps.

### 7. Prioritize the weekly decision queue

Evaluate each item using:

- Deadline and time sensitivity.
- Consequence of delay.
- Strategic and operational value.
- Material risk.
- Downstream dependencies.
- Executive-specific authority.
- Stakeholder commitments.
- Readiness.
- Reversibility.
- Preparation and review effort.

Do not prioritize an item solely because the requestor is senior.

### 8. Sequence decisions across the week

- Place prerequisite decisions before dependent approvals or deliverables.
- Schedule preparation and pre-read distribution before review.
- Account for decision-maker, specialist, and stakeholder availability.
- Avoid concentrating too many complex decisions into one forum.
- Allow time for revision, conditional requirements, and follow-up.
- Identify scheduling conflicts that threaten appropriate review.

### 9. Close readiness gaps

For each material gap:

- State what is missing.
- Explain how it affects the decision.
- Identify a responsible owner.
- Establish a completion deadline.
- Determine whether direction or conditional approval remains possible.
- Avoid requesting evidence that would not materially change the decision.

### 10. Prepare the decision brief

- Lead with the decision required, authority, and deadline.
- Present options and trade-offs neutrally.
- Identify the documented recommendation without assuming approval.
- Surface risks, dependencies, uncertainty, and dissent.
- State the readiness assessment.
- Specify the exact human action requested.

### 11. Recommend a weekly disposition

Recommend one of the following:

- Decide or approve.
- Approve with defined conditions.
- Request targeted evidence.
- Seek specialist review.
- Return for revision.
- Separate into multiple decisions.
- Defer to a specific date or condition.
- Escalate to the correct authority.
- Decline or withdraw.

Include the rationale and next action.

### 12. Preserve the decision record and continuity

Following authorized human action:

- Record the decision, decision-maker, date, rationale, and evidence.
- Capture conditions, limitations, expiration, and dissent.
- Create or update follow-up actions.
- Link the decision to affected initiatives, risks, deliverables, and meetings.
- Carry unresolved decisions into the following week with full history.
- Preserve the original source and prior status.

## Decision rules

- Do not treat recommendations, endorsements, or informal agreement as approval.
- Do not infer approval from silence, attendance, or lack of objection.
- Do not classify a decision as ready when material authority, evidence, or required review is missing.
- Do not request immaterial information that unnecessarily delays action.
- Do not recommend conditional approval without measurable conditions, owners, deadlines, and verification authority.
- Do not sequence a dependent approval before its prerequisite decision.
- Do not hide prior deferrals or missed decision dates.
- Preserve rejected, withdrawn, deferred, and superseded outcomes in the history.
- Flag conflicts with policy, authority, or prior decisions.
- Require human confirmation before changing any decision status.

## Quality standards

A high-quality weekly decision plan must be:

- **Clear:** each decision statement is explicit.
- **Authorized:** the correct decision-maker and governance process are identified.
- **Prioritized:** attention reflects consequence and timing.
- **Sequenced:** dependencies and preparation occur in the correct order.
- **Balanced:** options and trade-offs are fairly represented.
- **Evidence-based:** material claims connect to current sources.
- **Risk-aware:** consequences, uncertainty, and dependencies are visible.
- **Realistic:** decision load fits available preparation and review capacity.
- **Traceable:** decisions, rationale, conditions, and follow-up are recorded.
- **Human-controlled:** no authorization occurs automatically.

## Safeguards and limitations

- Do not independently make, approve, reject, authorize, endorse, defer, withdraw, or escalate decisions.
- Do not fabricate authority, deadlines, options, recommendations, evidence, stakeholder positions, or approval status.
- Do not accept financial, operational, legal, clinical, security, privacy, compliance, procurement, personnel, or reputational risk.
- Do not communicate a draft decision as final.
- Do not expose confidential decision material to unauthorized audiences.
- Do not suppress dissenting views, uncertainty, missing evidence, or conflicts.
- Do not make specialist determinations requiring qualified review.
- Clearly distinguish pending, recommended, conditional, and final statuses.

## Human role

The authorized executive, manager, committee, or governance body must:

- Confirm the decision statement and urgency.
- Validate authority and required process.
- Review options, evidence, risks, and stakeholder input.
- Decide, approve, condition, defer, reject, withdraw, or escalate the request.
- Approve the rationale and any conditions.
- Authorize communication and implementation.
- Confirm that the decision record is accurate.
- Determine what carries into the following week.
