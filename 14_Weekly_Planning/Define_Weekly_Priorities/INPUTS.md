# Inputs

## Weekly planning context

- Week start and end dates, time zone, and working-day assumptions.
- Executive role, responsibilities, and decision authority.
- Current organizational strategy, annual goals, quarterly objectives, or OKRs.
- Major operational conditions or leadership concerns affecting the week.
- Planned leave, travel, holidays, reduced availability, and protected focus periods.
- Review audience and the person authorized to confirm weekly priorities.

## Candidate weekly priorities

For each proposed priority:

- Concise description of the required outcome.
- Related strategic goal, objective, key result, initiative, program, or operational responsibility.
- Sponsor, accountable owner, responsible lead, and supporting participants.
- Source of the priority and source date.
- Current status and work completed.
- Required completion or progress condition for the week.
- Due date, milestone, meeting, or decision deadline.
- Estimated executive and team effort.
- Expected value or benefit.
- Consequence of delay, deferral, or non-completion.
- Whether executive attention, decision, approval, sponsorship, communication, or escalation is required.

## Prior-week continuity

- Priorities carried forward from the previous week.
- Previous weekly commitments and expected outcomes.
- Work completed, partially completed, deferred, blocked, or canceled.
- Reasons unfinished priorities remain open.
- Decisions, actions, or commitments created during the prior week.
- Changes in assumptions, scope, timing, ownership, or urgency.
- Lessons affecting the upcoming plan.

## Calendar and meeting commitments

- Scheduled meetings and events for the week.
- Meeting objectives and expected outcomes.
- Executive’s required role.
- Decisions, approvals, or alignment expected.
- Required preparation and follow-up time.
- Fixed versus flexible events.
- Meetings that could advance an existing priority.
- Travel, transition, and scheduling constraints.

## Decisions and approvals

- Decisions or approvals due during the week.
- Decision statement and deadline.
- Decision-maker or approving authority.
- Options and documented recommendation.
- Required evidence and specialist review.
- Current readiness.
- Consequence of delay or no decision.
- Initiatives, milestones, risks, or stakeholders affected.

## Deliverables and milestones

- Deliverables and milestones due during or shortly after the week.
- Related initiative, program, or objective.
- Owner, due date, status, and completion criteria.
- Required executive review, approval, or sponsorship.
- Dependencies and resource requirements.
- Evidence supporting the reported status.
- Consequence of missing the milestone.

## Actions and commitments

- Open actions assigned to the executive or leadership team.
- Commitments made to internal or external stakeholders.
- Owner, due date, current status, and source.
- Overdue or blocked actions.
- Work that can be delegated or completed asynchronously.
- Commitments that may compete with proposed weekly priorities.
- Downstream work dependent on completion.

## Risks, issues, and blockers

- Material risks and issues requiring attention during the week.
- Owner, rating, status, trend, and response.
- Blockers affecting proposed priorities.
- Mitigation, containment, or resolution actions.
- Escalation thresholds and deadlines.
- Executive decision, authority, communication, or resources required.
- Consequence of not responding during the week.

## Stakeholder requirements

- Stakeholders requiring a decision, response, update, meeting, or engagement.
- Stakeholder role, interests, influence, and documented concerns.
- Existing commitments and communication deadlines.
- Sensitive relationships requiring executive involvement.
- Stakeholder engagements that may be delegated.
- Desired outcome of each interaction.

## Dependencies and strategic alignment

- Connection between each candidate priority and approved strategy.
- Predecessor decisions, tasks, approvals, or milestones.
- Successor activities or outcomes.
- Cross-initiative dependencies.
- Shared resources or participants.
- Priority conflicts.
- Work that will unblock multiple activities.
- Assumptions underlying the proposed alignment.

## Capacity and effort

- Executive availability by day.
- Team capacity and key participant availability.
- Scheduled meeting, preparation, travel, and follow-up time.
- Estimated effort required for each candidate priority.
- Existing operational workload.
- Resource or expertise constraints.
- Contingency capacity for unexpected work.
- Work that may be delegated, deferred, combined, or stopped.

## Prioritization criteria

- Approved organizational or executive criteria.
- Strategic alignment.
- Value or expected benefit.
- Urgency and deadline.
- Consequence of delay.
- Risk reduction.
- Stakeholder impact.
- Executive-specific contribution.
- Dependency and unblocking effect.
- Readiness.
- Effort and available capacity.
- Reversibility and opportunity window.
- Required weighting or scoring method, if applicable.

## Source and data quality

- Source and date for each candidate priority.
- Source for deadlines, ownership, strategic alignment, and expected value.
- Distinction between confirmed commitments, proposed priorities, assumptions, and estimates.
- Missing, outdated, conflicting, or unverified information.
- Confidence in impact, effort, readiness, and urgency assessments.
- Items requiring executive, owner, or specialist confirmation.
