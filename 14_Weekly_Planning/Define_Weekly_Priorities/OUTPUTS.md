# Outputs

## Weekly priority summary

- Week dates and concise statement of the week’s leadership focus.
- Three to seven recommended weekly priorities.
- Required outcome for each priority.
- Why each priority matters this week.
- Executive role required.
- Primary risks, dependencies, and capacity constraints.
- Material changes from the previous weekly plan.

## Ranked weekly priorities

For each priority:

- Rank and priority title.
- Required weekly outcome.
- Related strategic objective, initiative, or commitment.
- Priority classification: critical, high, standard, defer, delegate, or stop.
- Executive role: decide, approve, review, sponsor, communicate, prepare, or unblock.
- Accountable owner and responsible lead.
- Target date.
- Estimated executive and team effort.
- Readiness classification.
- Dependencies and blockers.
- Consequence of delay.
- Weekly success measure.
- Recommended next action.
- Source and confidence level.

## Priority rationale

For each ranked item:

- Strategic alignment.
- Expected value or benefit.
- Deadline and urgency.
- Consequence of delay.
- Risk reduction.
- Stakeholder impact.
- Executive-specific contribution.
- Ability to unblock downstream work.
- Readiness and effort.
- Reason for its position relative to competing priorities.

## Weekly outcome plan

- Outcome to achieve.
- Required activities or decisions.
- Owner and supporting participants.
- Target completion or progress point.
- Key meeting or milestone.
- Preparation and review requirements.
- Evidence required to confirm success.
- Follow-up or continuity into the next week.

## Decisions and approvals required

- Decision or approval statement.
- Related weekly priority.
- Decision-maker or approving authority.
- Deadline.
- Available options and trade-offs.
- Current recommendation, if documented.
- Readiness status.
- Required evidence or specialist review.
- Consequence of delay.
- Preparation owner and next action.

## Priority dependency map

- Priority and required predecessor.
- Shared resource, owner, system, or approval.
- Downstream work affected.
- Sequencing requirement.
- Cross-priority conflict.
- Risk created by the dependency.
- Recommended coordination action.

## Capacity and feasibility assessment

- Total estimated effort compared with available capacity.
- Executive time required.
- Team time required.
- Meeting and preparation load.
- Days or roles with excessive demand.
- Priorities that do not fit within realistic capacity.
- Required reductions, delegation, rescheduling, or additional resources.
- Contingency capacity preserved.

## Trade-off recommendations

For each item not selected as a primary weekly priority:

- Candidate item.
- Recommendation: delegate, defer, combine, reduce scope, continue as routine work, or stop.
- Reason for the recommendation.
- Consequence and risk.
- Proposed owner or revised timing.
- Stakeholder communication required.
- Human authorization needed.

## Risks and blockers to weekly success

- Affected priority.
- Risk, issue, or blocker.
- Owner and current status.
- Required mitigation or resolution.
- Executive action needed.
- Response deadline.
- Escalation condition.
- Consequence if unresolved.

## Weekly success measures

- Observable outcome for each priority.
- Decision made or approval recorded.
- Deliverable completed or accepted.
- Milestone achieved.
- Risk reduced or escalated.
- Stakeholder commitment fulfilled.
- Blocker removed.
- Owner, deadline, or next stage confirmed.
- Evidence source and acceptance authority.

## Source and confidence report

- Sources used to define and rank priorities.
- Confirmed facts, estimates, assumptions, and interpretations.
- Date and freshness of each source.
- Missing or conflicting information.
- Confidence in each priority recommendation.
- Items requiring human validation before the plan is confirmed.
