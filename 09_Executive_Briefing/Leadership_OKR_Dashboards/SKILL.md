# Skill: Leadership OKR Dashboards

## Purpose

This skill produces an executive dashboard that shows progress, trajectory, confidence, alignment, and leadership needs across organizational Objectives and Key Results.

It helps leaders understand:

- What the organization is trying to achieve.
- How success is measured.
- Whether current evidence indicates the objectives will be achieved.
- Which initiatives contribute to each key result.
- What changed since the previous check-in.
- Where risks, dependencies, or resource conflicts threaten outcomes.
- Which decisions or interventions leadership must make.
- Whether OKR data and scoring are reliable.

The skill supports OKR review and leadership decision-making. It does not approve objectives, alter targets, certify performance, or replace accountable-owner judgment.

## Core concepts

### Objective

A qualitative statement describing a meaningful outcome or direction.

A strong objective should be:

- Strategically relevant.
- Outcome-oriented.
- Clear.
- Time-bound by the OKR cycle.
- Inspiring but understandable.
- Within the organization’s meaningful influence.

An objective should not merely describe routine work.

### Key result

A measurable result showing whether the objective is being achieved.

A strong key result should include:

- A defined metric.
- A baseline.
- A target.
- A timeframe.
- An owner.
- A data source.
- A clear direction of improvement.

A key result should generally measure an outcome rather than an activity or task.

### Initiative

A project, program, workstream, or action intended to influence a key result.

Completing an initiative does not prove that the key result was achieved.

### Progress

The current measured movement from baseline toward target.

### Confidence

The owner’s evidence-based assessment of whether the key result or objective is likely to be achieved by the end of the cycle.

Progress and confidence are different:

- Progress describes the current state.
- Confidence estimates the likelihood of final achievement.

### Score

A value calculated using the approved OKR scoring method.

The score should not replace the underlying metric, trend, confidence, or narrative.

## Core activities

### 1. Validate the OKR structure

For every objective:

- Confirm strategic alignment.
- Confirm ownership.
- Confirm the cycle.
- Confirm it describes an outcome rather than routine activity.

For every key result:

- Confirm the metric.
- Confirm baseline and target.
- Confirm owner and source.
- Confirm reporting period.
- Confirm that the measure reasonably indicates achievement of the objective.

Flag:

- Objectives without measurable key results.
- Key results without baselines or targets.
- Activity-based key results.
- Duplicate key results.
- Metrics the owner cannot influence.
- Key results disconnected from the stated objective.

### 2. Apply the approved scoring method

Use the organization’s approved methodology.
