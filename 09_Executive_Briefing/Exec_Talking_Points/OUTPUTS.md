# Outputs

## Executive message frame

- Engagement purpose.
- Audience.
- Desired outcome.
- Primary message.
- Two to four supporting messages.
- Required call to action.
- Recommended tone.
- Material sensitivities and limitations.

## Opening talking points

- Audience acknowledgment.
- Purpose of the engagement.
- Relevant context.
- Concise statement of the primary message.
- Connection to organizational mission, priorities, or stakeholder needs.
- Transition to the main discussion.

## Core talking points

For each major point:

- Short spoken-language headline.
- Supporting explanation.
- Approved fact, example, or evidence.
- Why the point matters to the audience.
- Connection to the desired outcome.
- Source reference.
- Optional transition to the next point.

## Decision and action points

- Decisions already made.
- Decisions or alignment still required.
- Leadership expectations.
- Specific audience action requested.
- Owner and deadline, when approved.
- Commitments leadership is prepared to make.
- Matters leadership is not yet prepared to commit to.

## Difficult topics and sensitivities

- Concern or objection.
- Recommended acknowledgment.
- Approved response.
- Supporting evidence.
- Words or claims to avoid.
- Bridge to the primary message.
- Escalation or referral when further detail is inappropriate.

## Anticipated questions and responses

For each likely question:

- Concise answer.
- Supporting message.
- Approved evidence.
- Disclosure limitation.
- Bridge statement.
- Follow-up owner if additional information is required.

## Closing talking points

- Summary of the primary message.
- Desired action or next step.
- Appreciation or acknowledgment.
- Forward-looking statement.
- Closing sentence aligned with the executive’s voice.

## Delivery guidance

- Estimated speaking time.
- Points to emphasize.
- Optional points if additional time is available.
- Points to omit if time is shortened.
- Recommended pauses and transitions.
- Pronunciation or terminology notes.
- Areas requiring the executive’s personal wording.
- Facts or claims requiring final confirmation.

## Source and approval checklist

- Sources supporting material statements.
- Approved statistics, dates, and quotations.
- Statements requiring executive confirmation.
- Sensitive content requiring specialized review.
- Final communication owner.
- Required approval before use.
