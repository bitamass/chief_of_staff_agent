# Inputs

## Engagement context

- Event, meeting, presentation, interview, town hall, board session, or stakeholder discussion.
- Date, location, format, and duration.
- Executive speaker.
- Intended audience.
- Audience size and composition.
- Purpose of the engagement.
- Desired outcome.
- Executive’s role in the engagement.
- Other speakers or participants.
- Agenda and speaking order.
- Time allocated for opening remarks, main points, questions, and closing.

## Communication objective

- Primary message the audience should remember.
- Decisions, actions, understanding, confidence, or support sought.
- Organizational priorities to reinforce.
- Position leadership intends to communicate.
- Topics that must be addressed.
- Topics that should remain out of scope.
- Required call to action.
- Desired tone: informative, reassuring, motivational, directive, diplomatic, celebratory, or corrective.
- Desired level of detail.
- Communication success measures.

## Audience context

- Audience roles, responsibilities, and familiarity with the subject.
- Audience interests and information needs.
- Likely concerns, objections, or questions.
- Current level of support or understanding.
- Previous communications with the audience.
- Cultural, geographic, political, professional, or organizational context.
- Accessibility and language considerations.
- Information the audience is authorized to receive.
- Stakeholders whose reactions may influence others.

## Subject-matter context

- Relevant initiative, program, decision, policy, event, or organizational change.
- Background and current status.
- Decisions already made.
- Decisions still pending.
- Major accomplishments.
- Current risks, issues, or dependencies.
- Upcoming milestones and deadlines.
- Stakeholder commitments.
- Approved strategic, operational, financial, clinical, technical, or performance information.
- Relevant external developments approved for use.

## Executive position and voice

- Executive’s approved position.
- Executive priorities and leadership principles.
- Previous remarks, communications, and recurring themes.
- Preferred tone and vocabulary.
- Words, phrases, or framing to use.
- Words, phrases, claims, or topics to avoid.
- Approved quotations or personal examples.
- Commitments the executive is authorized to make.
- Matters requiring cautious or conditional language.

## Supporting evidence

- Approved facts, dates, statistics, and performance measures.
- Source documents.
- Relevant decisions and minutes.
- Research or benchmark information.
- Approved examples and accomplishments.
- Applicable policies, standards, or requirements.
- Source, owner, reporting period, and confidence for every material claim.

## Risk and sensitivity inputs

- Legal, regulatory, privacy, security, clinical, financial, labor, personnel, public-records, or reputational considerations.
- Confidential or embargoed information.
- Topics likely to generate disagreement.
- Active incidents, investigations, negotiations, or litigation.
- Known misinformation or misunderstanding.
- Questions requiring referral to another leader or subject-matter expert.
- Matters for which “no comment” or limited response has been approved.
- Required communication, legal, HR, or policy review.

## Anticipated questions

For each likely question:

- Question.
- Audience or stakeholder likely to ask it.
- Approved answer.
- Supporting evidence.
- Sensitivities.
- Information that cannot be disclosed.
- Appropriate bridge statement.
- Referral owner when the executive should not answer directly.
