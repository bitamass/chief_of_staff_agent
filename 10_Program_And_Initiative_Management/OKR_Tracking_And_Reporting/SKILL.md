# Skill: OKR Tracking and Reporting

## Purpose

This skill maintains a source-linked record of Objectives and Key Results and produces consistent periodic reports on progress, trajectory, confidence, alignment, risks, and leadership needs.

It helps leadership determine:

- What outcomes the organization committed to achieve.
- How those outcomes are measured.
- What progress occurred during the reporting period.
- Whether each objective and key result remains achievable.
- What changed from the previous check-in.
- Which initiatives contribute to the results.
- What risks, dependencies, decisions, or resource constraints require attention.
- Whether the underlying measures and scores are reliable.

This skill focuses on the ongoing OKR management cycle. It does not approve OKRs, alter targets, score individual performance, or replace accountable-owner judgment.

## Core concepts

### Objective

A qualitative, outcome-oriented statement describing what the organization intends to achieve.

### Key result

A measurable result that indicates whether the objective is being achieved.

A key result should normally contain:

- Metric.
- Baseline.
- Target.
- Timeframe.
- Owner.
- Data source.
- Direction of improvement.

### Initiative

A project, program, or action intended to influence a key result.

Completing an initiative does not prove achievement of the key result.

### Progress

Measured movement from the baseline toward the target.

### Score

A calculated representation of progress using the approved OKR method.

### Status

A management classification indicating whether performance currently meets expectations.

### Confidence

An evidence-based estimate of whether the result will be achieved by the end of the cycle.

Progress, score, status, and confidence must remain distinct.

## Core activities

### 1. Establish the OKR record

For every objective and key result:

- Confirm approval status.
- Identify owner and sponsor.
- Record baseline and target.
- Record the measurement method.
- Identify data source and owner.
- Record the cycle.
- Connect the objective to strategy.
- Connect supporting initiatives.
- Flag missing information.

### 2. Validate the OKR structure

Check for:

- Objectives written as routine activities.
- Objectives with no measurable key results.
- Key results with no baseline or target.
- Key results measuring tasks rather than outcomes.
- Metrics outside the owner’s meaningful influence.
- Duplicate or conflicting objectives.
- Initiatives disconnected from an OKR.
- Objectives unsupported by initiatives or resources.

### 3. Collect periodic updates

At each check-in:

- Retrieve current metric values.
- Confirm the reporting period.
- Record owner commentary.
- Update supporting-initiative status.
- Record risks and dependencies.
- Record decisions and actions.
- Compare with the previous report.
- Validate data freshness and quality.

Do not carry forward old values without showing their age.

Apply the approved methodology.
