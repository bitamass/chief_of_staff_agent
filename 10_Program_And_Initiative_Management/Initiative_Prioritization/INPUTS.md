# Inputs

## Portfolio context

- Portfolio, business unit, or organizational scope.
- Prioritization purpose and decision deadline.
- Planning period.
- Executive sponsor and authorized decision-maker.
- Approved strategic priorities.
- Available funding, staffing, technology capacity, and executive attention.
- Mandatory commitments and regulatory obligations.
- Current portfolio roadmap.
- Approved prioritization criteria and weights.
- Previous prioritization decision.

## Initiative information

For each initiative:

- Initiative ID and name.
- Objective and expected outcome.
- Strategic priority supported.
- Executive sponsor and accountable owner.
- Current phase and status.
- Proposed or approved start and completion dates.
- Scope and major deliverables.
- Intended users or beneficiaries.
- Expected value and success measures.
- Estimated cost and resource requirements.
- Current investment and sunk cost.
- Required capabilities and expertise.
- Risks, issues, assumptions, and dependencies.
- Governance and approval requirements.
- Evidence source and confidence level.

## Strategic-alignment data

- Strategic objectives supported.
- Strength and directness of alignment.
- Executive or board commitments.
- Mandatory policy, regulatory, clinical, legal, security, privacy, or operational obligations.
- Contribution to organizational outcomes.
- Contribution to multiple priorities.
- Potential conflict with another objective.
- Evidence supporting the alignment assessment.

## Value and benefit data

- Expected financial benefit.
- Avoided cost or loss.
- Operational improvement.
- Quality or service improvement.
- Clinical, research, workforce, customer, student, or stakeholder outcome.
- Risk reduction.
- Strategic capability created.
- Time to value.
- Duration of expected value.
- Benefits owner.
- Baseline and target.
- Confidence and supporting evidence.

## Cost and resource data

- Initial cost.
- Ongoing operating cost.
- Staffing requirement.
- Specialized expertise.
- Technology, data, vendor, and infrastructure needs.
- Change-management and support requirements.
- Available capacity.
- Opportunity cost.
- Competing demand for the same resources.
- Confidence and assumptions behind estimates.

## Readiness data

- Charter readiness.
- Scope clarity.
- Ownership.
- Business-case maturity.
- Requirements maturity.
- Architecture and technical readiness.
- Data readiness.
- Security, privacy, legal, procurement, clinical, regulatory, and governance readiness.
- Stakeholder alignment.
- Resource availability.
- Dependency readiness.
- Implementation and adoption readiness.
- Evidence and remaining gaps.

## Risk and dependency data

- Inherent and residual risks.
- Risks above tolerance.
- Critical issues.
- Upstream and downstream dependencies.
- Shared-resource conflicts.
- Single points of failure.
- Vendor and procurement dependencies.
- Pending decisions and approvals.
- Mitigation and contingency plans.
- Effect on other initiatives if accelerated, delayed, or stopped.

## Urgency and timing data

- Required decision or implementation date.
- External deadline.
- Regulatory or contractual deadline.
- Window of opportunity.
- Consequence of delay.
- Cost of delay.
- Time sensitivity of expected value.
- Dependencies on seasonal, budgetary, operational, or governance cycles.

## Performance data for active initiatives

- Progress against plan.
- Milestone performance.
- Deliverable and quality status.
- Financial performance.
- Benefits realized.
- Adoption.
- Risk and issue trends.
- Confidence in successful completion.
- Recovery plan.
- Remaining cost and effort.

## Data-quality inputs

- Source documents and owners.
- Reporting cutoff date.
- Missing estimates.
- Conflicting assessments.
- Stale status.
- Unsupported benefit claims.
- Inconsistent costing methods.
- Unverified readiness.
- Confidence level.
- Items requiring specialist or human validation.
