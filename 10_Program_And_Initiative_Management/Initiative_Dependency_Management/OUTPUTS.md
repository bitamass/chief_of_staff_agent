# Outputs

## Dependency register

For every dependency:

- Dependency ID.
- Description.
- Requesting initiative.
- Providing initiative, team, or party.
- Dependency type.
- Requesting owner.
- Provider owner.
- Required-by date.
- Current forecast date.
- Status.
- Criticality.
- Readiness and acceptance criteria.
- Downstream milestone affected.
- Risk and impact.
- Next action.
- Escalation status.
- Source and confidence level.

## Status classification

Each dependency should be classified as:

- Identified.
- Not started.
- In progress.
- On track.
- Watch.
- At risk.
- Blocked.
- Ready for handoff.
- Delivered.
- Pending validation.
- Accepted.
- Rejected.
- Deferred.
- Cancelled.
- Superseded.

The output must explain the basis for Watch, At risk, Blocked, or Rejected classifications.

## Dependency map

- Upstream and downstream relationships.
- Initiatives linked through shared deliverables or decisions.
- Critical-path dependencies.
- Multiple initiatives relying on the same provider or resource.
- Single points of failure.
- Circular dependencies.
- Long dependency chains.
- Bottlenecks and highly connected nodes.
- Unclear or missing relationships.

## Timeline and milestone view

- Dependencies due soon.
- Required-by dates.
- Current forecast dates.
- Variance.
- Dependent milestones.
- Lead time remaining.
- Review and approval windows.
- Recovery dates.
- Dependencies likely to miss the available decision or implementation window.

## Critical dependency summary

For each material dependency:

- What is required.
- Why it matters.
- Current status.
- Provider and consumer.
- Required date.
- Current forecast.
- Impact of delay.
- Workaround.
- Decision or support required.
- Escalation level.
- Next checkpoint.

## Readiness and acceptance view

- Dependencies ready for handoff.
- Inputs delivered but awaiting validation.
- Inputs rejected or incomplete.
- Missing acceptance criteria.
- Required approvals.
- Testing or documentation gaps.
- Differences between provider-complete and consumer-accepted status.

## Ownership and accountability gaps

- Dependencies without owners.
- Provider and consumer ownership conflicts.
- Missing decision authority.
- Unclear escalation path.
- Responsibilities inconsistent with the RACI.
- Dependencies owned only by a department rather than a specific role.
- Owners lacking authority or resources to resolve the dependency.

## Portfolio impact analysis

- Initiatives affected by each critical dependency.
- Shared resource or technology bottlenecks.
- Schedule exposure across initiatives.
- Dependencies affecting multiple strategic priorities.
- Concentration of dependency risk by team, vendor, system, or approval body.
- Common dependency types.
- Repeated handoff or acceptance failures.

## Recommended actions

- Routine monitoring.
- Status validation.
- Owner alignment.
- Acceptance-criteria clarification.
- Schedule coordination.
- Resource adjustment.
- Alternative provider or workaround.
- Decision acceleration.
- Sponsor intervention.
- Executive escalation.
- Roadmap resequencing.
- Formal change control.

Each recommendation should identify the owner, required action, and target date.

## Executive attention

- Dependencies threatening major decisions or milestones.
- Cross-initiative conflicts.
- Shared-resource constraints.
- Vendor or approval bottlenecks.
- Required priority decisions.
- Dependencies requiring funding or executive authority.
- Recommended decision-maker and decision date.
