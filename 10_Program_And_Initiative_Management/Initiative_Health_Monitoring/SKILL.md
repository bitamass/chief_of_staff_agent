# Skill: Initiative Health Monitoring

## Purpose

This skill produces a holistic, evidence-based assessment of whether an initiative is positioned to achieve its approved objectives and outcomes.

It helps leadership understand:

- Whether the initiative remains strategically relevant.
- Whether scope, schedule, funding, resources, and deliverables are controlled.
- Whether expected outcomes and benefits remain achievable.
- Whether risks, issues, dependencies, decisions, or stakeholder concerns threaten success.
- What changed since the previous review.
- What corrective action or leadership intervention is required.
- How reliable the assessment is.

Initiative health is broader than schedule status. An initiative may be on schedule but unhealthy because its scope, outcomes, adoption, governance, risk, or resources are inadequate.

## Core health dimensions

### Strategic alignment

Determines whether the initiative:

- Supports an approved strategic priority.
- Remains relevant under current organizational conditions.
- Has a clear executive sponsor.
- Has outcomes that justify continued investment.
- Avoids unnecessary duplication.

### Scope health

Determines whether:

- Scope is clear.
- Requirements are stable enough for the current phase.
- Scope changes are controlled.
- Deliverables and acceptance criteria are defined.
- The initiative is experiencing uncontrolled expansion or reduction.

### Schedule health

Determines whether:

- Milestones are being achieved.
- The forecast remains credible.
- Critical-path work is progressing.
- Review and approval time is included.
- Recovery plans are sufficient.

### Financial health

Determines whether:

- Spending is within approved expectations.
- Forecasts are credible.
- Funding remains sufficient.
- Costs and benefits remain proportionate.
- Material financial decisions are unresolved.

### Resource health

Determines whether:

- Required staffing and expertise are available.
- Capacity is sufficient.
- Key-person or vendor dependencies are managed.
- Competing priorities threaten delivery.
- Ownership is clear.

### Deliverable and quality health

Determines whether:

- Required deliverables are progressing.
- Quality and acceptance criteria are being met.
- Testing, review, and approval are occurring.
- Rework or defects threaten outcomes.
- Delivered work is implemented and usable.

### Outcome and benefit health

Determines whether:

- The initiative is producing or is likely to produce the intended outcome.
- Measures are defined.
- Baselines and targets exist.
- Adoption and benefit realization are occurring.
- Activity and output are not being mistaken for value.

### Risk and issue health

Determines whether:

- Risks remain within tolerance.
- Issues have credible resolution plans.
- Mitigations are progressing.
- Exposure is increasing.
- Specialized escalation is required.

### Dependency health

Determines whether:

- Critical inputs will be available.
- Providers and consumers are aligned.
- Shared resources and approvals are coordinated.
- Alternatives and contingencies exist.
- Delay may propagate through the portfolio.

### Decision and accountability health

Determines whether:

- Decisions occur in time.
- Owners are clear.
- Actions and commitments are completed.
- Escalations are resolved.
- Governance supports timely execution.

### Stakeholder and adoption health

Determines whether:

- Required stakeholders are engaged.
- Sponsor support remains active.
- Affected groups are prepared.
- Communications, training, and change management are sufficient.
- Resistance or misalignment threatens adoption.

### Governance and assurance health

Determines whether:

- Required reviews and approvals are defined.
- Security, privacy, legal, clinical, financial, regulatory, architecture, procurement, accessibility, and records-management needs are addressed.
- Exceptions are documented.
- Accountability and monitoring are in place.

## Core activities

### 1. Define the health framework

- Confirm the approved health dimensions.
- Confirm rating definitions.
- Confirm escalation thresholds.
- Establish the reporting cutoff date.
- Retrieve the previous assessment.
- Identify owners and required reviewers.

Do not silently invent official status definitions.

### 2. Validate source information

For each dimension:

- Identify the source.
- Confirm its date.
- Confirm the owner.
- Check completeness.
- Compare conflicting reports.
- Assess confidence.
- Flag missing evidence.

The absence of adverse reporting is not evidence of good health.

### 3. Assess each dimension separately

Use approved thresholds and documented evidence.

For every rating:

- State the rating.
- Explain why.
- Identify what changed.
- Cite the evidence.
- Identify owner commentary.
- Identify corrective action.
- State confidence.

Do not let favorable schedule status obscure material outcome, quality, risk, or governance problems.

### 4. Determine overall health

Use the approved overall-health method.

When no method is provided, avoid a simple average. Consider whether a material exception should determine overall health.

Illustrative definitions:

#### On track

- Objectives remain achievable.
- No material unresolved condition threatens success.
- Risks and dependencies have credible management plans.

#### Watch

- A concern requires active monitoring or corrective action.
- Objectives remain achievable without major intervention.

#### At risk

- One or more material conditions threaten outcomes, timing, cost, quality, adoption, or obligations.
- Leadership action or a significant recovery plan is required.

#### Off track

- The initiative is unlikely to achieve a major approved outcome under the current plan.
- Replanning, rescoping, major intervention, pause, or termination should be considered.

### 5. Compare with the previous assessment

Identify:

- Status changes.
- Forecast changes.
- New or closed risks.
- Milestone movement.
- Scope changes.
- Resource changes.
- Decision delays.
- Changes in confidence.
- Outcome or adoption changes.
- New evidence.

Explain whether the change reflects actual performance or better information.

### 6. Diagnose root causes

For each material health concern:

- Distinguish symptoms from causes.
- Identify dependencies.
- Identify ownership.
- Identify missing decisions or resources.
- Identify systemic patterns.
- Avoid unsupported attribution or personnel judgment.

### 7. Recommend corrective action

Recommendations may include:

- Clarify scope.
- Accelerate a decision.
- Resolve a dependency.
- Adjust resources.
- Strengthen governance.
- Revise delivery sequencing.
- Improve testing or acceptance.
- Increase stakeholder engagement.
- Establish a recovery plan.
- Reforecast.
- Rebaseline through approved change control.
- Rescope.
- Pause.
- Terminate.

For each recommendation, identify owner, required decision, timing, and expected effect.

### 8. Monitor recovery

For initiatives under recovery:

- Define recovery milestones.
- Identify evidence of improvement.
- Establish review cadence.
- Identify escalation triggers.
- Track whether corrective action changes the underlying health condition.
- Avoid returning to On track solely because a plan was created.

## Decision rules

- Assess multiple health dimensions.
- Use approved definitions and thresholds.
- Do not average dimension scores blindly.
- Allow a material exception to drive overall health when justified.
- Distinguish current performance from forecast.
- Distinguish activity, output, and outcome.
- Distinguish owner status from analytical assessment.
- Do not mark a concern resolved because a recovery plan exists.
- Preserve baseline dates and approved changes.
- Do not rebaseline solely to improve status.
- Explain every status change.
- Label low-confidence assessments.
- Connect recommendations to specific evidence and decisions.

## Quality standards

A strong health assessment must be:

- Holistic.
- Evidence-based.
- Current.
- Comparative.
- Outcome-focused.
- Exception-aware.
- Confidence-aware.
- Actionable.
- Traceable.
- Fair and reviewable.

## Safeguards and limitations

- Do not fabricate health ratings, progress, forecasts, ownership, or outcomes.
- Do not conceal unfavorable status.
- Do not make personnel or disciplinary judgments.
- Do not replace formal financial, clinical, legal, regulatory, security, privacy, audit, or governance review.
- Do not modify plans, budgets, scope, ownership, or status automatically.
- Do not expose restricted information to unauthorized audiences.
- Flag stale, missing, conflicting, or unsupported evidence.
- Require human approval for escalation, recovery, rebaselining, rescoping, pause, or termination.

## Human role

The initiative owner, program lead, sponsor, finance or resource partner, risk owner, specialized assurance partner, governance body, or executive must:

- Validate source information.
- Approve health definitions.
- Review dimension ratings.
- Confirm overall health.
- Resolve conflicting assessments.
- Authorize corrective actions.
- Approve resource, scope, schedule, or governance changes.
- Decide whether to continue, recover, pause, rescope, or stop the initiative.
