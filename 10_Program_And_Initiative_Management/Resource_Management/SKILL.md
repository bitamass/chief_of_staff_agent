# Skill: Resource Management

## Purpose

This skill evaluates resource supply, demand, allocation, capacity, capability, cost, and continuity across initiatives and operational commitments.

It helps leadership determine:

- What people, skills, funding, technology, vendors, facilities, or equipment are required.
- What capacity is currently available.
- Where overallocations and shortages exist.
- Which initiatives compete for the same constrained resources.
- Which capabilities require hiring, contracting, training, or partnership.
- Whether current assignments are sustainable.
- What work must be sequenced, rescoped, deferred, or stopped.
- Which resource decisions leadership must make.

The skill provides planning and decision support. It does not hire, assign, reassign, schedule, evaluate, or terminate personnel automatically.

## Resource categories

Resources may include:

- People and teams.
- Specialized expertise.
- Executive and management attention.
- Contractors and vendors.
- Budget and funding.
- Technology platforms and environments.
- Data access and analytical capacity.
- Facilities and equipment.
- Governance and assurance capacity.
- Operational support and change-management capacity.

Resource analysis should not be limited to headcount.

## Core activities

### 1. Establish planning scope

Confirm:

- Planning period.
- Initiatives and operations in scope.
- Strategic priorities.
- Mandatory commitments.
- Budget limits.
- Minimum operational coverage.
- Approved allocation assumptions.
- Decision authority.
- Data cutoff date.

### 2. Create the resource inventory

Identify available:

- Roles.
- Skills.
- Teams.
- Vendors.
- Budget.
- Technology.
- Facilities.
- Equipment.
- Leadership and governance capacity.

For every resource:

- Identify ownership.
- Confirm availability.
- Record constraints.
- Identify existing assignments.
- Identify backup coverage.
- Confirm source and date.

### 3. Define demand

For each initiative or operational commitment:

- Identify the specific capability required.
- Estimate level of effort.
- Define start and end dates.
- Identify critical milestones.
- Identify minimum skill or authorization.
- Identify whether the demand is confirmed or forecast.
- State consequences if demand is unmet.

Avoid requests such as “need more resources” without specifying role, skill, timing, allocation, and outcome.
