# Skill: Milestone Tracking

## Purpose

This skill identifies, monitors, and reports meaningful initiative and program milestones.

It helps leadership understand:

- Which important events, decisions, approvals, deliverables, or outcomes are approaching.
- Whether milestone criteria are likely to be satisfied on time.
- What has changed from the approved baseline.
- Which dependencies, risks, issues, resources, or decisions threaten progress.
- How a delayed milestone affects downstream work.
- What recovery action or leadership intervention is required.
- Whether a reported milestone was actually achieved.

A milestone is a significant zero-duration checkpoint or outcome. It is not merely a routine task or a percentage-complete measure.

## Core milestone types

### Decision milestone

An authorized decision must be made before work can continue.

### Approval milestone

A designated person or body must formally approve an artifact, design, investment, exception, or implementation.

### Deliverable milestone

A material deliverable reaches a defined completion or acceptance point.

### Technical milestone

A platform, architecture, integration, environment, test, or release condition is achieved.

### Governance or assurance milestone

A required security, privacy, legal, clinical, regulatory, procurement, finance, accessibility, or policy review is completed.

### Operational-readiness milestone

Training, support, documentation, communications, staffing, adoption, and continuity requirements are ready.

### Outcome milestone

A measurable result, adoption level, or benefit threshold is reached.

## Core activities

### 1. Identify meaningful milestones

Extract milestones from:

- Charters.
- Roadmaps.
- Integrated schedules.
- Deliverable plans.
- Decision logs.
- Governance requirements.
- Contracts.
- Meeting records.
- Risk and dependency registers.
- Operational-readiness plans.

A valid milestone should represent a meaningful checkpoint that affects decisions, sequencing, accountability, or outcomes.

Avoid classifying every task completion as a milestone.

### 2. Define completion criteria

For every milestone:

- Define what must be true.
- Identify required evidence.
- Identify required deliverables.
- Identify reviewer and approver.
- Identify required dependencies.
- Identify governance and assurance requirements.
- Define acceptance or exit criteria.

A milestone without objective completion criteria cannot be reliably tracked.

### 3. Preserve the baseline

Record:

- Original baseline date.
- Approved revised baseline, when applicable.
- Current forecast.
- Actual completion date.

Do not overwrite the original baseline when the forecast changes. Preserve schedule history and the authority approving any rebaseline.

### 4. Assess readiness

Evaluate:

- Supporting work.
- Deliverable readiness.
- Dependency readiness.
- Resource availability.
- Review and approval readiness.
- Risk and issue status.
- Evidence completeness.
- Decision-material readiness.
- Remaining time.

Readiness should reflect the likelihood that all criteria will be satisfied—not merely whether work is underway.

### 5. Determine milestone status

Use approved definitions when available.

Illustrative status guidance:

#### On track

- Criteria are expected to be met by the current target date.
- No material unresolved condition threatens achievement.

#### Watch

- A concern requires monitoring or corrective action.
- Achievement remains likely without major intervention.

#### At risk

- A material dependency, issue, decision, resource constraint, or incomplete criterion threatens the date.
- Recovery action or leadership support is required.

#### Off track

- The milestone is unlikely to be achieved by the target date under the current plan.
- A revised plan or decision is required.

#### Blocked

- Progress cannot continue because a required input, decision, approval, or resource is unavailable.

#### Achieved

- All defined completion and acceptance criteria are satisfied.
- Required evidence exists.
- Authorized validation is complete.

#### Achieved with conditions

- The milestone was approved or accepted subject to documented remaining conditions.
- Conditions, owners, and due dates must remain visible.

### 6. Analyze schedule impact

When a milestone forecast changes:

- Calculate variance.
- Identify the cause.
- Identify critical-path effect.
- Identify downstream milestones.
- Assess available float.
- Identify recovery options.
- Update forecasts without rewriting the baseline.
- Determine required escalation.

Do not assume every milestone delay changes the final completion date; evaluate schedule logic.

### 7. Track decisions and approvals

For every decision or approval milestone:

- Identify the authorized person or body.
- Define required materials.
- Confirm submission timing.
- Record outstanding questions.
- Track review duration.
- Identify consequence of delay.
- Escalate preparation gaps early enough for action.

### 8. Recommend recovery actions

Potential responses include:

- Complete missing criteria.
- Resolve a dependency.
- Accelerate a decision.
- Add review capacity.
- Resequence work.
- Use a partial or phased milestone.
- Activate contingency.
- Revise scope.
- Adjust resources.
- Reforecast.
- Rebaseline through approved change control.
- Escalate.

Every recommendation must identify the owner, required action, and date.

### 9. Validate achievement

A milestone should be marked Achieved only when:

- Criteria are met.
- Evidence exists.
- Required review or approval occurred.
- Acceptance is documented.
- Residual conditions are recorded.

Attendance at a meeting, submission of a draft, or owner assertion alone may not prove achievement.

### 10. Analyze milestone reliability

Across the portfolio, detect:

- Repeatedly moved milestones.
- Optimistic forecasting.
- Approval bottlenecks.
- Weak completion criteria.
- Dependency failures.
- Resource constraints.
- Milestones achieved without evidence.
- Schedule changes without formal authorization.
- Patterns affecting future planning confidence.

## Decision rules

- Track meaningful checkpoints, not every task.
- Define completion and acceptance criteria.
- Preserve baseline, forecast, and actual dates separately.
- Do not mark a milestone achieved without evidence.
- Distinguish ready for review, pending approval, and achieved.
- Distinguish achieved from achieved with conditions.
- Do not infer approval from submission or silence.
- Do not overwrite schedule history.
- Do not rebaseline solely to eliminate unfavorable variance.
- Assess downstream impact using schedule logic.
- Separate current status from recommended change.
- Label confidence and missing information.

## Quality standards

A strong milestone-tracking output must be:

- Significant.
- Criteria-based.
- Time-bound.
- Baseline-aware.
- Dependency-aware.
- Evidence-based.
- Forward-looking.
- Exception-oriented.
- Traceable.
- Actionable.

## Safeguards and limitations

- Do not fabricate milestones, dates, ownership, evidence, approval, or achievement.
- Do not modify schedules or baselines automatically.
- Do not treat generated status as formal acceptance.
- Do not conceal missed dates.
- Do not make personnel or performance judgments.
- Do not expose restricted financial, clinical, personnel, legal, security, privacy, vendor, or regulatory information.
- Flag stale, conflicting, incomplete, or unsupported information.
- Require human approval for escalation, recovery, schedule change, rebaseline, deferment, cancellation, or closure.

## Human role

The milestone owner, program or project manager, approver, initiative owner, sponsor, governance body, or executive must:

- Confirm milestone significance.
- Approve completion criteria.
- Validate dates and forecasts.
- Confirm dependencies.
- Authorize recovery and schedule changes.
- Approve rebaselines.
- Validate achievement or conditional achievement.
- Resolve escalations and priority conflicts.
