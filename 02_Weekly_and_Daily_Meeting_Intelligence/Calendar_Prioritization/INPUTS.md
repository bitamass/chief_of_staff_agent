# Inputs — Calendar Prioritization

## Required inputs

* Executive calendar for the defined review period.
* Meeting title, date, start time, duration, location, and format.
* Meeting organizer and invited participants.
* Participant names, roles, organizations, and relationship to the executive.
* Meeting objective, agenda, and expected outcome.
* Decisions, approvals, or executive contributions required.
* Strategic goal, priority, initiative, or commitment supported by each meeting.
* Executive role in the meeting: decision-maker, presenter, advisor, participant, or optional attendee.
* Preparation requirements and required materials.
* Scheduling constraints, travel time, transition time, and protected focus time.
* Known deadlines, milestones, risks, dependencies, and time-sensitive commitments.
* Prior meeting history, unresolved actions, and prior attendance commitments.
* Delegation options and qualified alternative attendees.

## Calendar-priority factors

For each calendar item, collect:

* Strategic relevance.
* Decision importance.
* Executive participation requirement.
* Urgency and time sensitivity.
* Stakeholder importance.
* Consequence of nonattendance.
* Opportunity cost.
* Preparation effort.
* Ability to delegate.
* Ability to reschedule.
* Recurrence and frequency.
* Relationship to other meetings, deadlines, or decisions.
* Confidence that the meeting objective and expected outcome are clear.

## Scheduling constraints

Document:

* Fixed or immovable meetings.
* Regulatory, governance, board, clinical, or contractual commitments.
* Required travel and transition time.
* Time-zone considerations.
* Protected preparation, focus, and recovery time.
* Personal or confidential calendar blocks.
* Maximum preferred meeting load.
* Required buffers before high-stakes meetings.
* Scheduling preferences established by the executive.
* Meetings that require specific participants to attend together.

## Calendar-conflict signals

Look for:

* Overlapping meetings.
* Insufficient preparation time.
* Back-to-back high-stakes meetings.
* Meetings without a clear purpose, agenda, or expected outcome.
* Meetings that do not require executive participation.
* Recurring meetings producing limited value.
* Multiple meetings addressing the same subject.
* Meetings scheduled before required evidence or decisions are available.
* Missing decision-makers or essential participants.
* Meetings that conflict with strategic deadlines or protected focus time.
* Excessive travel or context-switching.
* Invitations whose attendance status is unclear.

## Optional supporting inputs

* Executive priorities and weekly or daily plan.
* Initiative and portfolio roadmaps.
* Decision and action-item registers.
* Stakeholder relationship information.
* Meeting effectiveness feedback.
* Historical attendance and cancellation patterns.
* Delegation-of-authority guidance.
* Travel schedules.
* Preparation materials and briefing requirements.
* Organizational scheduling policies.

## Input validation

Before prioritizing the calendar:

* Confirm that calendar information is current and covers the same review period.
* Verify meeting objectives, expected outcomes, and the executive’s required role.
* Confirm whether attendance is required, optional, or delegable.
* Identify missing agendas, participants, preparation requirements, or decision needs.
* Distinguish confirmed meetings from tentative holds.
* Identify confidential or personal entries that should not be analyzed or disclosed.
* Flag duplicate, overlapping, outdated, cancelled, or incomplete invitations.
* Do not assume a senior attendee or large participant list automatically makes a meeting important.
* Do not infer that the executive can delegate, decline, shorten, or reschedule a meeting without authorization.
