# Outputs — Calendar Prioritization

## Executive calendar summary

Provide a concise overview of:

* Highest-priority meetings.
* Meetings requiring significant preparation.
* Calendar conflicts or insufficient transition time.
* Meetings that may be delegated, shortened, rescheduled, combined, or declined.
* Important decisions, deadlines, and stakeholder interactions.
* Protected focus or preparation time at risk.

## Prioritized calendar

Present calendar items in date and time order. For each item, provide:

* Meeting title.
* Date, time, duration, location, and format.
* Organizer and key participants.
* Meeting objective and expected outcome.
* Strategic goal, initiative, decision, or commitment supported.
* Executive role.
* Priority: critical, high, medium, or low.
* Attendance recommendation.
* Preparation required.
* Key materials needed.
* Conflicts, dependencies, or risks.
* Confidence level: high, medium, or low.

## Attendance recommendations

Assign one recommendation to each meeting:

* **Attend:** Executive participation provides material value.
* **Attend with preparation:** Participation is important and requires briefing or review.
* **Attend selected portion:** Executive presence is required only for a defined agenda item.
* **Delegate:** A qualified representative can fulfill the required role.
* **Shorten:** The objective can be achieved in less time.
* **Combine:** The meeting substantially overlaps with another calendar item.
* **Reschedule:** Timing prevents effective participation or required preparation.
* **Decline:** Executive participation is not necessary or the meeting lacks sufficient value.
* **Request clarification:** Purpose, agenda, participants, or expected outcome is insufficiently defined.

Explain the evidence and rationale for each recommendation.

## Calendar conflicts and capacity concerns

Identify:

* Overlapping meetings.
* Back-to-back high-stakes meetings.
* Insufficient preparation or transition time.
* Missing travel time.
* Excessive context switching.
* Strategic deadlines without protected work time.
* Recurring meetings with limited demonstrated value.
* Meetings scheduled before necessary evidence or participants are available.
* Confidential or personal calendar entries excluded from analysis.

## Preparation priorities

For each high-priority meeting, identify:

* Decisions or approvals expected.
* Key questions the executive should consider.
* Required background materials.
* Prior decisions and commitments to review.
* Open actions, risks, and dependencies.
* Stakeholders requiring attention.
* Preparation owner.
* Preparation deadline.
* Recommended preparation time.

## Recommended calendar changes

Provide a proposed list of:

* Meetings to protect.
* Meetings to delegate.
* Meetings to shorten.
* Meetings to combine.
* Meetings to reschedule.
* Meetings to decline.
* Focus or preparation blocks to add.
* Buffers or travel time to protect.

Present all changes as recommendations, not confirmed calendar updates.

## Executive decisions required

Identify calendar choices that require executive direction, including:

* Conflicting high-priority meetings.
* Delegation decisions.
* Trade-offs between strategic and operational commitments.
* Meetings requiring changed attendance or scope.
* Requests that exceed available calendar capacity.

For each, provide the decision deadline and consequence of delay.

## Information gaps and confidence

Identify:

* Missing agendas or meeting objectives.
* Unclear executive role.
* Unconfirmed participants.
* Unknown preparation requirements.
* Unclear delegation authority.
* Tentative or outdated meeting information.
* Confidence level for each material recommendation.

## Human-review requirement

The executive or authorized calendar manager must approve all attendance, delegation, cancellation, rescheduling, or calendar-block recommendations before any calendar is changed or any response is sent.
