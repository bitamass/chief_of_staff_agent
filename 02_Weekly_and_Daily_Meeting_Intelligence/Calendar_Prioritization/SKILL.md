# Calendar Prioritization

## Purpose

Help an executive use limited time intentionally by identifying which meetings require participation, preparation, delegation, or calendar adjustment. Protect time for strategic work, decisions, preparation, travel, and transitions.

## Instructions

1. Review the calendar and supporting materials defined in `INPUTS.md`.
2. Confirm the review period, executive priorities, scheduling constraints, and protected time.
3. For each meeting, identify:

   * Objective and expected outcome.
   * Organizer and essential participants.
   * Strategic goal, initiative, decision, or commitment supported.
   * Executive role.
   * Decisions or contributions required.
   * Preparation effort.
   * Consequence of nonattendance.
   * Delegation and rescheduling options.
4. Distinguish confirmed meetings from tentative holds, cancelled events, focus blocks, travel time, and personal or confidential entries.
5. Evaluate each meeting using:

   * Strategic relevance.
   * Decision importance.
   * Executive participation requirement.
   * Urgency.
   * Stakeholder importance.
   * Consequence of nonattendance.
   * Opportunity cost.
   * Preparation effort.
   * Ability to delegate.
   * Ability to reschedule.
6. Assign a priority:

   * **Critical:** Attendance is essential for a significant decision, obligation, risk, or stakeholder outcome.
   * **High:** Executive participation provides substantial strategic or operational value.
   * **Medium:** The meeting is useful but may be delegated, shortened, or rescheduled.
   * **Low:** Executive participation has limited demonstrated value.
7. Assign one attendance recommendation:

   * Attend.
   * Attend with preparation.
   * Attend selected portion.
   * Delegate.
   * Shorten.
   * Combine.
   * Reschedule.
   * Decline.
   * Request clarification.
8. Explain each recommendation using the available evidence.
9. Identify calendar conflicts, including:

   * Overlapping meetings.
   * Insufficient preparation or transition time.
   * Back-to-back high-stakes meetings.
   * Missing travel time.
   * Excessive context switching.
   * Strategic deadlines without protected work time.
10. Identify recurring or overlapping meetings that may provide limited value or duplicate another forum.
11. Determine preparation requirements for critical and high-priority meetings.
12. Recommend protected preparation, focus, travel, and transition time where needed.
13. Identify attendance or scheduling choices requiring executive direction.
14. Present the results using the structure defined in `OUTPUTS.md`.

## Prioritization principles

* A senior organizer or large participant list does not automatically make a meeting important.
* Urgency and strategic importance are different considerations.
* A meeting without a clear objective or expected outcome should be clarified before it receives high priority.
* Executive attendance should be based on the required contribution, not habit or visibility alone.
* Delegation is appropriate only when a qualified person has the necessary authority and context.
* Calendar optimization should account for preparation, travel, transition, and focused work—not just open time.
* Previously accepted meetings should not be changed without reviewing commitments and stakeholder effects.

## Guardrails

* Use only approved calendar information, priorities, constraints, and supporting evidence.
* Do not disclose or analyze personal or confidential calendar details beyond the authorized purpose.
* Do not invent agendas, participant roles, attendance requirements, or delegation authority.
* Do not assume an executive may decline, delegate, shorten, combine, or reschedule a meeting.
* Clearly distinguish confirmed information, assumptions, analysis, and recommendations.
* Flag missing, conflicting, outdated, or tentative calendar information.
* Do not send responses, contact participants, or modify calendar events.
* Require approval from the executive or authorized calendar manager before any calendar change or communication.
