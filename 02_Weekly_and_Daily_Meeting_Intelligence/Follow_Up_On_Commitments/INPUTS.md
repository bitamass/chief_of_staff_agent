# Inputs — Follow Up on Commitments

## Required inputs

* Meeting notes, transcripts, summaries, and decision records.
* Existing commitment, action-item, and deliverable trackers.
* Confirmed commitments made by executives, teams, stakeholders, or external partners.
* Commitment owner and supporting contributors.
* Commitment date and source meeting or communication.
* Agreed deliverable, outcome, or completion criteria.
* Original due date and any authorized revised due date.
* Current status and most recent owner update.
* Evidence of progress or completion.
* Dependencies, risks, issues, and blockers.
* Related initiative, strategic goal, decision, milestone, or stakeholder expectation.
* Upcoming meetings, deadlines, or events affected by the commitment.
* Prior follow-up attempts, responses, and escalation history.

## Required commitment fields

For each commitment, collect:

* Unique commitment identifier.
* Clear commitment statement.
* Person or group that made the commitment.
* Accountable owner.
* Commitment recipient or affected stakeholder.
* Source meeting, decision, or communication.
* Date committed.
* Due date.
* Expected outcome or deliverable.
* Completion criteria.
* Current status.
* Latest update date.
* Dependencies and blockers.
* Follow-up date.
* Evidence of completion.
* Escalation history, if applicable.

## Commitment-status definitions

Use consistent status values:

* **Pending confirmation:** The commitment appears in the record but has not been verified.
* **Confirmed:** The owner, outcome, and due date are agreed.
* **In progress:** Work has started and evidence of progress is available.
* **At risk:** Delivery by the agreed date is uncertain.
* **Blocked:** Progress depends on an unresolved issue, resource, or decision.
* **Overdue:** The agreed due date has passed without verified completion.
* **Fulfilled:** The agreed completion criteria have been met.
* **Renegotiated:** Scope or timing changed through an authorized agreement.
* **Withdrawn:** The commitment was ended through an authorized decision.

## Follow-up signals

Look for commitments that:

* Are approaching their due date.
* Are overdue.
* Have not received a recent status update.
* Are blocked by an unresolved dependency or decision.
* Have been repeatedly delayed or renegotiated.
* Affect an upcoming executive meeting, milestone, or decision.
* Carry significant operational, financial, clinical, security, privacy, compliance, or reputational consequences.
* Have unclear ownership or completion criteria.
* Were stated verbally but not formally documented.
* Were reportedly completed without supporting evidence.
* Depend on another commitment that is at risk or overdue.

## Follow-up preferences and constraints

Document:

* Authorized follow-up sender.
* Appropriate communication channel.
* Preferred tone and level of formality.
* Internal or external recipient status.
* Expected response timeframe.
* Reminder frequency.
* Escalation thresholds.
* Individuals who should be copied or informed.
* Confidentiality or sensitivity restrictions.
* Matters requiring executive approval before contact.

## Optional supporting inputs

* Email and collaboration-platform correspondence.
* Project plans and initiative charters.
* Decision and action-item registers.
* Stakeholder relationship information.
* Resource and capacity information.
* Risk and issue registers.
* Prior escalation decisions.
* Organizational communication protocols.
* External agreements, contracts, or service commitments.

## Input validation

Before recommending follow-up:

* Confirm that the commitment was explicitly made or formally assigned.
* Verify the accountable owner, expected outcome, and due date.
* Distinguish a commitment from a proposed action, request, discussion point, or assumption.
* Confirm whether scope or timing was officially changed.
* Verify status and completion using current evidence.
* Review prior follow-up to avoid unnecessary or duplicate outreach.
* Identify confidential, sensitive, or external matters requiring approval.
* Flag missing, conflicting, outdated, or unverified information.
* Do not infer that silence means agreement, progress, refusal, or completion.
* Do not assume authority to contact or escalate to any stakeholder.
