# Outputs — Follow Up on Commitments

## Executive commitment summary

Provide a concise overview of:

* Commitments due soon.
* Overdue, blocked, or at-risk commitments.
* Commitments fulfilled since the last review.
* Commitments without recent updates.
* Commitments requiring leadership attention or escalation.
* Upcoming meetings, milestones, or decisions affected by delayed commitments.

## Commitment register

For each commitment, provide:

* Commitment identifier.
* Clear commitment statement.
* Accountable owner.
* Commitment recipient or affected stakeholder.
* Source meeting, decision, or communication.
* Date committed.
* Original due date.
* Current authorized due date.
* Expected outcome or deliverable.
* Completion criteria.
* Current status.
* Latest update date.
* Dependencies and blockers.
* Related initiative, milestone, or decision.
* Evidence and source.
* Confidence level: high, medium, or low.

## Follow-up priorities

Organize commitments into:

* **Immediate follow-up:** Overdue, blocked, high-risk, or required for an imminent decision.
* **Follow up soon:** Approaching the due date or missing a current status update.
* **Monitor:** Progress is documented and the commitment is currently on track.
* **Verify completion:** Reported complete but supporting evidence or acceptance is missing.
* **No follow-up required:** Fulfilled, properly renegotiated, or formally withdrawn.

Explain the reason for each classification.

## Recommended follow-up actions

For each commitment requiring attention, provide:

* Recommended follow-up action.
* Authorized sender or role.
* Intended recipient.
* Recommended communication channel.
* Recommended follow-up date.
* Information or evidence requested.
* Expected response date.
* Stakeholders to inform, if authorized.
* Consequence of further delay.
* Escalation threshold.

## Draft follow-up messages

When requested, prepare a draft message that includes:

* The original commitment.
* Source and date of the commitment.
* Agreed deliverable or outcome.
* Current due date.
* Most recent known status.
* Requested update, evidence, or action.
* Requested response date.
* Relevant meeting, milestone, or decision affected.
* Professional and proportionate tone.

Label every message as a draft requiring human review and approval.

## Escalation candidates

Identify commitments that may require escalation because they:

* Are materially overdue.
* Have been repeatedly delayed or renegotiated.
* Block a strategic initiative, decision, or milestone.
* Create significant organizational risk.
* Lack an accountable owner.
* Require authority or resources beyond the owner’s control.
* Have received no response after authorized follow-up.

For each escalation candidate, provide:

* Reason for escalation.
* Prior follow-up history.
* Recommended escalation recipient or role.
* Decision or support required.
* Consequence of further delay.

Do not perform the escalation.

## Upcoming review timeline

Present commitments in date order, showing:

* Next follow-up date.
* Current due date.
* Related meeting or milestone.
* Expected evidence.
* Owner.
* Escalation threshold.

## Information gaps and confidence

Identify:

* Unconfirmed commitments.
* Missing owners or due dates.
* Unclear completion criteria.
* Missing status updates.
* Unverified completion claims.
* Conflicting follow-up records.
* Unauthorized changes to scope or timing.
* Confidence level for each material status assessment.

## Human-review requirement

An authorized human must review and approve all follow-up messages, recipients, due-date changes, status changes, and escalation recommendations before communication or external-system updates occur.
