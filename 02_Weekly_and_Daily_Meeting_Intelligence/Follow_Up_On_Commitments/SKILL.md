# Follow Up on Commitments

## Purpose

Help leadership maintain accountability for confirmed promises and obligations. Determine which commitments require follow-up, what information is needed, and when leadership involvement may be appropriate.

## Instructions

1. Review the commitment records and supporting materials defined in `INPUTS.md`.
2. Identify explicit commitments made in meetings, decisions, correspondence, and approved trackers.
3. Distinguish a confirmed commitment from:

   * A proposed action.
   * A request.
   * A discussion point.
   * An assumption.
   * A general expression of intent.
4. For each commitment, verify:

   * Commitment statement.
   * Accountable owner.
   * Recipient or affected stakeholder.
   * Source and commitment date.
   * Expected outcome or deliverable.
   * Completion criteria.
   * Original and current authorized due dates.
   * Current status.
   * Latest update.
   * Dependencies and blockers.
5. Compare current records with prior updates and follow-up history.
6. Classify each commitment as:

   * Pending confirmation.
   * Confirmed.
   * In progress.
   * At risk.
   * Blocked.
   * Overdue.
   * Fulfilled.
   * Renegotiated.
   * Withdrawn.
7. Verify completion using the agreed completion criteria and available evidence.
8. Prioritize commitments into:

   * Immediate follow-up.
   * Follow up soon.
   * Monitor.
   * Verify completion.
   * No follow-up required.
9. Consider urgency, strategic impact, consequence of delay, upcoming meetings, stakeholder sensitivity, and prior follow-up attempts.
10. Recommend the appropriate:

    * Follow-up date.
    * Sender or role.
    * Recipient.
    * Communication channel.
    * Information or evidence requested.
    * Response deadline.
11. Draft a follow-up message only when requested.
12. Ensure each draft accurately references the commitment, due date, known status, requested response, and consequence for related work.
13. Identify escalation candidates based on approved thresholds, material delay, repeated nonresponse, unresolved blockers, or significant organizational consequences.
14. Present results using the structure defined in `OUTPUTS.md`.

## Follow-up principles

* Follow-up should be timely, specific, professional, and proportionate.
* A reminder should state what was committed, when it was due, and what response is needed.
* Follow-up should not imply misconduct when evidence only shows delay or missing information.
* Repeated reminders should consider prior responses, revised dates, and stakeholder context.
* Escalation should seek resolution or support, not punishment.
* Reported completion should be verified against the agreed criteria.
* Authorized changes to scope or timing should remain traceable.

## Guardrails

* Use only confirmed commitments and evidence supplied in the approved inputs.
* Do not invent commitments, owners, due dates, status updates, responses, or completion evidence.
* Do not assume silence means agreement, refusal, progress, or completion.
* Do not contact stakeholders, send reminders, or update external systems.
* Do not escalate a commitment automatically.
* Do not change ownership, scope, due dates, or status without authorized evidence.
* Clearly distinguish facts, assumptions, recommendations, and draft communications.
* Preserve the source and follow-up history of each commitment.
* Flag confidential, sensitive, external, conflicting, or unverified information.
* Require authorized human review before any follow-up or escalation communication is sent.
