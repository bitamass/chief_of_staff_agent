# Create Agendas

## Purpose

Create an agenda that directs the meeting toward defined outcomes. Ensure each agenda item has a clear purpose, owner, required participants, preparation materials, expected result, and realistic time allocation.

## Instructions

1. Review the meeting information and supporting materials defined in `INPUTS.md`.
2. Confirm:

   * Meeting purpose.
   * Desired outcome.
   * Date, duration, location, and format.
   * Meeting owner and facilitator.
   * Required participants and decision-makers.
3. Review prior decisions, unresolved action items, milestones, risks, issues, and upcoming deadlines relevant to the meeting.
4. Collect proposed agenda topics from the supplied materials.
5. For each proposed topic, determine whether its purpose is to:

   * Inform.
   * Discuss.
   * Decide.
   * Review.
   * Plan.
   * Escalate.
6. Remove, combine, defer, or place in a parking lot topics that:

   * Do not support the meeting purpose.
   * Duplicate another item.
   * Can be handled asynchronously.
   * Lack essential evidence or participants.
   * Cannot fit within the available time.
7. Prioritize remaining items using:

   * Strategic relevance.
   * Urgency.
   * Decision deadline.
   * Consequence of delay.
   * Readiness.
   * Need for synchronous discussion.
8. For each agenda item, define:

   * Title.
   * Purpose.
   * Desired outcome.
   * Owner or presenter.
   * Required participants.
   * Supporting materials.
   * Allocated time.
   * Decision or action expected.
9. Sequence items according to dependencies, participant availability, urgency, and meeting flow.
10. For every decision item, confirm:

    * Decision question.
    * Decision authority.
    * Available options.
    * Required evidence.
    * Decision-readiness status.
11. Include time to:

    * Confirm the meeting objective.
    * Review relevant prior commitments.
    * Transition between items.
    * Summarize decisions and action items.
    * Confirm owners, due dates, and next steps.
12. Verify that the total allocated time does not exceed the meeting duration.
13. Identify missing materials, participants, owners, or preparation requirements.
14. Assign an agenda-readiness status:

    * Ready.
    * Partially ready.
    * Not ready.
15. Present the agenda using the structure defined in `OUTPUTS.md`.

## Agenda-design principles

* Every agenda item must support the stated meeting purpose.
* Every item should have a defined outcome, not merely a topic.
* Decision items should be clearly distinguished from informational updates.
* Items that can be resolved asynchronously should not consume meeting time without justification.
* High-priority items should receive sufficient discussion or decision time.
* The agenda should reserve time to confirm decisions, actions, owners, and due dates.
* A full agenda is not necessarily an effective agenda.

## Guardrails

* Use only approved meeting information and supporting evidence.
* Do not invent meeting objectives, agenda topics, participants, owners, decisions, or materials.
* Do not assume a person has agreed to present, attend, or make a decision.
* Do not mark a decision item ready when material evidence or authority is missing.
* Clearly distinguish confirmed items from proposed or incomplete items.
* Flag confidential topics requiring restricted attendance or materials.
* Do not distribute the agenda, invite participants, or modify calendar events.
* Require approval from the meeting owner or authorized agenda manager before distribution.
