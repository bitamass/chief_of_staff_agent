# Inputs — Create Agendas

## Required inputs

* Meeting title, date, start time, duration, location, and format.
* Meeting purpose and desired outcome.
* Meeting owner, facilitator, and agenda-preparation owner.
* Invited participants and their roles.
* Required decision-makers and subject-matter experts.
* Strategic goal, initiative, project, or operational responsibility supported by the meeting.
* Proposed discussion topics and requests from participants.
* Decisions, approvals, or recommendations expected.
* Prior meeting notes, decisions, commitments, and unresolved action items.
* Relevant milestones, deliverables, deadlines, risks, issues, and dependencies.
* Required pre-reading or supporting materials.
* Time constraints and fixed agenda items.

## Required agenda-item fields

For each proposed agenda item, collect:

* Agenda-item title.
* Purpose:

  * Inform.
  * Discuss.
  * Decide.
  * Review.
  * Plan.
  * Escalate.
* Desired outcome.
* Item owner or presenter.
* Required participants.
* Supporting materials.
* Estimated time.
* Decision or action expected.
* Related initiative, milestone, risk, issue, or prior commitment.
* Priority.
* Readiness status.
* Dependencies on other agenda items.

## Agenda-prioritization factors

Evaluate each proposed item based on:

* Relevance to the meeting purpose.
* Strategic or operational importance.
* Urgency.
* Decision deadline.
* Consequence of delay.
* Participant availability.
* Readiness of evidence or materials.
* Need for synchronous discussion.
* Relationship to unresolved decisions or commitments.
* Time required relative to available meeting time.

## Agenda-design constraints

Document:

* Total meeting duration.
* Required opening, closing, and transition time.
* Fixed agenda items.
* Participant arrival or departure constraints.
* Required sequencing of agenda items.
* Confidential or restricted topics.
* Decisions requiring quorum or specific authority.
* Accessibility, format, or technology requirements.
* Topics that should be handled asynchronously.
* Required preparation time before the meeting.

## Optional supporting inputs

* Meeting charter or recurring-meeting purpose.
* Prior agendas.
* Decision and action-item registers.
* Executive priorities.
* Initiative and portfolio status reports.
* Risk and issue registers.
* Stakeholder requests.
* Participant biographies or role descriptions.
* Governance and approval requirements.
* Relevant executive briefing materials.
* Feedback on previous meeting effectiveness.

## Input validation

Before creating the agenda:

* Confirm that the meeting has a clear purpose and desired outcome.
* Verify the meeting duration and essential participants.
* Confirm that decision-makers have the required authority.
* Identify agenda items that are incomplete, duplicative, or unrelated to the meeting purpose.
* Confirm that decision items have sufficient evidence and defined options.
* Identify missing item owners, materials, desired outcomes, or time estimates.
* Verify that the total proposed time fits within the meeting duration.
* Distinguish items requiring live discussion from items that can be handled asynchronously.
* Flag confidential topics requiring restricted materials or attendance.
* Do not infer that a proposed topic has been approved for the agenda.
