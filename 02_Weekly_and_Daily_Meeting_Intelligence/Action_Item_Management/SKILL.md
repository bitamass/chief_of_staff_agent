# Action Item Management

## Purpose

Maintain a complete and traceable record of executive action items from assignment through verified completion. Make ownership, deadlines, dependencies, blockers, and accountability concerns visible.

## Instructions

1. Review the materials defined in `INPUTS.md`.
2. Identify explicit and potential action items in meeting notes, decisions, commitments, and follow-up records.
3. Distinguish each item as:

   * Action item.
   * Decision.
   * Discussion topic.
   * Informational update.
   * Risk or issue.
4. Write each action as a clear statement beginning with a verb.
5. For every action item, capture:

   * Unique identifier.
   * Source meeting or decision.
   * Related initiative, deliverable, or strategic goal.
   * Accountable owner.
   * Supporting contributors.
   * Date assigned.
   * Due date.
   * Priority.
   * Completion criteria.
   * Dependencies.
   * Risks or blockers.
   * Next review date or meeting.
6. Mark an action as **Proposed** when ownership, scope, or deadline has not been confirmed.
7. Use the status definitions in `INPUTS.md` consistently.
8. Compare current actions with prior records to identify:

   * New actions.
   * Changed actions.
   * Completed actions.
   * Overdue actions.
   * Blocked or at-risk actions.
   * Deferred or cancelled actions.
   * Duplicate, conflicting, or superseded actions.
9. Verify status changes using current supporting evidence.
10. Do not mark an action complete unless its completion criteria have been met and evidence is available.
11. Organize actions by status, due date, initiative, owner, and executive importance.
12. Identify actions due before upcoming meetings, milestones, or decisions.
13. Highlight actions missing an owner, due date, priority, dependency, or completion criterion.
14. Recommend follow-up or escalation when an action:

* Is overdue and strategically important.
* Blocks another commitment or decision.
* Has been repeatedly deferred.
* Has no accountable owner.
* Requires authority or resources beyond the owner’s control.
* Creates material risk if delayed.

15. Present the results using the structure defined in `OUTPUTS.md`.

## Action-management principles

* A discussion point is not an action item.
* A decision may create an action, but the decision and action must remain separate records.
* A person mentioned during discussion is not automatically the accountable owner.
* “In progress” does not mean “on track.”
* An owner’s statement that work is finished is not sufficient when defined completion evidence is missing.
* Changing a due date does not erase the original deadline or delay history.
* Closed, cancelled, and deferred items should remain traceable.

## Guardrails

* Use only evidence supplied in the approved inputs.
* Do not invent actions, owners, assignments, deadlines, status updates, dependencies, or completion evidence.
* Do not assign an action to a person without authorized confirmation.
* Do not change an action’s scope, priority, owner, due date, or status without evidence.
* Clearly distinguish facts, assumptions, proposed actions, and confirmed actions.
* Preserve the source and change history of each action.
* Flag incomplete, conflicting, outdated, or unverified information.
* Do not automatically send reminders, update external systems, close actions, or escalate items.
* Require authorized human review before the action register is treated as authoritative or communicated to stakeholders.
