# Outputs — Action Item Management

## Executive action summary

Provide a concise overview of:

* Total open action items.
* Actions due soon.
* Overdue, blocked, or at-risk actions.
* Actions completed since the last review.
* Actions missing owners, due dates, or completion criteria.
* Items requiring leadership intervention or escalation.

## Action-item register

For each action item, provide:

* Action-item identifier.
* Clear action statement.
* Source meeting or decision.
* Related initiative, deliverable, or strategic goal.
* Accountable owner.
* Supporting contributors.
* Date assigned.
* Due date.
* Priority.
* Current status.
* Completion criteria.
* Dependencies.
* Risks or blockers.
* Next review date or meeting.
* Evidence or source.
* Last-updated date.

## Action status categories

Organize action items into:

* **Due soon:** Approaching the due date.
* **In progress:** Work is underway and currently on track.
* **At risk:** Completion by the due date is uncertain.
* **Blocked:** Progress depends on an unresolved decision, dependency, or issue.
* **Overdue:** Due date has passed without verified completion.
* **Complete:** Completion criteria have been met and supporting evidence is available.
* **Needs clarification:** Owner, due date, scope, or completion criteria are missing.
* **Deferred or cancelled:** Status change is supported by an authorized decision.

## Upcoming action timeline

Present action items in due-date order and identify:

* Actions due before the next executive meeting.
* Actions affecting upcoming milestones or decisions.
* Actions dependent on other incomplete work.
* Actions requiring stakeholder coordination.
* Actions whose delay may affect more than one initiative.

## Exceptions and accountability concerns

Identify:

* Actions without accountable owners.
* Actions without due dates.
* Actions without measurable completion criteria.
* Repeatedly deferred or overdue actions.
* Conflicting or duplicate actions.
* Status updates without supporting evidence.
* Completed actions awaiting verification.
* Actions assigned beyond an owner’s authority or capacity.
* Actions that no longer align with an active decision or priority.

## Recommended follow-up

For each action requiring attention, provide:

* Recommended follow-up.
* Responsible owner.
* Target follow-up date.
* Appropriate review meeting or forum.
* Evidence required.
* Consequence of further delay.
* Whether leadership intervention may be needed.

## Escalation candidates

Identify actions that may require escalation because they:

* Are overdue and materially affect an executive priority.
* Block another initiative, milestone, or decision.
* Present significant operational, financial, clinical, security, privacy, compliance, or reputational risk.
* Have no accountable owner.
* Have been repeatedly deferred.
* Require authority or resources beyond the owner’s control.

Present escalation as a recommendation, not an automatic action.

## Information gaps and confidence

Identify:

* Missing or unconfirmed ownership.
* Unclear deadlines.
* Undefined completion criteria.
* Unverified status updates.
* Missing dependencies.
* Conflicting source information.
* Confidence level for each major status assessment: high, medium, or low.

## Human-review requirement

An authorized human must review action assignments, status changes, deadline changes, completion, cancellation, and escalation before the action register is treated as authoritative or communicated to stakeholders.
