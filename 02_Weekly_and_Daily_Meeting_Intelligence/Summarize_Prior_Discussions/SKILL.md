# Summarize Prior Discussions

## Purpose

Create an accurate history of prior discussions so leadership can understand how the current situation developed. Preserve chronology, attribution, decisions, disagreements, commitments, changes, and unresolved questions.

## Instructions

1. Review the requested scope, time period, audience, and authorized sources defined in `INPUTS.md`.
2. Identify all relevant meetings, documents, decisions, communications, and tracking records.
3. Order the source materials chronologically.
4. For each relevant discussion, capture:

   * Date and source.
   * Purpose and context.
   * Key participants.
   * Topics and questions discussed.
   * Options or proposals considered.
   * Documented stakeholder positions.
   * Areas of agreement and disagreement.
   * Decisions made.
   * Matters deferred.
   * Open questions.
   * Actions and commitments created.
5. Distinguish clearly among:

   * Documented fact.
   * Participant position.
   * Proposal.
   * Assumption.
   * Recommendation.
   * Formal decision.
6. Track how the discussion evolved, including changes in:

   * Objectives or scope.
   * Evidence and assumptions.
   * Stakeholder positions.
   * Options under consideration.
   * Risks and issues.
   * Dependencies and constraints.
7. Identify formal decisions and determine whether each remains active, has been implemented, was superseded, was reversed, or requires confirmation.
8. Identify commitments and determine their current status using available evidence.
9. Preserve material disagreement, uncertainty, and minority positions.
10. Identify recurring concerns, unresolved questions, and gaps in follow-through.
11. Explain the current implications of the discussion history:

    * Prior decisions that remain relevant.
    * Commitments requiring attention.
    * Questions that remain open.
    * Matters that may require reconsideration because conditions changed.
12. Cite the source supporting every material decision, position, commitment, or change.
13. Identify missing meetings, incomplete records, conflicting sources, and uncertain attribution.
14. Present the history using the structure defined in `OUTPUTS.md`.

## Summarization principles

* Summarize the development of the discussion, not merely each document separately.
* Preserve chronology when sequence affects meaning.
* Formal decisions must remain distinct from proposals and recommendations.
* Lack of recorded objection does not establish consensus.
* Repeated discussion does not mean a decision was made.
* A later source does not automatically supersede an earlier decision.
* Concision must not remove material disagreement, uncertainty, or conditions attached to decisions.
* Historical summaries should explain what changed and why, when the evidence supports that conclusion.

## Guardrails

* Use only authorized and relevant source materials.
* Do not invent discussions, decisions, positions, participants, rationale, commitments, or consensus.
* Do not infer a participant’s position from title, attendance, silence, or organizational role.
* Do not attribute a statement when the source does not support attribution.
* Clearly distinguish facts, positions, proposals, assumptions, recommendations, and decisions.
* Flag conflicting, incomplete, outdated, inaccessible, or unverified records.
* Protect confidential, sensitive, personnel, clinical, legal, security, and privacy-restricted information.
* Do not communicate the summary as an official institutional record without authorization.
* Require human review for factual accuracy, attribution, completeness, and confidentiality.
