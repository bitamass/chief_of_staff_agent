# Inputs — Summarize Prior Discussions

## Required scope

* Topic, initiative, decision, issue, or question to be summarized.
* Intended audience.
* Purpose of the summary.
* Relevant time period.
* Required level of detail.
* Authorized sources and permitted data classifications.
* Confidentiality and distribution restrictions.

## Required source materials

* Meeting agendas.
* Meeting notes, minutes, transcripts, and summaries.
* Prior executive briefings.
* Decision logs and approval records.
* Action-item and commitment trackers.
* Relevant emails and collaboration-platform discussions.
* Initiative charters, business cases, and status reports.
* Risk and issue registers.
* Supporting presentations, reports, analyses, and data.
* Document owners, dates, versions, and source locations.

## Discussion-level information

For each relevant discussion, collect:

* Meeting or communication title.
* Date.
* Participants and roles.
* Topic or question discussed.
* Context and reason for discussion.
* Options or proposals considered.
* Documented stakeholder positions and concerns.
* Evidence or assumptions discussed.
* Areas of agreement.
* Areas of disagreement.
* Decisions made.
* Matters deferred.
* Open questions.
* Actions and commitments created.
* Changes from earlier discussions.
* Source reference.

## Continuity information

Collect:

* Earlier decisions still governing the topic.
* Decisions that were revised, superseded, or reversed.
* Commitments made and their current status.
* Actions completed, open, blocked, at risk, or overdue.
* Risks, issues, and dependencies raised over time.
* Recurring questions or concerns.
* Changes in scope, ownership, timing, evidence, or stakeholder position.
* Upcoming meetings, milestones, or decisions dependent on the history.

## Attribution requirements

For material statements, capture:

* Individual, group, or document associated with the statement.
* Whether it represents:

  * A documented fact.
  * A participant’s stated position.
  * A proposal.
  * An assumption.
  * A recommendation.
  * A formal decision.
* Source title.
* Source date.
* Relevant section or context.
* Confidence in the attribution.

## Optional supporting inputs

* Stakeholder profiles.
* Governance and approval requirements.
* Historical roadmaps or plans.
* Prior versions of proposals.
* External developments referenced during discussions.
* Meeting recordings, when authorized.
* Glossaries for specialized terminology.
* Relevant policies or institutional guidance.

## Input validation

Before summarizing prior discussions:

* Confirm that the requested scope and time period are clear.
* Verify that source materials are authorized, relevant, and current enough for the purpose.
* Distinguish formal minutes and decisions from informal notes or recollections.
* Identify duplicate records and conflicting versions.
* Confirm participant names, roles, dates, and attribution when available.
* Separate decisions from recommendations, preferences, proposals, and discussion.
* Preserve material disagreement and uncertainty.
* Identify missing meetings, documents, or time periods.
* Flag incomplete, conflicting, outdated, inaccessible, or unverified sources.
* Do not infer consensus from silence or lack of recorded objection.
* Do not infer a participant’s position from title, attendance, or organizational role.
