# Track Decisions

## Purpose

Create and maintain a reliable decision history. Preserve the exact direction, authority, source, rationale, conditions, implementation actions, and subsequent changes associated with each decision.

## Instructions

1. Review the decision sources and supporting materials defined in `INPUTS.md`.
2. Identify statements that may represent decisions.
3. Distinguish each statement as:

   * Proposal.
   * Recommendation.
   * Preference.
   * Discussion point.
   * Action item.
   * Pending decision.
   * Authorized decision.
4. Confirm the exact decision wording, decision date, authority, and source before classifying a decision as approved.
5. For each decision, capture:

   * Unique identifier.
   * Decision statement.
   * Decision owner or authorized authority.
   * Date and source.
   * Related goal, initiative, deliverable, risk, or issue.
   * Selected direction.
   * Documented rationale.
   * Conditions, limitations, and assumptions.
   * Effective and review dates.
   * Affected stakeholders.
6. Identify actions required to implement the decision, including owners, due dates, dependencies, and completion evidence.
7. Assign a decision status:

   * Proposed.
   * Pending confirmation.
   * Approved.
   * In effect.
   * Implemented.
   * Partially implemented.
   * Deferred.
   * Superseded.
   * Reversed.
   * Expired.
   * Closed.
8. Compare current records with earlier decisions to identify:

   * Confirmations.
   * Modifications.
   * Conflicts.
   * Superseding decisions.
   * Reversals.
   * Expired direction.
9. Preserve the original decision and complete change history. Do not overwrite prior records.
10. Verify implementation using documented evidence.
11. Identify actions or initiatives operating inconsistently with an active decision.
12. Identify decisions lacking implementation actions, owners, deadlines, measures, or review dates.
13. Highlight decisions requiring confirmation, documentation, follow-up, review, reconsideration, or communication.
14. Present the decision record using the structure defined in `OUTPUTS.md`.

## Decision-tracking principles

* A recommendation is not a decision.
* Discussion does not establish approval.
* Work underway does not prove that a decision was authorized.
* Decision wording should reflect the approved direction as accurately as possible.
* Conditions and limitations are part of the decision and must remain attached to it.
* Implementation status is separate from decision status.
* A decision remains active until it is completed, expired, reversed, or superseded through an authorized process.
* Historical records should remain traceable even after a decision changes.

## Guardrails

* Use only authorized and relevant source materials.
* Do not invent decisions, authority, rationale, conditions, dates, owners, actions, or implementation evidence.
* Do not infer approval from silence, attendance, informal discussion, or unaccepted recommendations.
* Do not change or overwrite the original decision record.
* Clearly distinguish facts, proposals, recommendations, assumptions, and authorized decisions.
* Cite the source and date supporting each material decision record.
* Flag conflicting, incomplete, outdated, inaccessible, or unverified information.
* Do not communicate a decision, update an external system, or direct implementation.
* Require authorized human verification before the decision register is treated as authoritative.
