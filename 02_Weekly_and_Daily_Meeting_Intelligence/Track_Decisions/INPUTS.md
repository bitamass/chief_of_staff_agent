# Inputs — Track Decisions

## Required source materials

* Approved meeting minutes and decision records.
* Meeting notes, transcripts, and summaries.
* Written approvals, resolutions, directives, and executive correspondence.
* Existing decision logs.
* Initiative charters, governance records, and approval documentation.
* Action-item, commitment, risk, and issue registers.
* Implementation plans and status reports.
* Source titles, owners, dates, versions, and locations.

## Required decision fields

For each decision, collect:

* Unique decision identifier.
* Clear decision statement.
* Decision date.
* Decision owner or authorized authority.
* Source meeting, document, or communication.
* Strategic goal, initiative, deliverable, risk, or issue affected.
* Context and reason the decision was required.
* Options considered, when documented.
* Selected option or approved direction.
* Documented rationale.
* Conditions, limitations, or assumptions.
* Stakeholders consulted, informed, or affected.
* Effective date.
* Review or expiration date, when applicable.
* Required actions and accountable owners.
* Dependencies.
* Expected outcome or success measure.
* Current decision status.
* Implementation status.
* Superseding or related decisions.
* Evidence and source reference.

## Decision-status definitions

Use consistent status values:

* **Proposed:** Direction has been suggested but not approved.
* **Pending confirmation:** The record indicates a possible decision, but authority or wording has not been verified.
* **Approved:** An authorized decision has been formally made.
* **In effect:** The decision is currently governing activity.
* **Implemented:** Required actions have been completed.
* **Partially implemented:** Some required actions remain incomplete.
* **Deferred:** The decision was formally postponed.
* **Superseded:** A later authorized decision replaced it.
* **Reversed:** Authorized leadership formally changed the direction.
* **Expired:** The decision reached a defined end or review date.
* **Closed:** Implementation and follow-through are complete.

## Decision-follow-through inputs

For each approved decision, collect:

* Actions required to implement the decision.
* Action owners and due dates.
* Required communications.
* Policy, process, system, funding, staffing, or scope changes.
* Dependencies and approvals.
* Implementation progress.
* Evidence of implementation.
* Risks or issues affecting implementation.
* Upcoming review meeting or checkpoint.
* Success measures and observed outcomes.
* Variance from the approved direction.

## Decision-change signals

Look for:

* New direction that may replace an earlier decision.
* Actions that conflict with an active decision.
* Changes in scope, owner, funding, timing, or conditions.
* Decisions reaching an expiration or review date.
* Material changes in evidence or assumptions.
* Decisions discussed as complete without implementation evidence.
* Multiple records containing inconsistent decision language.
* Decisions made verbally but not formally documented.
* Actions proceeding without a traceable decision.
* Decisions lacking owners, conditions, or follow-through.

## Optional supporting inputs

* Business cases and option analyses.
* Financial, operational, technical, clinical, security, privacy, or compliance reviews.
* Governance and delegation-of-authority documentation.
* Stakeholder communications.
* Historical decision records.
* Portfolio and initiative roadmaps.
* Benefits-realization reports.
* Audit findings.
* External mandates or deadlines.

## Input validation

Before adding or updating a decision:

* Confirm that an authorized decision was actually made.
* Verify the exact decision wording, authority, date, and source.
* Distinguish a decision from a recommendation, proposal, preference, action item, or discussion.
* Confirm whether conditions or limitations were attached.
* Verify whether a later decision superseded, reversed, or modified it.
* Confirm implementation status using current evidence.
* Identify missing owners, actions, dates, measures, or review requirements.
* Preserve the original decision and change history.
* Flag conflicting, incomplete, outdated, inaccessible, or unverified records.
* Do not infer approval from silence, meeting attendance, work underway, or an unaccepted recommendation.
