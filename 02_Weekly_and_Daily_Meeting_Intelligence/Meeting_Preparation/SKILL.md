# Meeting Preparation

## Purpose

Determine whether an executive meeting is positioned to achieve its intended outcome. Identify and organize the remaining work required across participants, decisions, materials, prior commitments, logistics, access, and contingencies.

## Instructions

1. Review the meeting information and supporting materials defined in `INPUTS.md`.
2. Confirm:

   * Meeting purpose.
   * Desired outcome.
   * Date, duration, location, and format.
   * Meeting owner and facilitator.
   * Executive’s expected role.
   * Confidentiality and access requirements.
3. Verify that the agenda supports the meeting purpose and fits within the available time.
4. Confirm required participants, presenters, subject-matter experts, and decision-makers.
5. Verify that each decision-maker has the appropriate authority.
6. Review prior meeting notes, decisions, commitments, and unresolved action items.
7. Identify decisions and material discussions expected during the meeting.
8. For each decision, assess:

   * Clarity of the decision question.
   * Available options.
   * Supporting evidence.
   * Risks and trade-offs.
   * Required stakeholders.
   * Decision authority.
   * Consequence of delay.
9. Review required pre-reading, presentations, reports, data, briefing materials, and other supporting documents.
10. Verify that each material has:

    * An owner.
    * A purpose.
    * A completion status.
    * A distribution deadline.
    * Appropriate access permissions.
11. Review meeting logistics, including:

    * Room or meeting link.
    * Technology.
    * Accessibility.
    * Travel and transition time.
    * Quorum requirements.
    * Note-taking and decision-recording method.
12. Identify preparation gaps, risks, unresolved dependencies, conflicting information, or missing approvals.
13. Create a preparation action plan with owners, deadlines, and completion evidence.
14. Recommend contingencies for missing participants, materials, evidence, or technology.
15. Assess each readiness area as:

    * Ready.
    * Partially ready.
    * Not ready.
    * Not applicable.
16. Assign an overall readiness status:

    * Ready.
    * Ready with minor gaps.
    * At risk.
    * Not ready.
17. Present the assessment using the structure defined in `OUTPUTS.md`.

## Readiness principles

* A scheduled meeting is not necessarily a prepared meeting.
* A complete agenda does not establish decision readiness.
* Decision items require clear questions, authority, options, and evidence.
* Essential participants should be identified by their required contribution, not title alone.
* Materials should be current, relevant, accessible, and distributed with enough review time.
* Prior decisions and commitments should provide continuity into the meeting.
* Material preparation gaps should be visible before the meeting, not discovered during it.

## Guardrails

* Use only approved meeting information and supporting evidence.
* Do not invent objectives, participants, authority, options, stakeholder positions, approvals, or materials.
* Do not mark a meeting ready when essential evidence, authority, participants, or logistics are unresolved.
* Clearly distinguish confirmed information, assumptions, proposed content, and recommendations.
* Flag missing, conflicting, outdated, inaccessible, confidential, or unverified information.
* Do not contact participants, distribute materials, modify calendar events, or change the agenda.
* Do not create or expand access permissions.
* Require review by the meeting owner, executive, or authorized preparation coordinator before recommendations are implemented.
