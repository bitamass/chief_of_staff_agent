# Inputs — Meeting Preparation

## Required meeting information

* Meeting title, date, start time, duration, location, and format.
* Meeting purpose and desired outcome.
* Meeting owner, facilitator, and preparation coordinator.
* Approved agenda and agenda-item owners.
* Invited participants and their roles.
* Required decision-makers, presenters, and subject-matter experts.
* Executive’s expected role in the meeting.
* Strategic goal, initiative, project, or operational responsibility supported.
* Confidentiality, access, recording, or distribution restrictions.

## Meeting-content inputs

* Prior meeting notes and summaries.
* Prior decisions and unresolved decision questions.
* Open action items and commitments.
* Current initiative, project, or portfolio status.
* Relevant milestones, deliverables, deadlines, dependencies, risks, and issues.
* Proposals, recommendations, and options expected to be discussed.
* Stakeholder positions, concerns, and prior communications.
* Supporting data, analysis, reports, and source documents.
* Changes since the previous meeting or briefing.

## Decision-preparation inputs

For each expected decision, collect:

* Decision question.
* Decision owner and approval authority.
* Available options.
* Benefits, costs, risks, and trade-offs.
* Supporting evidence.
* Stakeholders who must be consulted.
* Dependencies and prerequisite decisions.
* Decision deadline.
* Consequence of delay.
* Decision-readiness status.

## Participant context

For each key participant, collect:

* Name, title, role, and organization.
* Relationship to the meeting objective.
* Decision authority.
* Expertise or information expected.
* Known position, concern, or commitment when documented.
* Relevant prior interactions.
* Required preparation or assigned agenda item.
* Attendance status.

## Materials and logistics

* Required pre-reading.
* Executive briefing materials.
* Presentation, dashboard, report, or decision document.
* Material owner and delivery deadline.
* Distribution list and access permissions.
* Meeting link, room, technology, and accessibility requirements.
* Travel and transition time.
* Quorum or governance requirements.
* Required note-taker and decision-recording method.
* Contingency plan for missing participants, materials, or technology.

## Readiness indicators

Review whether:

* The purpose and expected outcome are clear.
* The agenda fits within the available time.
* Essential participants have confirmed attendance.
* Decision-makers have the necessary authority.
* Decision items have sufficient evidence and defined options.
* Required materials are complete, accurate, current, and accessible.
* Prior decisions, actions, and commitments are incorporated.
* Risks, issues, and disagreements are visible.
* Owners understand their preparation responsibilities.
* Logistics, access, confidentiality, and technology are confirmed.

## Optional supporting inputs

* Meeting charter or recurring-meeting terms of reference.
* Prior agendas and briefing materials.
* Executive calendar and weekly priorities.
* Stakeholder profiles.
* Decision, action, risk, and issue registers.
* Initiative charters and business cases.
* Governance and approval requirements.
* Meeting-effectiveness feedback.
* External developments relevant to the discussion.

## Input validation

Before assessing meeting readiness:

* Confirm that meeting information and source materials are current.
* Verify the purpose, desired outcome, agenda, and executive role.
* Confirm required participants, decision authority, and attendance.
* Identify missing, outdated, conflicting, or inaccessible materials.
* Identify decision items that are not ready.
* Verify that confidential information has appropriate access restrictions.
* Distinguish confirmed information from proposed content or assumptions.
* Do not infer participant positions, approvals, or attendance.
* Do not declare the meeting ready when essential participants, evidence, materials, or logistics remain unresolved.
