# Prepare Pre-Meeting Briefing Materials

## Purpose

Prepare an executive to enter a meeting with the relevant context, decisions, risks, commitments, stakeholder perspectives, and questions. Produce a concise briefing that is traceable to authorized source materials.

## Instructions

1. Review the meeting context and authorized materials defined in `INPUTS.md`.
2. Confirm:

   * Meeting purpose and desired outcome.
   * Executive’s role.
   * Decisions or contributions expected.
   * Intended briefing recipient.
   * Required format, length, and preparation deadline.
   * Confidentiality and distribution restrictions.
3. Review the agenda, prior meeting records, decisions, commitments, initiative materials, risks, issues, and relevant stakeholder communications.
4. Establish continuity by identifying:

   * What happened in prior relevant meetings.
   * Decisions already made.
   * Commitments already established.
   * Actions completed, open, blocked, at risk, or overdue.
   * Material changes since the previous discussion.
   * Upcoming milestones and deadlines.
5. Identify the decisions and alignment required in the upcoming meeting.
6. For each decision, summarize:

   * Decision question.
   * Decision owner and authority.
   * Available options.
   * Benefits, costs, risks, and trade-offs.
   * Dependencies.
   * Consequence of delay.
   * Readiness and missing information.
7. Identify material risks, issues, and dependencies. Keep these classifications distinct.
8. Summarize key participant context using documented roles, authority, positions, concerns, and prior commitments.
9. Explain why the meeting matters to the executive’s goals, responsibilities, and current priorities.
10. Develop focused executive questions that:

    * Test assumptions.
    * Clarify accountability.
    * Examine risks and trade-offs.
    * Resolve dependencies.
    * Establish decisions and next steps.
11. Recommend a meeting approach only when supported by evidence.
12. Identify areas where the executive should avoid premature commitment because evidence, authority, or stakeholder consultation is incomplete.
13. Create a preparation checklist identifying remaining materials, questions, owners, and deadlines.
14. Cite the source supporting every material fact, decision, commitment, risk, or stakeholder position.
15. Clearly separate:

    * Documented facts.
    * Assumptions.
    * Analysis.
    * Recommendations.
16. Assign a briefing-readiness status:

    * Ready.
    * Ready with limitations.
    * At risk.
    * Not ready.
17. Present the briefing using the structure defined in `OUTPUTS.md`.

## Briefing principles

* Prioritize relevance over volume.
* Connect historical context to the upcoming meeting’s decisions and outcomes.
* Include only information the executive needs to prepare effectively.
* Preserve important disagreement, uncertainty, and evidence gaps.
* Use concise summaries while retaining source traceability.
* Questions should help the executive reach clarity or a decision, not merely restate the briefing.
* Recommendations should be proportionate to the available evidence.

## Guardrails

* Use only authorized and relevant source materials.
* Do not invent facts, decisions, commitments, owners, participant positions, options, risks, or recommendations.
* Do not present assumptions or analysis as documented fact.
* Do not describe a recommendation as an approved decision.
* Do not infer stakeholder positions from titles, attendance, or silence.
* Flag conflicting, incomplete, outdated, inaccessible, or unverified information.
* Protect confidential, sensitive, clinical, security, privacy, personnel, and legally restricted information.
* Do not distribute the briefing, contact participants, or update external systems.
* Require review by the executive or authorized preparation owner before the briefing is used or shared.
