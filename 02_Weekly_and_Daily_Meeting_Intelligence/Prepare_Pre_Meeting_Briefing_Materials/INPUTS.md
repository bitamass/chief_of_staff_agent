# Inputs — Prepare Pre-Meeting Briefing Materials

## Required meeting context

* Meeting title, date, time, duration, location, and format.
* Meeting purpose and desired outcome.
* Approved agenda.
* Executive’s role in the meeting.
* Meeting owner and facilitator.
* Participants, titles, organizations, and roles.
* Required decision-makers and subject-matter experts.
* Strategic goal, initiative, project, or operational responsibility supported.
* Confidentiality, access, and distribution restrictions.

## Executive briefing requirements

* Intended executive or briefing recipient.
* Executive priorities relevant to the meeting.
* Decisions, approvals, recommendations, or contributions expected from the executive.
* Preferred briefing length, format, and level of detail.
* Specific questions or concerns raised by the executive.
* Known areas requiring additional explanation.
* Required preparation deadline.
* Authorized sources and permitted data classifications.

## Source materials

* Current meeting agenda.
* Prior meeting notes, summaries, and transcripts.
* Prior decisions and decision logs.
* Open action items, commitments, and follow-ups.
* Initiative charters, business cases, and status reports.
* Relevant milestones, deliverables, deadlines, dependencies, risks, and issues.
* Proposals, recommendations, options, and trade-off analyses.
* Financial, operational, technical, clinical, security, privacy, compliance, or workforce analyses.
* Stakeholder correspondence and documented positions.
* Relevant policies, governance requirements, and approval pathways.
* Approved external intelligence, when authorized.
* Source dates, owners, and document versions.

## Decision-briefing inputs

For each expected decision, collect:

* Decision question.
* Decision owner and approval authority.
* Decision deadline.
* Available options.
* Benefits, costs, risks, and trade-offs.
* Stakeholders affected.
* Dependencies and prerequisite decisions.
* Supporting evidence.
* Missing information.
* Consequence of delay or no decision.
* Decision-readiness status.

## Participant and stakeholder context

For each key participant, collect:

* Name, title, organization, and meeting role.
* Decision authority.
* Area of expertise or responsibility.
* Documented position, concern, or recommendation.
* Prior commitments or relevant decisions.
* Relationship to the executive or initiative.
* Expected contribution during the meeting.

## Continuity inputs

* What occurred in relevant prior meetings.
* Decisions already made.
* Commitments already established.
* Actions completed, open, blocked, at risk, or overdue.
* Changes since the previous meeting.
* Upcoming milestones, meetings, and deadlines affected by the current discussion.
* Conflicts between current proposals and prior decisions.
* Matters requiring confirmation, reconsideration, or escalation.

## Optional supporting inputs

* Executive calendar context.
* Stakeholder profiles.
* Risk and issue registers.
* Portfolio or initiative roadmaps.
* Resource and capacity plans.
* Historical briefings.
* Meeting-effectiveness feedback.
* Relevant external developments.
* Frequently asked executive questions.
* Approved briefing templates or style standards.

## Input validation

Before preparing the briefing materials:

* Confirm that all source materials are current, authorized, and relevant.
* Verify document owner, version, and effective date when available.
* Confirm the executive’s role and expected decisions.
* Distinguish documented facts from assumptions, analysis, and recommendations.
* Identify conflicting information across sources.
* Identify missing evidence, options, stakeholders, or decision authority.
* Confirm that sensitive information is appropriate for the intended recipient and distribution.
* Exclude unsupported claims and unauthorized information.
* Do not infer participant positions, decisions, approvals, or commitments.
* Preserve source traceability for material statements.
