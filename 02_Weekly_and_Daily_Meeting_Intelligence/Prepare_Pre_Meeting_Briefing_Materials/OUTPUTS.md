# Outputs — Prepare Pre-Meeting Briefing Materials

## Executive meeting brief

### Meeting overview

Provide:

* Meeting title.
* Date, time, duration, location, and format.
* Meeting purpose.
* Desired outcome.
* Executive’s expected role.
* Meeting owner and facilitator.
* Key participants and roles.
* Related strategic goal, initiative, project, or responsibility.

## Why this meeting matters

Explain concisely:

* Why the meeting requires executive attention.
* What is at stake.
* Which priorities, commitments, or stakeholders are affected.
* What outcome should be achieved.
* What could happen if the meeting does not produce the required result.

## Executive summary

Provide a concise summary of:

* Current situation.
* Material developments since the last relevant meeting.
* Decisions or alignment required.
* Most significant risks, issues, and dependencies.
* Prior commitments requiring attention.
* Recommended executive focus.
* Information gaps or limitations.

## What led to this meeting

Summarize the relevant continuity:

* Prior meetings and discussions.
* Decisions already made.
* Commitments already established.
* Completed, open, blocked, at-risk, or overdue actions.
* Changes in scope, evidence, assumptions, risks, or stakeholder positions.
* Upcoming milestones or deadlines affected by this meeting.

Include source references for material statements.

## Decisions and alignment required

For each decision or alignment item, provide:

* Clear decision question.
* Decision owner and approval authority.
* Decision deadline.
* Available options.
* Benefits, costs, risks, and trade-offs.
* Dependencies.
* Stakeholders affected.
* Consequence of delay or no decision.
* Decision-readiness status: ready, partially ready, or not ready.
* Missing evidence or consultation.
* Source references.

Do not present a recommendation as an approved decision.

## Key risks, issues, and dependencies

For each material item, provide:

* Description.
* Classification: risk, issue, or dependency.
* Affected initiative, decision, or deliverable.
* Likelihood and impact, when supported.
* Owner.
* Existing mitigation or resolution activity.
* Executive attention required.
* Source reference.

## Prior decisions and commitments

Provide:

* Prior decision or commitment.
* Source and date.
* Owner.
* Current status.
* Related action or deliverable.
* Relevance to the current meeting.
* Any conflict with the current proposal or evidence.

## Participant and stakeholder context

For each key participant, provide:

* Name, title, organization, and meeting role.
* Decision authority or expertise.
* Documented position, concern, or recommendation.
* Prior commitment or relevant interaction.
* Expected contribution during the meeting.

Include only documented and authorized information.

## Recommended executive questions

Provide focused questions that help the executive:

* Test assumptions.
* Clarify ownership and accountability.
* Examine risks and trade-offs.
* Confirm decision authority.
* Resolve dependencies.
* Evaluate resource requirements.
* Determine next steps and success measures.

Connect each question to the relevant evidence, gap, decision, or risk.

## Recommended meeting approach

When supported by the evidence, recommend:

* Topics to emphasize.
* Decisions to seek.
* Questions to ask.
* Stakeholder concerns to acknowledge.
* Areas where the executive should avoid premature commitment.
* Matters to defer pending additional evidence.
* Desired closing summary and next steps.

## Preparation checklist

List:

* Materials the executive should review.
* Questions requiring answers before the meeting.
* Data or analysis still needed.
* Participant follow-up required.
* Preparation owner.
* Completion deadline.
* Consequence if incomplete.

## Sources and traceability

List each source used, including:

* Document title.
* Source owner.
* Date or version.
* Sections or findings used.
* Any access, quality, or confidence limitations.

Clearly distinguish:

* Documented facts.
* Assumptions.
* Analysis.
* Recommendations.

## Briefing readiness status

Classify the briefing as:

* **Ready:** Material information is current, supported, and sufficient.
* **Ready with limitations:** The brief can be used, but identified evidence gaps remain.
* **At risk:** Missing or conflicting information may materially affect executive preparation.
* **Not ready:** Essential meeting context, evidence, decisions, or source materials are unavailable.

## Human-review requirement

The executive or authorized preparation owner must review the briefing for accuracy, relevance, sensitivity, and source support before it is distributed or used for the meeting.
