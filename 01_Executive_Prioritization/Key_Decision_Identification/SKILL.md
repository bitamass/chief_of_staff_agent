# Key Decision Identification

## Purpose

Identify decisions that require executive attention and distinguish them from informational updates, discussion topics, and action items. Prepare each decision for leadership review without making or approving the decision.

## Instructions

1. Review the materials defined in `INPUTS.md`.
2. Examine meeting agendas, notes, initiatives, commitments, risks, issues, proposals, and upcoming milestones for decision signals.
3. Identify situations involving:

   * A request for approval, authorization, funding, or direction.
   * Multiple options without a selected direction.
   * Work blocked by unresolved leadership guidance.
   * A deadline or milestone dependent on a decision.
   * Conflicting stakeholder recommendations.
   * Unresolved ownership, scope, resources, timing, or risk acceptance.
   * Direction that appears to have been given but was not formally documented.
4. Determine whether each item is:

   * A decision.
   * A discussion topic.
   * An informational update.
   * An action item.
   * An issue requiring resolution.
5. For each identified decision, define:

   * The decision question.
   * Why the decision is needed.
   * The affected goal, initiative, deliverable, risk, or issue.
   * The authorized decision owner.
   * Required contributors and affected stakeholders.
   * Decision deadline.
   * Consequence of delay or no decision.
6. Determine whether the decision has already been made, deferred, superseded, or requires reconsideration.
7. Identify available options, benefits, costs, risks, trade-offs, dependencies, and reversibility when supported by the inputs.
8. Assess decision readiness:

   * **Ready:** Authority, options, evidence, and required consultation are available.
   * **Partially ready:** A decision is possible, but material information is incomplete.
   * **Not ready:** Essential authority, evidence, options, or consultation is missing.
9. Classify the decision status:

   * Emerging.
   * In development.
   * Ready for decision.
   * Blocked.
   * Deferred.
   * Escalated.
   * Decided.
   * Requires reconsideration.
10. Prioritize decisions according to urgency, strategic impact, dependencies, consequence of delay, and readiness.
11. Place each decision into an executive agenda category:

    * Decide now.
    * Prepare for decision.
    * Monitor.
    * Confirm or document.
    * Reconsider.
12. For decisions that are not ready, identify the evidence, analysis, consultation, or dependency resolution required.
13. Present the findings using the structure defined in `OUTPUTS.md`.

## Decision-identification principles

* A question raised in a meeting is not automatically a decision.
* A recommendation is not an approved decision.
* An action item is not a decision unless leadership must select or authorize a direction.
* Stakeholder preference does not establish decision authority.
* Decisions should be written as clear questions that leadership can answer.
* Urgent decisions should not be labeled ready when essential evidence is missing.
* Previously made decisions should be reconsidered only when material conditions or evidence have changed.

## Guardrails

* Use only the evidence supplied in the approved inputs.
* Do not invent decisions, options, owners, authority, approvals, deadlines, or stakeholder positions.
* Do not treat discussion, silence, or implied direction as formal approval.
* Do not recommend an option without explaining the supporting evidence and trade-offs.
* Clearly distinguish documented facts, assumptions, analysis, and recommendations.
* Cite the source and date supporting each decision when available.
* Flag incomplete, conflicting, outdated, or unverified information.
* Do not make, approve, communicate, or record a leadership decision.
* Require authorized human confirmation before an identified decision is treated as final.
