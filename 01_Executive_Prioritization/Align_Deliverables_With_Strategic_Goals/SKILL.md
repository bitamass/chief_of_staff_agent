# Align Deliverables With Strategic Goals

## Purpose

Determine how strongly each initiative deliverable supports approved strategic goals and business priorities. Produce an evidence-based, prioritized recommendation for leadership review.

## Instructions

1. Review the approved strategic goals and top business priorities.
2. Identify the initiatives associated with each goal.
3. Review the deliverables, owners, due dates, expected outcomes, dependencies, risks, and constraints for each initiative.
4. Map every deliverable to one or more strategic goals.
5. Flag deliverables that do not have a clear connection to an approved goal or priority.
6. Evaluate each deliverable using the following criteria:

   * Strategic-goal alignment
   * Contribution to business priorities
   * Expected organizational value
   * Urgency or consequence of delay
   * Dependency or prerequisite importance
   * Feasibility within available capacity
7. Assign a score from 1 to 5 for each criterion:

   * **1 — No demonstrated alignment or value**
   * **2 — Weak or indirect alignment**
   * **3 — Moderate or supporting alignment**
   * **4 — Strong and direct alignment**
   * **5 — Critical to achieving the goal**
8. Explain every score using evidence from the provided materials.
9. Calculate an overall alignment score using the approved scoring weights. If weights are not provided, present the individual scores and clearly state that no weighted score was calculated.
10. Rank the deliverables from highest to lowest strategic relevance.
11. Identify gaps, duplication, conflicts, unsupported work, capacity concerns, and missing information.
12. Recommend one disposition for each deliverable:

    * Keep
    * Accelerate
    * Revise
    * Defer
    * Combine
    * Remove
    * Escalate for leadership decision

## Output requirements

Follow the structure defined in `OUTPUTS.md`. The output must include:

* Executive summary
* Strategic-goal evaluation
* Deliverable alignment matrix
* Prioritized deliverable list
* Strategic coverage gaps
* Recommended disposition for each deliverable
* Leadership decisions required
* Missing information and confidence limitations

## Guardrails

* Use only approved goals, priorities, initiatives, deliverables, and supporting evidence provided in the inputs.
* Do not invent goals, owners, approvals, dates, dependencies, benefits, or scoring evidence.
* Clearly distinguish documented facts, assumptions, analysis, and recommendations.
* Identify the source and effective date of material evidence when available.
* Mark incomplete or conflicting information instead of resolving it without evidence.
* Do not automatically cancel deliverables, change priorities, reassign resources, or modify commitments.
* Require authorized human review before recommendations are used to change priorities, funding, ownership, resources, or timelines.
