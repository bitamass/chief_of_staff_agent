# Inputs — Manage Competing Priorities

## Required inputs

* Approved strategic goals and current executive priorities.
* Complete list of active initiatives, programs, projects, and operational commitments.
* Priority level, rationale, executive sponsor, and accountable owner for each priority.
* Expected outcomes, benefits, and success measures.
* Key deliverables, milestones, deadlines, and time-sensitive commitments.
* Available and required budget, staffing, technology capacity, and specialized expertise.
* Resource assignments and current team capacity.
* Dependencies among priorities, initiatives, decisions, and deliverables.
* Known risks, issues, constraints, and consequences of delay.
* Regulatory, clinical, operational, security, privacy, or contractual requirements.
* Existing prioritization, funding, sequencing, and resource-allocation decisions.
* Upcoming meetings or decision forums where conflicts may be resolved.

## Priority-conflict signals

Look for:

* Multiple priorities requiring the same people, funding, systems, or leadership attention.
* Deadlines that overlap or cannot all be met.
* New urgent work displacing previously approved commitments.
* Mandatory work competing with discretionary initiatives.
* Conflicting executive or stakeholder direction.
* Initiatives dependent on the same unresolved decision or deliverable.
* Teams assigned beyond available capacity.
* Priorities with unclear owners or competing ownership.
* Short-term operational needs conflicting with long-term strategic goals.
* Local or departmental priorities conflicting with enterprise priorities.
* Work continuing despite changed assumptions or reduced value.
* Commitments that cannot be met without changing scope, timing, or resources.

## Required comparison factors

For each competing priority, collect:

* Strategic importance.
* Expected organizational value.
* Urgency and deadline.
* Consequence of delay.
* Mandatory or discretionary classification.
* Resource requirements.
* Current progress and cost already incurred.
* Dependencies and sequencing requirements.
* Risk of proceeding.
* Risk of delaying or stopping.
* Reversibility.
* Stakeholders affected.
* Evidence and confidence level.

## Optional supporting inputs

* Portfolio roadmaps.
* Resource and capacity plans.
* Financial forecasts and budget constraints.
* Initiative charters and business cases.
* Workforce allocation reports.
* Risk and issue registers.
* Operational performance reports.
* Stakeholder feedback and executive correspondence.
* Prior leadership decisions.
* Change-management capacity assessments.
* External mandates, market developments, or institutional deadlines.

## Input validation

Before assessing competing priorities:

* Confirm that priorities and strategic goals are current and approved.
* Verify the owner, sponsor, deadline, and expected outcome of each priority.
* Confirm that resource and capacity information covers the same planning period.
* Distinguish mandatory commitments from discretionary work.
* Identify missing cost, benefit, resource, dependency, or consequence information.
* Identify priorities that are duplicates, components of the same initiative, or not actually in conflict.
* Flag conflicting leadership direction or outdated prioritization decisions.
* Do not assume that the newest or most urgent request is automatically the highest priority.
* Do not treat missing information as evidence that a priority has low value.
