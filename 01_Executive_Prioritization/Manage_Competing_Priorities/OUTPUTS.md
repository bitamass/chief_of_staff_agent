# Outputs — Manage Competing Priorities

## Executive priority summary

Provide a concise overview of:

* The most significant priority conflicts.
* Resources, deadlines, or dependencies creating the conflicts.
* Commitments most at risk.
* Conflicts requiring immediate leadership direction.
* Recommended sequencing, scope, timing, or resource adjustments.

## Competing-priorities register

For each conflict, provide:

* Conflict title.
* Priorities, initiatives, or commitments involved.
* Strategic goals affected.
* Executive sponsors and accountable owners.
* Source of conflict:

  * Funding.
  * Staffing.
  * Specialized expertise.
  * Technology capacity.
  * Leadership attention.
  * Deadline.
  * Dependency.
  * Scope.
  * Conflicting direction.
* Mandatory or discretionary classification.
* Required decision date.
* Consequence if the conflict is not resolved.
* Evidence and source.
* Confidence level: high, medium, or low.

## Priority comparison

For each competing priority, provide:

* Strategic importance.
* Expected organizational value.
* Urgency and deadline.
* Consequence of delay.
* Resource requirements.
* Current progress.
* Dependencies.
* Risk of proceeding.
* Risk of delaying, reducing, or stopping.
* Reversibility.
* Stakeholders affected.
* Existing commitments or approvals.

Use approved criteria and scoring methods when available. Do not invent weights or scoring rules.

## Capacity and resource conflicts

Identify:

* People or teams assigned beyond available capacity.
* Competing demands for the same funding.
* Shared technology or integration constraints.
* Shortages of specialized expertise.
* Excessive demand on executive attention.
* Change-management capacity limitations.
* Commitments that cannot all be delivered within the same period.

## Recommended resolution options

When supported by the inputs, present options such as:

* Sequence the priorities.
* Reallocate resources.
* Adjust scope.
* Extend a deadline.
* Pause or defer lower-value work.
* Combine overlapping work.
* Resolve a shared dependency.
* Add capacity.
* Escalate for leadership direction.

For each option, provide:

* Priorities affected.
* Benefits.
* Costs and trade-offs.
* Risks.
* Dependencies.
* Consequences for current commitments.
* Reversibility.
* Evidence and assumptions.

## Recommended priority plan

Provide a proposed plan showing:

* Work to continue as planned.
* Work to accelerate.
* Work to sequence.
* Work to revise or reduce in scope.
* Work to defer or pause.
* Work requiring executive escalation.
* Recommended owners and review dates.

Present the plan as a recommendation, not an approved change.

## Leadership decisions required

For each required decision, provide:

* Decision question.
* Priorities affected.
* Available options.
* Recommended option, when supported by evidence.
* Trade-offs and consequences.
* Decision owner.
* Decision deadline.
* Consequence of delay.
* Additional evidence required.

## Information gaps and confidence

Identify:

* Missing or outdated capacity information.
* Unverified benefit or urgency claims.
* Unclear ownership or deadlines.
* Undocumented commitments.
* Conflicting stakeholder direction.
* Missing consequences of delay.
* Priorities that cannot be compared fairly.
* Confidence level for each major recommendation.

## Human-review requirement

Authorized leadership must review the analysis before priorities, commitments, funding, staffing, ownership, scope, or timelines are changed. The skill recommends ways to resolve conflicts; it does not modify or approve priorities.
