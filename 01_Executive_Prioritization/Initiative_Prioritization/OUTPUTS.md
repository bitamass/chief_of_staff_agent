# Outputs — Initiative Prioritization

## Executive portfolio summary

Provide a concise overview of:

* The highest-priority initiatives.
* Initiatives requiring immediate leadership decisions.
* Major portfolio constraints, risks, and dependencies.
* Initiatives with insufficient evidence for prioritization.
* Recommended changes to funding, sequencing, scope, or timing.

## Initiative prioritization matrix

For each initiative, provide:

* Initiative name.
* Objective.
* Executive sponsor and accountable owner.
* Current status.
* Strategic goals supported.
* Expected organizational value.
* Estimated cost and resource requirements.
* Target timeline.
* Key dependencies.
* Major risks and constraints.
* Mandatory or discretionary classification.
* Confidence in the supporting evidence.

## Prioritization scores

Score each initiative from 1 to 5 against the approved criteria:

* Strategic alignment.
* Expected organizational value.
* Urgency or consequence of delay.
* Clinical, operational, financial, or stakeholder impact.
* Regulatory, compliance, security, or safety necessity.
* Feasibility and organizational readiness.
* Dependency or prerequisite importance.
* Confidence in expected benefits.

For each score, provide:

* Score.
* Supporting evidence.
* Source.
* Assumptions or limitations.

Calculate a weighted overall score only when approved weights are provided. If weights are unavailable, present the individual scores without creating an assumed weighting model.

## Ranked initiative portfolio

Rank initiatives from highest to lowest priority. For each initiative, recommend one disposition:

* **Accelerate:** Increase attention, resources, or pace.
* **Proceed:** Continue according to the approved plan.
* **Sequence:** Proceed after a required dependency or higher-priority initiative.
* **Revise:** Modify scope, ownership, funding, timing, or expected outcomes.
* **Defer:** Delay until defined conditions are met.
* **Combine:** Consolidate with an overlapping initiative.
* **Pause:** Temporarily stop pending evidence or leadership direction.
* **Discontinue:** Recommend ending the initiative, subject to authorized review.
* **Escalate:** Obtain an executive decision before proceeding.

Include the rationale for each recommendation.

## Portfolio balance and conflicts

Identify:

* Duplicate or overlapping initiatives.
* Competing demands for the same resources.
* Initiatives dependent on unresolved decisions or deliverables.
* Strategic goals receiving excessive or insufficient investment.
* Mandatory work displacing discretionary priorities.
* Capacity, timing, or sequencing conflicts.
* Initiatives that could be combined or coordinated.
* Portfolio-level risks affecting multiple initiatives.

## Recommended resource and sequencing actions

Provide recommendations for:

* Initiative sequencing.
* Funding allocation.
* Staffing and specialized expertise.
* Technology or integration capacity.
* Leadership attention.
* Dependency resolution.
* Scope or schedule changes.

Do not present these recommendations as approved changes.

## Leadership decisions required

For each required decision, provide:

* Decision to be made.
* Initiatives affected.
* Recommended options.
* Trade-offs and consequences.
* Decision owner.
* Decision deadline.
* Consequence of delay.
* Evidence still required.

## Information gaps and confidence

Identify:

* Missing or unreliable cost estimates.
* Unverified benefit assumptions.
* Incomplete resource requirements.
* Unclear ownership or success measures.
* Unknown dependencies or risks.
* Initiatives that cannot be fairly compared.
* Confidence level for each major recommendation: high, medium, or low.

## Human-review requirement

Authorized leadership must review the prioritization before it is used to approve, fund, accelerate, defer, combine, pause, or discontinue an initiative, or to change ownership, resources, scope, or timelines.
