# Inputs — Initiative Prioritization

## Required inputs

* Approved strategic goals and current business priorities.
* Complete list of proposed and active initiatives.
* Initiative name, objective, executive sponsor, accountable owner, and current status.
* Expected outcomes, benefits, and success measures for each initiative.
* Estimated cost, staffing requirements, technology needs, and implementation effort.
* Proposed start date, target completion date, major milestones, and time sensitivity.
* Dependencies on other initiatives, systems, vendors, teams, or leadership decisions.
* Known risks, constraints, assumptions, and regulatory or operational requirements.
* Available budget, workforce capacity, and specialized resource constraints.
* Approved prioritization criteria, scoring scale, and weighting.

## Recommended prioritization criteria

Evaluate each initiative against:

* Strategic alignment.
* Expected organizational value.
* Urgency or consequence of delay.
* Clinical, operational, financial, or stakeholder impact.
* Regulatory, compliance, security, or safety necessity.
* Feasibility and organizational readiness.
* Cost and resource requirements.
* Risk and implementation complexity.
* Dependency or prerequisite importance.
* Confidence in benefits and supporting evidence.

## Optional supporting inputs

* Initiative charters and business cases.
* Financial models and benefit estimates.
* Portfolio roadmaps.
* Resource and capacity plans.
* Architecture, security, privacy, or compliance assessments.
* Prior funding and prioritization decisions.
* Stakeholder feedback.
* Lessons learned from comparable initiatives.
* External deadlines, mandates, or market developments.
* Current initiative performance and benefit-realization reports.

## Portfolio constraints

Document any limits affecting prioritization:

* Available funding.
* Staffing and specialized expertise.
* Technology and integration capacity.
* Leadership attention.
* Change-management capacity.
* Required sequencing.
* Fixed deadlines or mandates.
* Initiatives that must proceed regardless of comparative score.

## Input validation

Before prioritizing initiatives:

* Confirm that strategic goals and priorities are current and approved.
* Confirm each initiative has a defined objective, owner, expected outcome, and status.
* Identify missing cost, benefit, resource, risk, dependency, or timing information.
* Identify duplicate, overlapping, competing, or interdependent initiatives.
* Distinguish mandatory initiatives from discretionary initiatives.
* Flag estimates that are outdated, unsupported, or based on unverified assumptions.
* Do not treat missing information as a low score; identify it as an evidence gap.
* Do not compare initiatives using inconsistent criteria or scoring scales.
