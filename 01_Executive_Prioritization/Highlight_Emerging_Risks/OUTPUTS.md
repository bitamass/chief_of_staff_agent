# Outputs — Highlight Emerging Risks

## Executive risk summary

Provide a concise summary of:

* The most significant emerging risks.
* Risks that have increased in likelihood or impact.
* Issues requiring immediate leadership attention.
* Cross-initiative risks or patterns.
* Decisions or actions needed to reduce exposure.

## Emerging risk register

For each emerging risk, provide:

* Risk title.
* Risk description.
* Affected initiative, deliverable, or strategic goal.
* Risk category: operational, financial, schedule, workforce, technology, security, privacy, compliance, clinical, reputational, vendor, or dependency.
* Status: new, increasing, unchanged, decreasing, or realized issue.
* Likelihood: low, medium, or high.
* Impact: low, medium, or high.
* Overall risk level: low, moderate, high, or critical.
* Time horizon: immediate, near term, or longer term.
* Evidence and source.
* Risk owner, if assigned.
* Existing mitigation.
* Remaining exposure.
* Next review date.

## Leading indicators and warning signals

Identify the evidence suggesting that each risk is emerging or escalating, including:

* Repeated delays or missed milestones.
* Overdue or repeatedly deferred actions.
* Unresolved dependencies.
* Missing owners or mitigation plans.
* Budget, staffing, or capacity constraints.
* Changes in assumptions, requirements, or scope.
* Increasing incident frequency or severity.
* Stakeholder disagreement or delayed decisions.
* New security, privacy, regulatory, clinical, or operational concerns.

## Risk trends and relationships

Highlight:

* Risks affecting more than one initiative.
* Risks caused by a shared dependency.
* Risks that could combine or create cascading effects.
* Existing risks whose likelihood or impact has increased.
* Issues that may create additional risks if unresolved.
* Risks associated with upcoming meetings, decisions, or deadlines.

## Recommended leadership attention

For each significant risk, recommend one response:

* Monitor.
* Assign an owner.
* Develop or revise mitigation.
* Resolve a dependency.
* Request additional evidence.
* Escalate for leadership review.
* Accept the risk.
* Avoid or discontinue the affected activity.

Include the reason for the recommendation and the recommended completion or review date.

## Decisions and actions required

List:

* Leadership decisions needed.
* Recommended action.
* Accountable owner.
* Target date.
* Related initiative or deliverable.
* Consequence of delay.
* Recommended escalation level.

## Information gaps and confidence

Identify:

* Missing or outdated evidence.
* Unconfirmed assumptions.
* Risks without owners or mitigation plans.
* Risks that cannot be scored reliably.
* Confidence level for each major finding: high, medium, or low.

## Human-review requirement

An authorized human must review the risk assessment before risks are escalated, accepted, communicated externally, or used to change priorities, funding, staffing, ownership, or timelines.
