# Highlight Emerging Risks

## Purpose

Identify emerging risks early by reviewing initiative activity, decisions, commitments, dependencies, milestones, and recent changes. Help leadership understand what may happen, why it matters, and where intervention may be required.

## Instructions

1. Review the materials defined in `INPUTS.md`.
2. Identify warning signals, including:

   * Missed or repeatedly delayed milestones.
   * Overdue actions or unresolved commitments.
   * Decisions that are blocked or repeatedly deferred.
   * Unconfirmed dependencies.
   * Missing owners or mitigation plans.
   * Budget, staffing, or capacity constraints.
   * Changes in scope, assumptions, requirements, or expected outcomes.
   * New security, privacy, compliance, clinical, operational, financial, workforce, vendor, or reputational concerns.
3. Distinguish among:

   * **Emerging risk:** A newly identified uncertain event that may affect an objective.
   * **Increasing risk:** A known risk whose likelihood or impact is growing.
   * **Realized issue:** An event that has already occurred and requires resolution.
   * **Watch item:** A warning signal that does not yet have enough evidence to classify as a risk.
4. Connect each risk to the affected initiative, deliverable, strategic goal, meeting, decision, or dependency.
5. Determine whether risks share a common cause, affect multiple initiatives, or could create cascading effects.
6. Assess each risk using:

   * Likelihood: low, medium, or high.
   * Impact: low, medium, or high.
   * Time horizon: immediate, near term, or longer term.
   * Direction: new, increasing, unchanged, or decreasing.
   * Confidence: low, medium, or high.
7. Explain every assessment using evidence from the supplied materials.
8. Identify the existing owner, mitigation, and next review date when available.
9. Highlight missing ownership, incomplete mitigation, conflicting evidence, or inadequate monitoring.
10. Recommend an appropriate response:

    * Monitor.
    * Assign an owner.
    * Develop or revise mitigation.
    * Resolve a dependency.
    * Request additional evidence.
    * Escalate for leadership review.
    * Accept the risk.
    * Avoid or discontinue the affected activity.
11. Identify the leadership decisions and actions required, including owner, target date, and consequence of delay.
12. Present the findings using the structure defined in `OUTPUTS.md`.

## Prioritization guidance

Prioritize a risk when one or more of the following applies:

* It could materially affect a strategic goal or critical deliverable.
* Its likelihood or impact is increasing.
* It may affect multiple initiatives.
* It has no accountable owner or mitigation plan.
* It is approaching a decision, milestone, or compliance deadline.
* Delayed action would reduce leadership’s available response options.
* It involves significant clinical, security, privacy, financial, regulatory, or reputational consequences.

## Guardrails

* Use only evidence provided in the approved inputs.
* Do not describe a warning signal as a confirmed risk without sufficient evidence.
* Do not describe a risk as an issue unless the event has already occurred.
* Do not invent owners, impacts, likelihood ratings, deadlines, or mitigation plans.
* Clearly distinguish facts, assumptions, analysis, and recommendations.
* Cite the source and date supporting each significant finding when available.
* State when evidence is incomplete, conflicting, outdated, or unverified.
* Do not automatically escalate, accept, close, or communicate a risk.
* Require authorized human review before findings affect priorities, funding, staffing, commitments, or external communications.
