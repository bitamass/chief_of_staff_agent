# Inputs — Highlight Emerging Risks

## Required inputs

* Active initiatives, programs, projects, and strategic commitments.
* Current status reports, including milestones, deliverables, owners, due dates, and completion status.
* Existing risk and issue registers, including risk owner, severity, likelihood, impact, mitigation, and review date.
* Recent meeting notes, decisions, action items, and unresolved follow-ups.
* Changes to scope, schedule, budget, staffing, dependencies, technology, policy, or regulatory requirements.
* Missed deadlines, delayed decisions, incomplete deliverables, and overdue actions.
* Known operational, financial, security, privacy, compliance, clinical, reputational, and workforce concerns.
* Executive priorities and defined risk-tolerance or escalation thresholds.

## Risk indicators to review

* Repeated delays or missed milestones.
* Actions that remain open across multiple meetings.
* Decisions that are blocked or repeatedly deferred.
* Deliverables without an accountable owner.
* Dependencies without confirmed commitments.
* Budget, staffing, or capacity constraints.
* Conflicting priorities or resource demands.
* Changes in assumptions, requirements, or expected outcomes.
* Increasing issue frequency, severity, or duration.
* New security, privacy, regulatory, clinical, or operational concerns.
* Stakeholder disagreement or declining engagement.
* Risks that could affect multiple initiatives or strategic goals.

## Optional supporting inputs

* Financial forecasts and budget variance reports.
* Resource and capacity plans.
* Dependency maps.
* Audit, compliance, privacy, and security assessments.
* Incident reports and operational performance measures.
* Stakeholder feedback and executive correspondence.
* Vendor performance reports.
* External developments that could affect the initiative.
* Historical risk trends and lessons learned.

## Input validation

Before evaluating emerging risks:

* Confirm that source materials are current and dated.
* Identify the owner and source of each reported concern.
* Distinguish a risk—an event that may occur—from an issue that has already occurred.
* Separate new risks from existing risks whose likelihood or impact has changed.
* Identify missing owners, mitigation plans, review dates, or escalation thresholds.
* Flag conflicting, incomplete, outdated, or unverified information.
* Do not infer that a risk is confirmed when the evidence only indicates a possible warning signal.
