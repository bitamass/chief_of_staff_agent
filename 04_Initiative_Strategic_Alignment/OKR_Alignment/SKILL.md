# OKR Alignment

## Purpose

Determine whether OKRs translate strategic priorities into clear, measurable outcomes across organizational levels. Identify weak, duplicate, conflicting, unsupported, or activity-focused OKRs and recommend corrections for owner review.

## Instructions

1. Review the strategy, OKRs, initiatives, and supporting materials defined in `INPUTS.md`.
2. Confirm:

   * Strategic priorities are current and approved.
   * OKRs belong to the correct planning period.
   * Owners, baselines, targets, dates, and data sources are documented.
3. Distinguish among:

   * Strategic priority.
   * Objective.
   * Key result.
   * Initiative.
   * Deliverable.
   * Activity or task.
4. Evaluate each objective for:

   * Strategic relevance.
   * Clarity.
   * Outcome orientation.
   * Appropriate scope.
   * Time-bound focus.
   * Clear ownership.
5. Evaluate each key result for:

   * Specificity.
   * Measurability.
   * Baseline and target.
   * Outcome orientation.
   * Time boundary.
   * Reliable data source.
   * Clear ownership.
   * Ability to demonstrate progress toward its objective.
6. Classify each key result as:

   * Outcome.
   * Output.
   * Activity.
   * Milestone.
7. Assess vertical alignment across:

   * Strategic priorities.
   * Enterprise objectives.
   * Division or departmental objectives.
   * Team objectives.
   * Initiative objectives.
   * Key results.
8. Require each child objective or key result to define a distinct, evidence-supported contribution rather than repeat parent wording.
9. Assess horizontal alignment across teams by identifying:

   * Shared objectives.
   * Duplicate OKRs.
   * Conflicting targets or timelines.
   * Shared dependencies.
   * Competing resources.
   * Missing coordination or ownership.
10. Map initiatives and work to the OKRs they support using documented outcomes and measures.
11. Identify:

    * Strategic priorities without OKR coverage.
    * Objectives without measurable key results.
    * Key results without reliable measures.
    * Key results without supporting work.
    * Initiatives without demonstrated OKR alignment.
    * Operational obligations that should be tracked outside the OKR framework.
12. Evaluate progress using key-result evidence rather than activity completion.
13. Recommend clarification, revised wording, measurement improvements, ownership, coordination, or retirement of unsupported OKRs.
14. Label all proposed OKR wording as a draft requiring owner approval.
15. Present the analysis using the structure defined in `OUTPUTS.md`.

## OKR-alignment principles

* Objectives describe desired outcomes; key results provide measurable evidence of achievement.
* Activities and deliverables may support a key result but are not automatically key results.
* Similar wording does not establish alignment.
* Cascaded OKRs should define distinct contributions rather than repeat higher-level language.
* Progress should be based on outcome measures, not volume of activity.
* Not every operational responsibility or mandatory obligation needs an OKR.
* Shared key results require clear ownership, contribution expectations, and common measurement.
* Missing evidence should reduce confidence, not be interpreted as success or failure.

## Guardrails

* Use only approved strategy, OKRs, measures, and authorized supporting evidence.
* Do not invent objectives, key results, mappings, owners, baselines, targets, progress, data, or approvals.
* Do not change scoring methods or targets without authorization.
* Do not infer alignment from organizational structure, titles, or similar wording.
* Clearly distinguish documented facts, assumptions, analysis, and proposed revisions.
* Cite sources and reporting dates for material findings.
* Flag incomplete, conflicting, outdated, unreliable, or unverified information.
* Do not update OKR systems or performance records.
* Require authorized strategy and OKR-owner review before mappings, wording, targets, ownership, or status are changed.
