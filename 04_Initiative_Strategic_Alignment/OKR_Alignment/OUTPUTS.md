# Outputs — OKR Alignment

## Executive OKR summary

Provide a concise overview of:

* Overall alignment between strategic priorities and OKRs.
* Objectives with strong, partial, unclear, or no demonstrated alignment.
* Key results that effectively measure outcomes.
* Key results that measure activities or outputs rather than outcomes.
* Duplicate, conflicting, or unsupported OKRs.
* Strategic priorities without adequate OKR coverage.
* Cross-functional dependencies requiring leadership attention.
* Recommended corrections or clarifications.

## OKR inventory

For each objective, provide:

* Objective identifier and statement.
* Organizational level.
* Owner.
* Planning period.
* Strategic priority supported.
* Parent objective, if applicable.
* Related initiatives.
* Current status.
* Alignment classification:

  * Strong.
  * Partial.
  * Indirect.
  * Unclear.
  * No demonstrated alignment.
* Evidence and source.
* Confidence level: high, medium, or low.

## Objective quality assessment

Evaluate each objective for:

* Strategic relevance.
* Clarity.
* Outcome orientation.
* Appropriate scope.
* Time-bound focus.
* Clear ownership.

Classify the objective as:

* Well defined.
* Needs clarification.
* Too broad.
* Too narrow.
* Activity focused.
* Duplicative.
* Unsupported.

Explain the rationale.

## Key-result quality assessment

For each key result, provide:

* Key-result statement.
* Related objective.
* Owner.
* Metric.
* Baseline.
* Target.
* Current value.
* Measurement period.
* Data source.
* Measurement frequency.
* Classification:

  * Outcome.
  * Output.
  * Activity.
  * Milestone.
* Quality assessment.
* Evidence limitations.
* Confidence level.

Identify missing baselines, targets, dates, owners, or reliable data sources.

## Vertical alignment

Show the relationship among:

* Strategic priority.
* Enterprise objective.
* Division or departmental objective.
* Team objective.
* Initiative objective.
* Key result.
* Supporting work.

For each relationship, explain:

* Expected contribution.
* Shared or related measure.
* Dependency.
* Evidence.
* Strength of alignment.

Identify cascaded OKRs that merely repeat parent wording without defining a distinct contribution.

## Horizontal alignment

Identify:

* Teams supporting the same objective or key result.
* Shared dependencies.
* Duplicate or overlapping OKRs.
* Conflicting targets or timelines.
* Competing resource requirements.
* Missing coordination or shared ownership.
* Measures requiring a common definition or data source.

## Strategic coverage gaps

Identify:

* Strategic priorities without supporting objectives.
* Objectives without measurable key results.
* Key results without supporting initiatives or work.
* Initiatives without a demonstrated connection to an OKR.
* Operational obligations incorrectly excluded because they do not fit the OKR framework.
* Objectives dependent on one high-risk initiative or data source.

## Recommended OKR revisions

For each concern, recommend one or more actions:

* Clarify the objective.
* Rewrite the key result as an outcome.
* Add a baseline or target.
* Confirm the metric or data source.
* Assign an owner.
* Narrow or expand scope.
* Remove duplication.
* Resolve conflicting targets.
* Establish cross-team ownership.
* Add a missing key result.
* Retire an unsupported OKR.
* Escalate for leadership direction.

When recommending revised wording, label it as a draft requiring owner approval.

## Progress and confidence assessment

For each objective, provide:

* Current progress.
* Supporting key-result performance.
* Forecasted likelihood of achievement, when supported.
* Major dependencies.
* Risks and blockers.
* Data quality.
* Confidence level.
* Next review date.

Do not infer progress from activity completion when outcome measures are unavailable.

## Leadership decisions required

For each required decision, provide:

* Decision question.
* Objectives or key results affected.
* Available options.
* Trade-offs.
* Decision owner.
* Decision deadline.
* Consequence of delay.
* Evidence still required.

## Human-review requirement

Authorized strategy and OKR owners must review proposed mappings, quality assessments, revised wording, targets, ownership, and recommendations before OKRs or performance records are changed.
