# Inputs — OKR Alignment

## Required strategic context

* Approved mission, vision, and strategic priorities.
* Current planning period.
* Organization-level objectives and key results.
* Executive owners.
* Strategic targets and time horizons.
* Mandatory institutional, clinical, regulatory, operational, or financial commitments.
* Approved OKR framework, terminology, and scoring method.
* Prior-period OKRs and final results.

## Required objective information

For each objective, collect:

* Objective identifier.
* Objective statement.
* Organizational level:

  * Enterprise.
  * Division.
  * Department.
  * Team.
  * Initiative.
  * Individual, when authorized.
* Objective owner.
* Parent objective, if applicable.
* Strategic priority supported.
* Planning period.
* Intended outcome.
* Rationale.
* Stakeholders affected.
* Current status.
* Dependencies.
* Source, version, and approval date.

## Required key-result information

For each key result, collect:

* Key-result identifier and statement.
* Related objective.
* Accountable owner.
* Metric.
* Baseline.
* Target.
* Current value.
* Unit of measurement.
* Data source.
* Measurement frequency.
* Start and end dates.
* Current status.
* Confidence in measurement.
* Dependencies.
* Risks and constraints.
* Whether the key result measures:

  * Outcome.
  * Output.
  * Activity.
  * Milestone.
* Source and last-updated date.

## Initiative and work alignment

For each initiative, project, or major workstream, collect:

* Name and owner.
* Objective and intended outcome.
* OKRs supported.
* Expected contribution to each key result.
* Deliverables and milestones.
* Resources required.
* Dependencies.
* Risks.
* Contribution measure.
* Time horizon.
* Evidence supporting the connection.

## Vertical alignment inputs

Collect relationships among:

* Strategic priorities.
* Enterprise objectives.
* Division or departmental objectives.
* Team objectives.
* Initiative objectives.
* Key results.
* Deliverables and activities.

For each relationship, capture:

* Parent and child objective.
* Expected contribution.
* Shared or related measure.
* Degree of dependency.
* Evidence.
* Confidence level.

## Horizontal alignment inputs

Collect information about:

* Objectives shared across teams.
* Multiple teams contributing to the same key result.
* Duplicate or competing objectives.
* Conflicting targets or timelines.
* Shared dependencies.
* Resource conflicts.
* Metric ownership.
* Cross-functional commitments.
* Required coordination forums.

## OKR-quality criteria

Evaluate objectives for:

* Strategic relevance.
* Clarity.
* Outcome orientation.
* Appropriate scope.
* Time-bound focus.
* Clear ownership.

Evaluate key results for:

* Specificity.
* Measurability.
* Baseline and target.
* Outcome orientation.
* Time-bound completion.
* Reliable data source.
* Clear ownership.
* Ability to demonstrate progress toward the objective.
* Appropriate level of ambition.
* Limited potential for gaming or unintended behavior.

## Optional supporting inputs

* Strategic plans and operating plans.
* Portfolio and initiative roadmaps.
* Performance dashboards.
* Benefits-realization reports.
* Resource and capacity plans.
* Decision logs.
* Risk and issue registers.
* Historical OKR results.
* Stakeholder feedback.
* Financial and operational forecasts.
* External mandates or deadlines.
* Approved OKR templates and guidance.

## Input validation

Before assessing OKR alignment:

* Confirm that the OKRs apply to the same planning period or document valid cross-period dependencies.
* Verify owners, baselines, targets, dates, metrics, and data sources.
* Distinguish objectives from key results, initiatives, deliverables, and tasks.
* Identify key results that measure activity rather than outcome.
* Identify objectives without measurable key results.
* Identify key results without reliable baselines or data sources.
* Identify duplicated, conflicting, overly broad, or cascading OKRs that merely repeat parent wording.
* Confirm that initiative contributions are supported by evidence.
* Flag missing, inconsistent, outdated, or unverified information.
* Do not infer alignment from similar language alone.
* Do not assume that every initiative or operational responsibility requires its own OKR.
