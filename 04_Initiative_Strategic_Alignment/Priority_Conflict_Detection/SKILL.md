# Priority Conflict Detection

## Purpose

Identify where approved or proposed priorities may be unable to coexist because they require incompatible direction, limited resources, overlapping authority, conflicting deadlines, or unresolved dependencies.

## Instructions

1. Review the strategic, initiative, resource, schedule, and governance information defined in `INPUTS.md`.
2. Confirm that priorities, requirements, capacity, and timelines cover the same planning period.
3. Identify potential conflicts involving:

   * Strategic direction.
   * Funding.
   * Staffing.
   * Specialized expertise.
   * Technology capacity.
   * Deadlines.
   * Dependencies.
   * Scope.
   * Ownership.
   * Governance.
   * Success measures.
   * Stakeholder expectations.
4. Compare required resources with available capacity.
5. Identify priorities requiring the same constrained resource during overlapping periods.
6. Review schedules and dependencies for:

   * Incompatible deadlines.
   * Impossible sequencing.
   * Missing prerequisite decisions.
   * Shared critical-path resources.
7. Review scope for duplication, overlap, or incompatible outcomes.
8. Review decision and governance records for conflicting direction or unclear authority.
9. Review objectives and success measures for incentives that may produce contradictory behavior.
10. Distinguish each finding as:

    * Confirmed conflict.
    * Emerging conflict.
    * Potential conflict.
    * Coordination need.
    * Not substantiated.
11. For each finding, identify:

    * Priorities or initiatives involved.
    * Source of the conflict.
    * Shared resource, dependency, or decision.
    * Time period affected.
    * Current and potential consequences.
    * Sponsors and owners involved.
    * Evidence and confidence.
12. Estimate when the conflict may become material, based on supported deadlines and dependencies.
13. Identify information required to validate potential conflicts.
14. Recommend only the appropriate next analytical or governance step:

    * Clarify ownership.
    * Validate capacity.
    * Confirm priority.
    * Resolve a dependency.
    * Coordinate sequencing.
    * Review scope.
    * Conduct trade-off analysis.
    * Apply `Manage Competing Priorities`.
    * Escalate for leadership review.
15. Present findings using the structure defined in `OUTPUTS.md`.

## Detection principles

* Similar subject matter does not automatically establish a conflict.
* Shared resources do not constitute a conflict when capacity and timing are sufficient.
* A coordination need should not be overstated as a priority conflict.
* Urgency does not establish strategic priority or decision authority.
* Potential conflicts should remain labeled as potential until evidence is sufficient.
* Conflict detection identifies problems; it does not choose which priority should prevail.
* Earlier visibility is valuable even when the exact impact remains uncertain.

## Guardrails

* Use only approved priorities and authorized planning, capacity, governance, and decision evidence.
* Do not invent conflicts, shortages, deadlines, owners, authority, dependencies, or consequences.
* Do not infer conflict solely from titles, organizational boundaries, or stakeholder disagreement.
* Do not recommend a final resolution before the conflict is validated and trade-offs are assessed.
* Clearly distinguish facts, warning signals, assumptions, analysis, and recommendations.
* Cite sources and reporting dates for material findings.
* Flag incomplete, conflicting, outdated, or unverified information.
* Do not change priorities, resources, funding, scope, sequencing, ownership, or deadlines.
* Require authorized portfolio or executive review before findings influence organizational commitments.
