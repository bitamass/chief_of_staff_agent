# Inputs — Priority Conflict Detection

## Required strategic context

* Approved strategic goals and executive priorities.
* Priority definitions and assigned priority levels.
* Planning period.
* Executive sponsors and accountable owners.
* Mandatory institutional, clinical, regulatory, operational, or contractual commitments.
* Approved prioritization criteria.
* Current leadership decisions affecting scope, funding, resources, sequencing, or deadlines.

## Required priority and initiative information

For each priority, initiative, project, or commitment, collect:

* Identifier and name.
* Objective and expected outcome.
* Strategic goal supported.
* Executive sponsor and accountable owner.
* Priority level and rationale.
* Mandatory or discretionary classification.
* Start date and target completion date.
* Major milestones and deliverables.
* Required resources.
* Assigned resources.
* Budget and funding source.
* Technology and integration requirements.
* Dependencies.
* Risks and constraints.
* Current status.
* Decision or approval requirements.
* Stakeholders affected.
* Source and last-updated date.

## Resource and capacity inputs

Collect:

* Available funding.
* Current and forecasted staffing capacity.
* Specialized skills and expertise.
* Team allocation by initiative.
* Technology and integration capacity.
* Vendor or partner capacity.
* Leadership and decision-maker availability.
* Change-management capacity.
* Protected operational responsibilities.
* Resource assumptions and planning horizon.

## Schedule and dependency inputs

Collect:

* Milestone and delivery calendars.
* Fixed and flexible deadlines.
* Shared dependencies.
* Prerequisite decisions and approvals.
* Shared systems, vendors, teams, or facilities.
* Required sequencing.
* External deadlines and mandates.
* Critical-path activities.
* Planned resource transitions.
* Upcoming executive meetings and decision dates.

## Conflict indicators

Look for:

* Two or more priorities requiring the same limited resource during the same period.
* Conflicting deadlines or milestone sequences.
* Competing funding requests.
* Incompatible strategic directions.
* Duplicate or overlapping scope.
* Conflicting executive or governance decisions.
* Different owners claiming authority over the same work.
* Mandatory work displacing approved strategic work.
* Local priorities conflicting with enterprise priorities.
* Operational continuity competing with transformation work.
* Short-term targets undermining long-term outcomes.
* Success measures encouraging contradictory behavior.
* One initiative creating risk or delay for another.
* Dependencies that cannot be satisfied simultaneously.
* Teams or individuals allocated beyond available capacity.
* Commitments made without corresponding resources.

## Required conflict record

For each potential conflict, collect:

* Priorities or initiatives involved.
* Type of conflict:

  * Strategic direction.
  * Funding.
  * Staffing.
  * Specialized expertise.
  * Technology capacity.
  * Deadline.
  * Dependency.
  * Scope.
  * Ownership.
  * Governance.
  * Success measure.
  * Stakeholder expectation.
* Shared resource, decision, or dependency.
* Time period affected.
* Evidence.
* Current and potential consequences.
* Owners and sponsors involved.
* Existing resolution activity.
* Required resolution date.
* Confidence level.

## Optional supporting inputs

* Portfolio roadmaps.
* Resource and workforce plans.
* Financial forecasts.
* Initiative charters and business cases.
* OKRs and performance measures.
* Decision logs.
* Risk and issue registers.
* Dependency maps.
* Executive calendars.
* Stakeholder correspondence.
* Governance and delegation-of-authority documentation.
* Prior conflict-resolution decisions.

## Input validation

Before classifying a conflict:

* Confirm that the priorities, deadlines, resource needs, and capacity data cover the same period.
* Verify that the work is approved, active, or formally proposed.
* Distinguish a genuine conflict from normal coordination or sequencing.
* Confirm that the resources or dependencies are actually shared and constrained.
* Identify duplicate, outdated, or superseded commitments.
* Verify ownership, sponsorship, and decision authority.
* Separate confirmed conflicts from possible warning signals.
* Flag missing, inconsistent, outdated, or unverified information.
* Do not infer conflict solely because initiatives have similar topics or stakeholders.
* Do not assume urgency establishes priority or authority.
