# Outputs — Priority Conflict Detection

## Executive conflict summary

Provide a concise overview of:

* Confirmed priority conflicts.
* Potential conflicts requiring validation.
* Resources, deadlines, decisions, or dependencies creating the conflicts.
* Strategic goals and commitments affected.
* Conflicts likely to become material soon.
* Matters requiring leadership attention.
* Overall confidence in the findings.

## Priority-conflict register

For each identified conflict, provide:

* Conflict identifier.
* Conflict title.
* Priorities, initiatives, projects, or commitments involved.
* Strategic goals affected.
* Executive sponsors and accountable owners.
* Conflict type:

  * Strategic direction.
  * Funding.
  * Staffing.
  * Specialized expertise.
  * Technology capacity.
  * Deadline.
  * Dependency.
  * Scope.
  * Ownership.
  * Governance.
  * Success measure.
  * Stakeholder expectation.
* Shared resource, decision, or dependency.
* Time period affected.
* Current status.
* Evidence and source.
* Confidence level: high, medium, or low.

## Conflict classification

Classify each item as:

* **Confirmed conflict:** Evidence shows the requirements cannot currently be satisfied together.
* **Emerging conflict:** Current trends indicate a conflict is likely without intervention.
* **Potential conflict:** Warning signals exist, but further evidence is required.
* **Coordination need:** Work can coexist but requires sequencing, clarification, or communication.
* **Not substantiated:** Available evidence does not establish a conflict.

Explain the reason for each classification.

## Conflict impact assessment

For each confirmed or emerging conflict, identify:

* Current operational effect.
* Potential future effect.
* Initiatives, milestones, or decisions at risk.
* Resource or capacity shortfall.
* Stakeholders affected.
* Consequence of no intervention.
* Time until the conflict becomes material.
* Reversibility.
* Related risks and issues.

## Resource and capacity conflicts

Identify:

* Teams or individuals allocated beyond capacity.
* Competing funding demands.
* Specialized-expertise shortages.
* Technology or integration bottlenecks.
* Vendor or partner constraints.
* Leadership-attention conflicts.
* Change-management overload.
* Commitments made without available resources.

## Schedule and dependency conflicts

Identify:

* Incompatible deadlines.
* Milestones requiring the same resource simultaneously.
* Dependencies that cannot be completed in the required sequence.
* Work proceeding before prerequisite decisions or approvals.
* External deadlines affecting multiple priorities.
* One initiative delaying or increasing the risk of another.

## Strategic and governance conflicts

Identify:

* Incompatible strategic directions.
* Local priorities conflicting with enterprise priorities.
* Short-term targets undermining long-term outcomes.
* Conflicting success measures.
* Duplicate or overlapping scope.
* Conflicting executive or governance decisions.
* Unclear or overlapping ownership and authority.

## Conflicts requiring validation

For each possible conflict lacking sufficient evidence, provide:

* Warning signal.
* Missing information.
* Owner responsible for clarification.
* Recommended validation action.
* Target date.
* Consequence if not validated.
* Confidence level.

## Recommended next step

For each confirmed or emerging conflict, recommend one next step:

* Clarify ownership or authority.
* Validate resource requirements.
* Confirm priority level.
* Resolve a dependency.
* Coordinate sequencing.
* Review scope overlap.
* Conduct trade-off analysis.
* Apply the `Manage Competing Priorities` skill.
* Escalate for leadership review.

Do not recommend a final resolution unless sufficient analysis has been completed.

## Leadership decisions required

For each matter requiring leadership direction, provide:

* Decision question.
* Priorities affected.
* Reason leadership involvement is required.
* Decision owner.
* Decision deadline.
* Consequence of delay.
* Evidence still required.

## Human-review requirement

Authorized portfolio or executive leadership must verify conflict classifications and evidence before priorities, funding, resources, scope, ownership, sequencing, or deadlines are changed.
