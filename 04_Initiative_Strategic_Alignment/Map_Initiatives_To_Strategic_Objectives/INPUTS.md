# Inputs — Map Initiatives to Strategic Objectives

## Required strategy inputs

* Approved mission, vision, and strategic plan.
* Strategic goals and objectives.
* Objective identifiers and descriptions.
* Executive owner for each objective.
* Intended outcomes.
* Success measures, key performance indicators, and targets.
* Time horizon and target dates.
* Current executive and business priorities.
* Mandatory institutional, clinical, regulatory, or operational commitments.
* Strategy version, approval date, and effective period.

## Required initiative inputs

For each initiative, collect:

* Initiative identifier and name.
* Objective and problem being addressed.
* Executive sponsor and accountable owner.
* Current lifecycle stage and status.
* Intended outcomes and expected benefits.
* Success measures and targets.
* Primary deliverables.
* Scope and exclusions.
* Target population, service, stakeholder, or organizational unit.
* Start date, milestones, and target completion date.
* Budget and resource requirements.
* Dependencies.
* Risks and constraints.
* Source, version, and last-updated date.

## Required mapping evidence

For every proposed initiative-to-objective connection, collect:

* Strategic outcome the initiative is expected to influence.
* Initiative outcome contributing to that objective.
* Shared or related success measure.
* Expected direction and magnitude of contribution, when supported.
* Time horizon for contribution.
* Direct or indirect relationship.
* Evidence supporting the relationship.
* Assumptions and dependencies.
* Contribution owner.
* Confidence level.

## Mapping classifications

Classify each relationship as:

* **Primary:** The initiative directly exists to advance the strategic objective.
* **Secondary:** The initiative materially supports the objective but has another primary purpose.
* **Enabling:** The initiative provides infrastructure, capacity, governance, or capability required by other strategic work.
* **Indirect:** The relationship is plausible but dependent on additional activities or assumptions.
* **Unclear:** Available evidence does not establish a reliable connection.
* **No demonstrated alignment:** No supported relationship to an approved objective has been identified.

## Alignment-strength factors

Evaluate each proposed mapping using:

* Direct connection between initiative and objective.
* Contribution to the objective’s intended outcome.
* Shared measures or measurable causal relationship.
* Scale of expected contribution.
* Timing of the contribution.
* Dependency on other initiatives or conditions.
* Strength and quality of supporting evidence.
* Consistency with approved scope and funding rationale.
* Executive or governance confirmation of the relationship.

## Portfolio-wide mapping inputs

* Complete initiative inventory.
* Complete strategic-objective inventory.
* Current resource allocation.
* Expected benefits by initiative.
* Existing portfolio-to-strategy maps.
* Strategic objectives without active initiatives.
* Initiatives mapped to multiple objectives.
* Duplicate or overlapping initiatives.
* Prior alignment decisions.
* Upcoming strategy or portfolio review dates.

## Optional supporting inputs

* Initiative charters and business cases.
* Benefits-realization plans.
* Portfolio roadmaps.
* OKRs and performance dashboards.
* Financial and resource plans.
* Governance and funding decisions.
* Stakeholder feedback.
* Risk and dependency maps.
* Historical initiative outcomes.
* Audit or assurance findings.
* External mandates and commitments.

## Input validation

Before mapping initiatives:

* Confirm that the strategic objectives are current, approved, and clearly defined.
* Verify that initiative objectives, outcomes, measures, owners, and scope are current.
* Distinguish an initiative’s intended outcome from its activities or deliverables.
* Require evidence beyond similar terminology.
* Identify initiatives with broad claims of supporting every objective.
* Identify objectives with no measurable outcomes or targets.
* Flag initiatives without clear objectives, benefits, or success measures.
* Identify conflicting, incomplete, outdated, or unverified information.
* Do not infer strategic alignment from sponsorship, funding, organizational location, or initiative title alone.
* Do not force every initiative to map to a strategic objective when evidence is insufficient.
