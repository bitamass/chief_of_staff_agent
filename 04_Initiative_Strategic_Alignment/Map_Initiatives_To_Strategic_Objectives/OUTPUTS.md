# Outputs — Map Initiatives to Strategic Objectives

## Executive alignment summary

Provide a concise overview of:

* Overall initiative alignment with approved strategic objectives.
* Objectives receiving strong, limited, excessive, or no initiative support.
* Initiatives with strong, indirect, unclear, or no demonstrated alignment.
* Duplicate or overlapping investments.
* Strategic gaps requiring leadership attention.
* Material evidence limitations.
* Recommended mapping clarifications or portfolio actions.

## Strategic objective coverage

For each strategic objective, provide:

* Objective identifier and title.
* Executive owner.
* Intended outcome.
* Success measures and targets.
* Time horizon.
* Supporting initiatives.
* Total investment or resources, when available.
* Coverage assessment:

  * Strong.
  * Moderate.
  * Limited.
  * Excessive or duplicative.
  * No demonstrated coverage.
  * Insufficient evidence.
* Coverage gaps or overlaps.
* Confidence level: high, medium, or low.

## Initiative-to-objective mapping matrix

For each initiative, provide:

* Initiative identifier and name.
* Executive sponsor and accountable owner.
* Initiative objective.
* Intended outcome.
* Primary strategic objective supported.
* Additional strategic objectives supported.
* Relationship classification:

  * Primary.
  * Secondary.
  * Enabling.
  * Indirect.
  * Unclear.
  * No demonstrated alignment.
* Alignment strength: strong, moderate, weak, or unsupported.
* Expected contribution.
* Shared measure or indicator.
* Contribution time horizon.
* Dependencies and assumptions.
* Evidence and source.
* Confidence level.

## Mapping rationale

For every mapped relationship, explain:

* How the initiative contributes to the objective.
* Which initiative outcome supports the strategic outcome.
* What evidence supports the connection.
* How the contribution will be measured.
* Whether the relationship is direct or dependent on other work.
* Material assumptions and limitations.

Do not rely only on similar language between initiative and objective names.

## Strategic coverage gaps

Identify:

* Objectives without supporting initiatives.
* Objectives supported only by indirect or enabling work.
* Objectives lacking measurable initiatives or outcomes.
* Objectives dependent on one high-risk initiative.
* Objectives with insufficient investment or capacity.
* Objectives whose supporting initiatives do not align with the required time horizon.

## Misalignment and duplication

Identify:

* Initiatives without demonstrated strategic alignment.
* Initiatives claiming alignment with too many objectives.
* Duplicate or overlapping initiatives supporting the same outcome.
* Initiatives whose current scope differs from their original strategic rationale.
* Initiatives with deliverables but no measurable strategic outcome.
* Investments concentrated excessively in one objective.
* Conflicts between current work and approved strategic direction.

## Recommended mapping and portfolio actions

For each identified concern, recommend one or more actions:

* Confirm the mapping.
* Clarify the initiative objective.
* Define or revise success measures.
* Provide additional evidence.
* Narrow the claimed strategic alignment.
* Assign a contribution owner.
* Resolve duplication.
* Coordinate related initiatives.
* Review initiative scope.
* Review investment or capacity.
* Escalate for leadership direction.

Present recommendations as proposed actions, not approved changes.

## Leadership decisions required

For each required decision, provide:

* Decision question.
* Strategic objectives and initiatives affected.
* Available options.
* Trade-offs.
* Decision owner.
* Decision deadline.
* Consequence of delay.
* Evidence still required.

## Information gaps and confidence

Identify:

* Missing or outdated strategic objectives.
* Initiatives without clear outcomes or measures.
* Unsupported alignment claims.
* Unknown investment or resource levels.
* Missing contribution owners.
* Conflicting strategy or initiative records.
* Relationships that cannot be verified.
* Confidence level for each major finding.

## Human-review requirement

Authorized strategy and portfolio owners must review and approve initiative-to-objective mappings before they are used for funding, prioritization, performance evaluation, or portfolio changes.
