# Map Initiatives to Strategic Objectives

## Purpose

Create an evidence-based connection between initiatives and approved strategic objectives. Show how each initiative contributes to strategic outcomes, how that contribution will be measured, and where alignment gaps, overlap, or unsupported claims exist.

## Instructions

1. Review the strategic and initiative materials defined in `INPUTS.md`.
2. Confirm that the strategic plan, goals, objectives, measures, and effective dates are current and approved.
3. Confirm that each initiative has a current:

   * Objective.
   * Scope.
   * Owner and sponsor.
   * Intended outcome.
   * Expected benefit.
   * Success measure.
   * Timeline.
4. Distinguish initiative activities and deliverables from intended organizational outcomes.
5. For each initiative, identify the strategic objective it is expected to advance.
6. Require evidence demonstrating:

   * Which initiative outcome contributes to the strategic outcome.
   * How the contribution occurs.
   * How the contribution will be measured.
   * When the contribution is expected.
   * Which dependencies or assumptions affect the relationship.
7. Classify each initiative-to-objective relationship as:

   * Primary.
   * Secondary.
   * Enabling.
   * Indirect.
   * Unclear.
   * No demonstrated alignment.
8. Assess alignment strength as:

   * Strong.
   * Moderate.
   * Weak.
   * Unsupported.
9. Explain every classification using source-linked evidence.
10. Assign a primary objective where supported. Do not allow broad claims that an initiative equally supports every objective without evidence.
11. Review each strategic objective to identify:

    * Supporting initiatives.
    * Expected contributions.
    * Measures and targets.
    * Resource coverage.
    * Gaps.
    * Duplication.
    * Dependence on a single high-risk initiative.
12. Identify:

    * Objectives without initiative support.
    * Initiatives without demonstrated strategic alignment.
    * Duplicate or overlapping initiatives.
    * Initiatives whose current scope differs from their original strategic rationale.
    * Initiatives with outputs but no measurable strategic outcome.
13. Recommend clarification, measurement, coordination, scope review, or leadership direction where necessary.
14. Present the mapping using the structure defined in `OUTPUTS.md`.

## Mapping principles

* Similar terminology does not establish strategic alignment.
* Sponsorship, funding, or organizational placement does not prove alignment.
* Activities and deliverables are not strategic outcomes.
* Enabling initiatives may contribute indirectly and should be labeled accordingly.
* An initiative may support multiple objectives, but its primary contribution should be clear.
* Not every initiative must map to a strategic objective if it exists for a mandatory operational, regulatory, clinical, or risk-related purpose; document that basis separately.
* Missing evidence should result in an unclear or unsupported mapping—not a forced connection.
* Strategic alignment should be demonstrated through outcomes and measures whenever possible.

## Guardrails

* Use only current, approved strategic objectives and authorized initiative evidence.
* Do not invent goals, outcomes, measures, owners, benefits, dependencies, mappings, or approvals.
* Do not infer alignment from initiative titles, broad descriptions, or similar language alone.
* Do not force every initiative to map to an objective.
* Clearly distinguish documented facts, assumptions, analysis, and recommendations.
* Cite the source and effective date supporting each material mapping.
* Flag incomplete, conflicting, outdated, overly broad, or unverified claims.
* Do not change initiative scope, funding, ownership, measures, or strategic classification.
* Require authorized strategy and portfolio-owner review before mappings influence prioritization, funding, performance evaluation, or portfolio decisions.
