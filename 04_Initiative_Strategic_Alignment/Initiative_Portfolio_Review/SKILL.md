# Initiative Portfolio Review

## Purpose

Provide leadership with an evidence-based view of portfolio health and balance. Identify where initiatives are succeeding, where intervention is needed, and where investments, capacity, risks, or dependencies may be misaligned.

## Instructions

1. Review the portfolio context and initiative data defined in `INPUTS.md`.
2. Confirm:

   * Review period.
   * Strategic goals and priorities.
   * Complete initiative inventory.
   * Portfolio owner and governance body.
   * Approved health and evaluation criteria.
   * Available funding and organizational capacity.
3. Separate initiatives by lifecycle stage:

   * Proposed.
   * Approved.
   * Active.
   * Paused.
   * Completed.
   * Closed.
4. Distinguish mandatory initiatives from discretionary work.
5. For each initiative, assess:

   * Strategic alignment.
   * Scope.
   * Schedule.
   * Budget.
   * Resource capacity.
   * Delivery progress.
   * Expected value.
   * Benefits realization.
   * Risks and issues.
   * Dependencies.
   * Governance and decision readiness.
   * Confidence in reporting.
6. Assign an overall health status using approved definitions:

   * Green.
   * Amber.
   * Red.
   * Insufficient evidence.
7. Do not assign a positive health status when material evidence is missing.
8. Evaluate strategic coverage by identifying:

   * Goals with sufficient support.
   * Goals with insufficient support.
   * Goals receiving excessive or duplicate investment.
   * Initiatives without a clear strategic connection.
9. Assess portfolio balance across:

   * Short- and long-term outcomes.
   * Mandatory and discretionary work.
   * Innovation and operational stability.
   * Risk levels.
   * Investment horizons.
   * Organizational units and stakeholder groups.
10. Analyze funding, staffing, technology capacity, leadership attention, and change-management capacity.
11. Identify:

    * Overallocated resources.
    * Competing initiatives.
    * Shared dependencies.
    * Systemic risks.
    * Cascading issues.
    * Unresolved portfolio decisions.
12. Compare expected benefits with benefits realized to date.
13. Identify initiatives whose value is unclear, delayed, unsupported, or declining.
14. Recommend evidence-based portfolio actions:

    * Continue.
    * Accelerate.
    * Monitor.
    * Correct.
    * Rebaseline.
    * Revise scope.
    * Resolve dependency.
    * Review benefits.
    * Sequence.
    * Combine.
    * Pause.
    * Consider discontinuation.
    * Escalate.
15. Identify leadership decisions required, including trade-offs, owners, deadlines, and consequences of delay.
16. Present the review using the structure defined in `OUTPUTS.md`.

## Portfolio-review principles

* Portfolio review evaluates collective health and balance, not only individual initiative performance.
* Green status requires current supporting evidence.
* Delivery progress and benefit realization are different measures.
* Mandatory work should remain visible in resource and capacity analysis.
* Completed initiatives should remain in review until benefits and follow-through are assessed.
* High investment does not establish high strategic value.
* Individual initiatives may appear healthy while the overall portfolio remains unbalanced.
* Recommendations should consider cross-initiative effects and portfolio capacity.

## Guardrails

* Use only approved strategic goals, criteria, portfolio data, and supporting evidence.
* Do not invent initiative status, benefits, costs, owners, risks, dependencies, health thresholds, or approvals.
* Do not compare initiatives using inconsistent definitions or time periods.
* Clearly distinguish documented facts, assumptions, analysis, and recommendations.
* Cite sources and reporting dates for material findings.
* Flag incomplete, conflicting, outdated, optimistic, or unverified information.
* Do not automatically change funding, resources, scope, sequencing, ownership, or lifecycle status.
* Do not present recommended interventions as approved decisions.
* Require authorized portfolio-leadership review before recommendations are implemented.
