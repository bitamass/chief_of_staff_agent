# Outputs — Initiative Portfolio Review

## Executive portfolio summary

Provide a concise overview of:

* Overall portfolio health.
* Strategic goals receiving sufficient, insufficient, or excessive initiative coverage.
* Initiatives performing well.
* Initiatives off track, at risk, or lacking evidence.
* Major resource, funding, capacity, and dependency constraints.
* Expected versus realized value.
* Portfolio-level decisions requiring leadership attention.
* Recommended interventions or further analysis.

## Portfolio overview

Provide:

* Review period.
* Portfolio owner and governance body.
* Number of initiatives by lifecycle stage.
* Total approved and forecasted investment.
* Available and allocated capacity.
* Mandatory and discretionary initiative counts.
* Expected and realized portfolio benefits.
* Major upcoming milestones and decision dates.
* Overall confidence in portfolio reporting.

## Initiative health summary

For each initiative, provide:

* Initiative name.
* Objective.
* Executive sponsor and accountable owner.
* Strategic goals supported.
* Lifecycle stage.
* Overall health: green, amber, red, or insufficient evidence.
* Scope status.
* Schedule status.
* Budget status.
* Resource status.
* Delivery progress.
* Expected and realized benefits.
* Risk and issue exposure.
* Dependency status.
* Governance or decision readiness.
* Required leadership attention.
* Evidence and last-updated date.
* Confidence level: high, medium, or low.

Do not assign a health status without defining and applying consistent criteria.

## Strategic alignment and coverage

For each strategic goal, identify:

* Supporting initiatives.
* Investment and resources allocated.
* Expected benefits.
* Coverage strength.
* Duplicate or overlapping work.
* Gaps in initiative support.
* Initiatives without a clear strategic connection.
* Confidence in the alignment evidence.

## Portfolio balance assessment

Assess portfolio balance across:

* Strategic goals.
* Mandatory and discretionary work.
* Short-term and long-term outcomes.
* Innovation and operational stability.
* Risk levels.
* Investment horizons.
* Organizational units and stakeholders.
* Resource concentration.
* Initiative timing and sequencing.
* Expected benefits and delivery capacity.

Identify material imbalances and their consequences.

## Resource and capacity assessment

Identify:

* Overallocated teams or specialized roles.
* Funding pressures and forecast variance.
* Technology or integration bottlenecks.
* Leadership-attention constraints.
* Change-management capacity limits.
* Initiatives competing for the same resources.
* Underutilized or misaligned capacity.
* Work that cannot proceed under current constraints.

## Portfolio risks, issues, and dependencies

Identify:

* Risks affecting multiple initiatives.
* Shared dependencies.
* Cascading risks or failure points.
* Repeated issues or delivery patterns.
* Initiatives blocked by unresolved decisions.
* Regulatory, clinical, security, privacy, financial, operational, or reputational exposure.
* Mitigation owners and review dates.

Keep risks, realized issues, and dependencies clearly separated.

## Benefits and value assessment

For each initiative, compare:

* Expected benefit.
* Benefit realized to date.
* Measurement method.
* Benefit owner.
* Time to value.
* Variance from expectation.
* Confidence in the evidence.

Identify initiatives with unclear, delayed, unsupported, or declining expected value.

## Recommended portfolio actions

For each initiative, recommend one or more actions:

* Continue.
* Accelerate.
* Monitor.
* Correct.
* Rebaseline.
* Revise scope.
* Resolve dependency.
* Review benefits.
* Sequence.
* Combine.
* Pause.
* Consider discontinuation.
* Escalate for leadership decision.

Explain the evidence, trade-offs, owner, and recommended review date. Do not present recommendations as approved changes.

## Leadership decisions required

For each required decision, provide:

* Decision question.
* Initiatives affected.
* Available options.
* Trade-offs and consequences.
* Decision owner.
* Decision deadline.
* Consequence of delay.
* Evidence still required.

## Information gaps and confidence

Identify:

* Missing or inconsistent health data.
* Outdated financial or resource information.
* Unverified benefits.
* Unclear ownership or success measures.
* Unknown dependencies.
* Conflicting status reports.
* Initiatives that cannot be evaluated fairly.
* Confidence level for each major finding.

## Human-review requirement

Authorized portfolio leadership must review the findings before initiative health, investment, resources, scope, sequencing, ownership, or lifecycle status is changed.
