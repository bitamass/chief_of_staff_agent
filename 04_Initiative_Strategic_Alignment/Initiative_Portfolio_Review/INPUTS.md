# Inputs — Initiative Portfolio Review

## Required portfolio context

* Approved strategic goals and current executive priorities.
* Portfolio objectives and review period.
* Complete list of proposed, approved, active, paused, completed, and closed initiatives.
* Portfolio owner and governance or review body.
* Approved portfolio evaluation criteria.
* Available funding, workforce, technology capacity, and change-management capacity.
* Prior portfolio-review decisions and recommendations.
* Organizational constraints, mandates, and risk-tolerance thresholds.

## Required initiative information

For each initiative, collect:

* Initiative identifier and name.
* Objective and expected outcome.
* Strategic goals and priorities supported.
* Executive sponsor and accountable owner.
* Current lifecycle stage.
* Current health status.
* Start date and target completion date.
* Major milestones and deliverables.
* Progress against plan.
* Approved budget and current forecast.
* Staffing and specialized resource requirements.
* Expected benefits and success measures.
* Benefits realized to date.
* Dependencies.
* Risks, issues, and constraints.
* Decisions and approvals required.
* Stakeholders affected.
* Current recommendation or requested leadership action.
* Source and last-updated date.

## Portfolio-level information

Collect:

* Total approved and forecasted portfolio cost.
* Resource allocation by initiative.
* Capacity by team, role, or specialized skill.
* Portfolio roadmap and initiative sequencing.
* Shared dependencies.
* Duplicate or overlapping initiatives.
* Competing demands for funding, staffing, systems, or leadership attention.
* Strategic goals with insufficient or excessive initiative coverage.
* Mandatory versus discretionary work.
* Portfolio-level risks and systemic issues.
* Expected benefit distribution across the portfolio.
* Planned and realized value.
* Portfolio change and delivery capacity.
* Upcoming decision, funding, and review dates.

## Initiative-health dimensions

Assess each initiative using approved measures such as:

* Strategic alignment.
* Scope.
* Schedule.
* Budget.
* Resource capacity.
* Delivery progress.
* Expected value.
* Benefits realization.
* Risk and issue exposure.
* Dependency status.
* Stakeholder alignment.
* Governance and decision readiness.
* Technical or operational readiness.
* Change-management readiness.
* Confidence in reporting.

## Portfolio-balance dimensions

Review the portfolio for balance across:

* Strategic goals.
* Short-term and long-term outcomes.
* Mandatory and discretionary work.
* Innovation and operational stability.
* Clinical, operational, technical, and administrative priorities.
* Risk level.
* Investment horizon.
* Organizational units and stakeholder groups.
* Resource concentration.
* Timing and sequencing.
* Expected benefits and organizational capacity.

## Optional supporting inputs

* Initiative charters and business cases.
* Financial forecasts.
* Benefits-realization reports.
* Resource and workforce plans.
* Portfolio dashboards.
* Risk and issue registers.
* Dependency maps.
* Architecture, security, privacy, compliance, and clinical assessments.
* Stakeholder feedback.
* Prior decision logs.
* Audit or assurance reports.
* External mandates, deadlines, or market developments.
* Lessons learned from completed or cancelled initiatives.

## Input validation

Before reviewing the portfolio:

* Confirm that the initiative inventory is complete and current.
* Verify that health data uses a consistent reporting period and definitions.
* Confirm sponsors, owners, lifecycle stages, budgets, timelines, and expected benefits.
* Distinguish approved initiatives from proposals or concepts.
* Separate mandatory initiatives from discretionary work.
* Identify missing or inconsistent status, cost, benefit, capacity, risk, or dependency information.
* Identify duplicate, overlapping, competing, or interdependent initiatives.
* Verify that completed or paused initiatives remain visible for benefits and follow-through review.
* Flag optimistic, outdated, unsupported, or conflicting status reports.
* Do not compare initiatives using inconsistent definitions or time horizons.
* Do not treat missing evidence as evidence of good health or low value.
