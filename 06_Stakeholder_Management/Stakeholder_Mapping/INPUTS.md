# Inputs

## Required inputs

- Initiative, program, decision, organizational change, or strategic objective being assessed.
- Purpose and scope of the stakeholder map.
- Known stakeholders, including individuals, groups, departments, committees, partners, vendors, regulators, and affected communities.
- Stakeholder names, roles, titles, organizations, and contact or relationship owners.
- Formal responsibilities, decision authority, governance roles, and approval rights.
- Known influence over funding, policy, implementation, adoption, operations, or other stakeholders.
- Degree to which each stakeholder is affected by the work.
- Current level of interest, participation, awareness, and support.
- Relevant decisions, commitments, concerns, risks, and prior interactions.
- Important milestones, deadlines, approvals, and dependencies.

## Stakeholder relationship inputs

- Reporting, governance, advisory, funding, operational, or partnership relationships.
- Known alliances, dependencies, conflicts, and communication pathways.
- Individuals who influence formal decision-makers.
- Stakeholders who control essential information, resources, access, or implementation capacity.
- Stakeholders who may enable, delay, redirect, or block progress.
- Existing executive sponsors, champions, advocates, neutral parties, concerned parties, and opponents.
- Stakeholder relationships that are indirect, informal, emerging, or uncertain.

## Assessment inputs

For each stakeholder, collect or estimate using documented evidence:

- Decision authority.
- Organizational influence.
- Level of interest.
- Degree of impact.
- Current awareness.
- Current level of support.
- Desired level of support or participation.
- Ability to enable or obstruct the work.
- Urgency of engagement.
- Relationship strength.
- Information needs.
- Communication preferences.
- Known concerns and sensitivities.
- Evidence supporting each assessment.

## Governance and risk inputs

- Formal approval bodies and governance committees.
- Legal, regulatory, privacy, security, clinical, labor, financial, procurement, or records-management responsibilities.
- Groups that must be consulted or informed.
- Confidentiality and information-sharing restrictions.
- Underrepresented or affected groups that may not have formal decision authority.
- Stakeholders whose omission could create operational, ethical, legal, reputational, or adoption risks.

## Optional supporting inputs

- Organization charts and governance diagrams.
- Initiative charters, roadmaps, business cases, and responsibility matrices.
- Meeting notes, decision logs, action trackers, and communication records.
- Previous stakeholder maps and engagement plans.
- Surveys, interviews, feedback, or sentiment data.
- Contracts, policies, regulatory requirements, and partnership agreements.
