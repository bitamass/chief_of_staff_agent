# Outputs

## Stakeholder inventory

A consolidated stakeholder list containing:

- Stakeholder name or group.
- Role, title, organization, or organizational unit.
- Relationship to the initiative or decision.
- Formal authority and responsibilities.
- Relationship owner.
- Evidence source.
- Information gaps requiring validation.

## Stakeholder classification

For each stakeholder:

- Stakeholder type:
  - Decision-maker.
  - Approver.
  - Sponsor.
  - Advisor.
  - Influencer.
  - Implementer.
  - Operational owner.
  - Funder.
  - Regulator or governance body.
  - Partner or vendor.
  - Affected group.
  - Information recipient.
- Level of authority.
- Level of influence.
- Level of interest.
- Degree of impact.
- Current support or position.
- Desired support or participation.
- Confidence level in the assessment.

## Influence–interest map

Placement of stakeholders into categories such as:

- High influence, high interest — manage closely.
- High influence, lower interest — keep satisfied.
- Lower influence, high interest — keep informed and involved appropriately.
- Lower influence, lower interest — monitor proportionately.

The output must explain why each material stakeholder was placed in a category and identify the evidence supporting that placement.

## Relationship map

- Formal reporting and governance relationships.
- Informal influence pathways.
- Dependencies among stakeholders.
- Alliances, shared interests, and areas of disagreement.
- Stakeholders who connect otherwise separate groups.
- Potential communication bottlenecks or single points of dependency.
- Unclear relationships requiring validation.

## Stakeholder gaps

- Missing decision-makers, approvers, operational owners, or subject-matter experts.
- Affected groups without representation.
- Stakeholders engaged too late or at an insufficient level.
- Duplicate or unclear ownership.
- Unverified assumptions about influence or support.
- Areas where the stakeholder map relies on incomplete or outdated information.

## Prioritization recommendations

- Stakeholders requiring immediate attention.
- Stakeholders requiring direct executive involvement.
- Stakeholders who should be consulted before key decisions.
- Stakeholders who need ongoing participation.
- Stakeholders who only require periodic information.
- Stakeholders who may create material risk or resistance.
- Recommended relationship owner for each priority stakeholder.

## Executive summary

- Most influential stakeholders.
- Required decision-makers and approvers.
- Strongest supporters or potential champions.
- Material concerns, conflicts, or opposition.
- Missing relationships or representation.
- Immediate stakeholder-management priorities.
- Matters requiring leadership validation.
