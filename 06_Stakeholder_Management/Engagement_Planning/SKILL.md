# Skill: Engagement Planning

## Purpose

This skill develops a structured stakeholder engagement plan for an initiative, decision, organizational change, or executive priority.

It helps leadership determine:

- Who must be engaged.
- Why their involvement matters.
- What outcome is needed from each stakeholder.
- When and how engagement should occur.
- Who should lead the engagement.
- What concerns, dependencies, or risks must be addressed.
- How commitments and changes in stakeholder support will be tracked.

The skill is intended to improve stakeholder alignment and decision readiness while reducing unnecessary meetings, duplicated outreach, inconsistent messages, and late discovery of opposition or unmet expectations.

## Core activities

### 1. Establish the engagement objective

- Identify the initiative, decision, change, or milestone requiring engagement.
- Define the outcome leadership is trying to achieve.
- Determine whether the engagement is intended to inform, consult, align, obtain approval, secure participation, resolve disagreement, or build commitment.
- Identify the decisions and deadlines that shape the engagement plan.

### 2. Identify relevant stakeholders

- Identify individuals and groups who approve, influence, advise, implement, fund, govern, support, or are affected by the work.
- Include stakeholders who may raise operational, financial, technical, clinical, workforce, security, privacy, legal, governance, or reputational concerns.
- Identify missing or underrepresented perspectives.
- Avoid including stakeholders who have no meaningful relationship to the objective.

### 3. Assess and prioritize stakeholders

Evaluate each stakeholder using documented evidence:

- Decision authority.
- Influence over other stakeholders.
- Level of interest.
- Degree of impact.
- Current position or support.
- Desired future position or participation.
- Urgency of engagement.
- Ability to enable, delay, block, or redirect the work.
- Relationship strength and previous commitments.

Use these factors to recommend an engagement priority. Do not assign a stakeholder position or level of support without evidence. If the position is unknown, label it as unknown and recommend validation.

### 4. Define the engagement approach

For each priority stakeholder, determine:

- The specific engagement objective.
- The decision, action, support, or information required.
- The information most relevant to that stakeholder.
- The appropriate message, level of detail, and communication channel.
- The appropriate sender or relationship owner.
- The correct timing and sequence.
- Likely questions, concerns, or objections.
- Required follow-up and evidence of successful engagement.

### 5. Sequence engagement activities

- Identify which stakeholders must be consulted before others.
- Account for formal governance, approval, labor, legal, privacy, security, or communication requirements.
- Prevent stakeholders from learning about material decisions through an inappropriate channel.
- Coordinate related meetings and communications to avoid duplication.
- Align engagement activities with decision dates, milestones, and executive availability.

### 6. Identify engagement risks

Surface risks such as:

- A decision-maker being engaged too late.
- Conflicting messages being delivered to different groups.
- Stakeholders being treated as informed when their approval is required.
- Confidential information being shared with an unauthorized audience.
- Important affected groups being omitted.
- Overreliance on a single stakeholder’s interpretation.
- Unresolved opposition being presented as alignment.
- Engagement activity occurring without a defined objective or owner.

For each material risk, recommend a mitigation, responsible owner, and escalation point.

### 7. Establish follow-up and measurement

- Capture commitments, owners, and due dates arising from engagement.
- Record changes in stakeholder position or support.
- Identify unresolved questions and requested evidence.
- Recommend when the engagement plan should be reviewed.
- Measure progress using observable indicators rather than unsupported perceptions.

## Decision rules

- Prioritize stakeholders based on documented authority, influence, impact, urgency, and dependency—not job title alone.
- Direct executive involvement should be reserved for high-impact relationships, major decisions, significant resistance, or matters requiring executive authority.
- Do not recommend a meeting when a written update or targeted consultation would accomplish the objective.
- Do not interpret silence as agreement.
- Do not describe consultation as approval.
- Do not treat stakeholder attendance as evidence of support.
- Label assumed or unknown stakeholder positions clearly.
- Separate confirmed commitments from proposed or expected actions.
- Preserve the difference between formal governance requirements and informal influence.

## Quality standards

A strong engagement plan must be:

- Purposeful: every engagement has a defined objective.
- Prioritized: effort is concentrated where it can affect outcomes.
- Specific: each activity has an audience, owner, channel, timing, and desired result.
- Sequenced: dependencies and approval requirements are respected.
- Evidence-based: stakeholder assessments are supported by available information.
- Actionable: follow-up responsibilities and deadlines are clear.
- Adaptive: the plan can be updated when stakeholder positions or circumstances change.
- Traceable: material assessments and commitments connect to source information.

## Safeguards and limitations

- Do not invent stakeholder opinions, influence, support, objections, or commitments.
- Do not disclose confidential, privileged, personnel, clinical, security, or restricted information to an unauthorized audience.
- Do not automatically send communications, schedule meetings, or assign work.
- Do not make formal commitments on behalf of an executive or organization.
- Flag missing, conflicting, outdated, or unverified stakeholder information.
- Clearly distinguish recommendations from approved actions.
- Require human review before the plan is used for consequential, sensitive, or external engagement.

## Human role

The executive sponsor or designated engagement owner must:

- Confirm the engagement objective.
- Validate the stakeholder list and assessments.
- Approve sensitive messages and sequencing.
- Decide when executive participation is appropriate.
- Review recommendations involving opposition, escalation, or confidential matters.
- Authorize any communication, meeting request, or organizational commitment.
