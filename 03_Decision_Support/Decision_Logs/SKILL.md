# Decision Logs

## Purpose

Create a complete and traceable record of what was decided, who had authority, why the decision was made, what conditions apply, and what follow-through is required.

## Instructions

1. Review the decision evidence and related materials defined in `INPUTS.md`.
2. Identify statements that may represent a decision.
3. Distinguish each statement as:

   * Proposal.
   * Recommendation.
   * Preference.
   * Discussion point.
   * Action item.
   * Pending decision.
   * Authorized decision.
4. Create a decision-log entry only when the decision wording, authority, date, and source can be verified.
5. Determine whether the decision is:

   * Final.
   * Conditional.
   * Interim.
   * Pending confirmation.
6. Record:

   * Decision identifier and title.
   * Exact decision statement.
   * Decision type.
   * Decision date and effective date.
   * Authorized decision-maker or governance body.
   * Source meeting, document, or communication.
7. Describe the decision context:

   * Problem or question addressed.
   * Goals, initiatives, risks, or issues affected.
   * Reason the decision was required.
   * Consequence of no decision.
8. Document the alternatives considered, including benefits, costs, risks, trade-offs, and dependencies when available.
9. Record the selected direction and documented rationale.
10. Preserve all:

    * Conditions.
    * Limitations.
    * Exceptions.
    * Assumptions.
    * Risks accepted.
    * Matters deferred or excluded.
11. Verify decision authority, required quorum, concurrence, consultation, and outstanding approvals.
12. Identify implementation actions, owners, due dates, dependencies, and completion evidence.
13. Record expected outcomes, success measures, review dates, expiration dates, and reconsideration triggers.
14. Link the decision to:

    * Prior related decisions.
    * Decisions modified or superseded.
    * Actions and commitments.
    * Risks and issues.
    * Policies, charters, plans, or agreements.
15. Cite the source supporting each material element.
16. Assign a record-completeness status:

    * Complete.
    * Complete with minor gaps.
    * Incomplete.
    * Pending confirmation.
17. Present the record using the structure defined in `OUTPUTS.md`.

## Decision-log principles

* A recommendation is not a decision.
* A discussion or expression of preference does not establish approval.
* Work underway does not prove that an authorized decision occurred.
* The decision statement should preserve the approved meaning without adding interpretation.
* Conditions, exceptions, and accepted risks are part of the decision record.
* Dissenting or alternative positions should be preserved when documented.
* The original record should not be overwritten when a later decision changes direction.
* Decision logging documents a decision; it does not implement or monitor it.

## Guardrails

* Use only authorized source materials and documented evidence.
* Do not invent decisions, authority, rationale, alternatives, conditions, approvals, owners, dates, or actions.
* Do not infer approval from silence, attendance, informal conversation, or an unaccepted recommendation.
* Do not omit material limitations, dissent, conditions, or evidence gaps.
* Clearly distinguish facts, assumptions, recommendations, and authorized decisions.
* Flag conflicting, incomplete, outdated, inaccessible, or unverified information.
* Protect confidential, legally restricted, personnel, clinical, security, and privacy-sensitive information.
* Do not communicate the decision, initiate implementation, or update an official system.
* Require verification by the authorized decision-maker, meeting owner, or governance record owner before the entry is treated as official.
