# Alternative and Recommendation Generation

## Purpose

Support executive decision-making by developing materially distinct alternatives, evaluating them consistently, and producing an evidence-based recommendation. Make assumptions, uncertainty, trade-offs, and conditions visible.

## Instructions

1. Review the decision context and supporting materials defined in `INPUTS.md`.
2. Confirm:

   * Decision question.
   * Decision owner and approval authority.
   * Desired outcome.
   * Scope and boundaries.
   * Decision deadline.
   * Success measures.
   * Constraints and non-negotiable requirements.
3. Establish the current-state or no-action baseline.
4. Review existing alternatives and generate additional feasible options when authorized.
5. Consider options such as:

   * Maintain the current state.
   * Take no action.
   * Delay.
   * Conduct a limited pilot.
   * Implement in phases.
   * Modify an existing approach.
   * Combine elements into a hybrid approach.
6. Ensure the alternatives are materially distinct, within scope, and capable of addressing the decision question.
7. Eliminate alternatives that violate non-negotiable requirements, and document the reason for exclusion.
8. For each viable alternative, identify:

   * Intended outcome.
   * Scope.
   * Required resources and cost.
   * Expected benefits.
   * Timeline and time to value.
   * Dependencies.
   * Risks and constraints.
   * Stakeholder effects.
   * Reversibility.
   * Assumptions.
9. Evaluate every alternative using the same approved criteria.
10. Use approved scores and weights when provided. Do not invent weighting.
11. Explain each assessment using source-linked evidence.
12. Compare the trade-offs, including:

    * What is gained.
    * What is given up.
    * Short- and long-term consequences.
    * Resources displaced.
    * Risks introduced or reduced.
    * Flexibility retained or lost.
13. Test how changes in important assumptions, evidence, or weights could affect the preferred option.
14. Recommend an alternative only when the evidence is sufficient.
15. State:

    * Why the recommended option is preferable.
    * Material disadvantages and risks.
    * Conditions required before proceeding.
    * Required approvals.
    * Success measures.
    * Review, pause, or reconsideration triggers.
16. Identify a fallback or contingent recommendation when appropriate.
17. Present the analysis using the structure defined in `OUTPUTS.md`.

## Recommendation principles

* Alternatives must be realistic, materially distinct, and within the defined scope.
* The current-state or no-action baseline should be included unless clearly inapplicable.
* All viable alternatives must be evaluated using consistent criteria.
* The fastest, cheapest, newest, or most familiar option is not automatically the best.
* A recommendation should acknowledge its disadvantages and residual risks.
* High uncertainty may justify a pilot, phased approach, or additional evidence rather than a final commitment.
* A recommendation is decision support, not an approved decision.

## Guardrails

* Use only authorized evidence, criteria, constraints, and stakeholder information.
* Do not invent alternatives that violate stated requirements or available authority.
* Do not invent costs, benefits, risks, stakeholder preferences, scoring weights, or approvals.
* Do not manipulate criteria or scoring to favor a preferred option.
* Clearly distinguish documented facts, estimates, assumptions, analysis, and recommendations.
* Cite sources supporting material assessments.
* Flag missing, conflicting, outdated, or unverified information.
* Do not select an alternative, approve resources, or direct implementation.
* Require authorized leadership review before the recommendation influences a decision or commitment.
