# Outputs — Alternative and Recommendation Generation

## Executive recommendation summary

Provide a concise overview of:

* Decision to be made.
* Desired outcome.
* Alternatives evaluated.
* Recommended alternative.
* Primary reason for the recommendation.
* Most significant benefits, risks, and trade-offs.
* Conditions required for proceeding.
* Confidence in the recommendation.
* Decision deadline and consequence of delay.

## Decision framing

Provide:

* Decision question.
* Decision owner and approval authority.
* Strategic goals and priorities affected.
* Current state or baseline.
* Reason the decision is required.
* Scope and boundaries.
* Success measures.
* Constraints and non-negotiable requirements.
* Consequence of maintaining the current state.

## Alternatives considered

For each alternative, provide:

* Option name.
* Description.
* Intended outcome.
* Scope.
* Required resources.
* Estimated cost.
* Expected benefits.
* Timeline and time to value.
* Dependencies.
* Risks and constraints.
* Stakeholders affected.
* Reversibility.
* Assumptions.
* Evidence and source.
* Confidence level: high, medium, or low.

Include a current-state or no-action alternative unless it is clearly inapplicable, and explain any exclusion.

## Evaluation matrix

Compare each alternative against the approved criteria:

* Strategic alignment.
* Expected organizational value.
* Clinical or operational impact.
* Financial cost and return.
* Implementation effort.
* Feasibility and readiness.
* Time to value.
* Risk.
* Security, privacy, regulatory, and policy requirements.
* Stakeholder impact.
* Workforce and change-management effects.
* Scalability and sustainability.
* Reversibility.
* Confidence in supporting evidence.

For each criterion, provide:

* Score or qualitative assessment.
* Supporting evidence.
* Source.
* Assumptions and limitations.

Calculate a weighted score only when approved weights are provided. Do not create assumed weights.

## Trade-off analysis

For each alternative, identify:

* What is gained.
* What is given up.
* Short-term and long-term consequences.
* Risks introduced or reduced.
* Resources displaced.
* Stakeholders advantaged or burdened.
* Dependencies created.
* Flexibility retained or lost.
* Conditions under which the alternative becomes preferable.

## Recommended alternative

State:

* Recommended option.
* Evidence supporting the recommendation.
* Why it is preferable to the other alternatives.
* Material risks and disadvantages.
* Conditions required before proceeding.
* Required approvals.
* Proposed owner.
* Recommended timing.
* Measures for determining success.
* Triggers for review, pause, or reconsideration.
* Confidence level.

Do not describe the recommendation as an approved decision.

## Secondary or contingent recommendation

When appropriate, identify:

* A fallback alternative.
* Conditions that would trigger its use.
* Evidence or events that could change the recommendation.
* Information required before switching approaches.

## Implementation considerations

Identify:

* Initial actions.
* Accountable owners.
* Required resources.
* Dependencies.
* Governance and approval steps.
* Security, privacy, legal, clinical, or compliance reviews.
* Stakeholder communication and change-management needs.
* Suggested pilot, phased approach, or review checkpoint.
* Decision and implementation deadlines.

## Information gaps and sensitivity

Identify:

* Missing or unreliable evidence.
* Unverified assumptions.
* Unknown costs, benefits, risks, or stakeholder effects.
* Criteria that materially influence the outcome.
* How reasonable changes to assumptions or weights could change the recommendation.
* Alternatives that cannot yet be compared fairly.

## Leadership decisions required

Provide:

* Decision question.
* Recommended option.
* Other viable options.
* Trade-offs requiring leadership judgment.
* Decision owner.
* Decision deadline.
* Consequence of delay.
* Additional evidence required.

## Human-review requirement

Authorized leadership must review the alternatives, assumptions, evidence, scoring, trade-offs, and recommendation before selecting an option, approving resources, or directing implementation.
