# Inputs — Alternative and Recommendation Generation

## Required decision context

* Clear decision question or problem statement.
* Decision owner and approval authority.
* Decision deadline.
* Strategic goals and business priorities affected.
* Initiative, project, service, policy, risk, or issue involved.
* Desired outcome and definition of success.
* Scope and boundaries of the decision.
* Current state or baseline.
* Reason a decision is required now.
* Consequence of delay or no decision.

## Known alternatives

For each existing option, collect:

* Option name and description.
* Intended outcome.
* Scope.
* Required resources.
* Estimated cost.
* Expected benefits.
* Timeline.
* Dependencies.
* Risks and constraints.
* Stakeholders affected.
* Reversibility.
* Supporting evidence.
* Assumptions.
* Proposal owner or source.

Include, when relevant:

* Maintain the current state.
* Take no action.
* Delay the decision.
* Implement a limited pilot.
* Implement in phases.
* Modify an existing approach.
* Combine elements from multiple alternatives.

## Evaluation criteria

Collect the approved criteria and weighting, including as applicable:

* Strategic alignment.
* Expected organizational value.
* Clinical or operational impact.
* Financial cost and return.
* Implementation effort.
* Feasibility and readiness.
* Time to value.
* Risk.
* Security and privacy.
* Regulatory or policy compliance.
* Stakeholder impact.
* Workforce and change-management effects.
* Technical sustainability.
* Scalability.
* Reversibility.
* Confidence in the evidence.

## Constraints and non-negotiable requirements

Document:

* Budget limits.
* Available staffing and specialized expertise.
* Required timelines.
* Technology or architecture requirements.
* Security, privacy, regulatory, clinical, legal, or policy requirements.
* Procurement or contracting limitations.
* Governance and approval requirements.
* Capacity and change-management constraints.
* Required dependencies.
* Prohibited approaches or out-of-scope activities.
* Minimum acceptable outcomes.

## Evidence and analysis

* Business cases and financial models.
* Operational and clinical analyses.
* Technical and architecture assessments.
* Security, privacy, compliance, and legal reviews.
* Risk and issue registers.
* Resource and capacity plans.
* Stakeholder research and feedback.
* Pilot or prototype results.
* Historical performance.
* Comparable initiatives or external benchmarks.
* Assumptions, data limitations, and confidence assessments.
* Source owners, dates, versions, and locations.

## Stakeholder context

For each relevant stakeholder, collect:

* Name, role, organization, or stakeholder group.
* Decision authority.
* Area of responsibility or expertise.
* Documented needs, concerns, and preferences.
* Expected impact from each alternative.
* Required consultation, approval, or communication.
* Conflicts of interest or competing priorities, when documented.

## Alternative-generation requirements

Define:

* Whether new alternatives may be developed.
* Minimum and maximum number of alternatives.
* Required baseline or no-action option.
* Whether pilot, phased, hybrid, or conditional options should be considered.
* Required level of detail.
* Acceptable degree of uncertainty.
* Conditions that would eliminate an option.
* Conditions that would trigger reconsideration.

## Input validation

Before generating alternatives or recommendations:

* Confirm that the decision question and desired outcome are clear.
* Verify the decision owner and approval authority.
* Confirm that evaluation criteria and weights are approved.
* Identify missing cost, benefit, risk, resource, stakeholder, or feasibility evidence.
* Ensure alternatives are materially distinct and within scope.
* Remove options that violate non-negotiable requirements, while documenting why.
* Distinguish evidence from assumptions and estimates.
* Identify conflicts among sources or stakeholder positions.
* Do not assume that the most familiar, least expensive, or fastest option is best.
* Do not invent evidence, stakeholder preferences, scoring weights, or approvals.
