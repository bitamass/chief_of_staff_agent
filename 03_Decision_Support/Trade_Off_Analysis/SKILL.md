# Trade-Off Analysis

## Purpose

Help leadership understand the full consequences of choosing among defined alternatives. Make benefits, costs, risks, opportunity costs, stakeholder effects, and reversibility visible without hiding disadvantages of a favored option.

## Instructions

1. Review the decision context, alternatives, and evidence defined in `INPUTS.md`.
2. Confirm:

   * Decision question.
   * Decision owner and approval authority.
   * Desired outcome.
   * Scope and time horizon.
   * Success measures.
   * Constraints and non-negotiable requirements.
3. Establish the current-state or no-action baseline when relevant.
4. Confirm that alternatives are materially distinct and feasible.
5. Ensure alternatives are evaluated over the same time horizon and using comparable assumptions.
6. For each alternative, document:

   * Intended outcome.
   * Cost and resource requirements.
   * Expected benefits.
   * Timeline and time to value.
   * Dependencies.
   * Risks.
   * Stakeholders affected.
   * Reversibility.
7. Compare all alternatives using the same approved trade-off dimensions.
8. For each alternative, identify:

   * What is gained.
   * What is given up.
   * What is delayed, reduced, or displaced.
   * Risks reduced, introduced, or retained.
   * Stakeholders benefiting or experiencing burden.
   * Future flexibility retained or lost.
9. Evaluate opportunity cost by identifying other work or investments that cannot proceed if the option is selected.
10. Separate sunk costs from future costs.
11. Assess:

    * Best-case outcome.
    * Expected outcome.
    * Worst-case outcome.
    * Residual risk after mitigation.
12. Conduct sensitivity analysis on material assumptions, including cost, benefit, timing, capacity, risk, and approved scoring weights.
13. Identify which assumptions or uncertainties most strongly affect the comparison.
14. Highlight trade-offs requiring leadership judgment rather than analytical resolution.
15. Identify information gaps that prevent fair comparison.
16. Present the analysis using the structure defined in `OUTPUTS.md`.

## Trade-off principles

* Every meaningful option has advantages and disadvantages.
* The current state also carries costs and risks.
* A low initial cost may create higher long-term operating or exit costs.
* Speed may reduce assurance, consultation, or implementation readiness.
* Prior investment does not justify continued investment by itself.
* Opportunity cost should include work, capacity, or flexibility displaced by the choice.
* Stakeholder burdens should be considered alongside organizational benefits.
* Quantitative scores support judgment but do not replace it.
* A trade-off analysis explains consequences; it does not make the decision.

## Guardrails

* Use only approved alternatives, criteria, constraints, and evidence.
* Do not invent costs, benefits, risks, stakeholder effects, weights, or approvals.
* Do not manipulate criteria, time horizons, or assumptions to favor an option.
* Do not omit material disadvantages, residual risks, or opportunity costs.
* Clearly distinguish facts, estimates, assumptions, analysis, and value judgments.
* Cite sources supporting material assessments.
* Flag missing, conflicting, outdated, or unverified information.
* Do not select an alternative, approve resources, or direct implementation.
* Require authorized leadership review before the analysis influences a decision or commitment.
