# Outputs — Trade-Off Analysis

## Executive trade-off summary

Provide a concise overview of:

* Decision being considered.
* Alternatives compared.
* Most consequential trade-offs.
* Benefits and disadvantages of each option.
* Resources or commitments displaced.
* Risks and uncertainties that may affect the choice.
* Trade-offs requiring leadership judgment.
* Decision deadline and consequence of delay.

## Decision framing

Provide:

* Decision question.
* Decision owner and approval authority.
* Desired outcome.
* Strategic goals and priorities affected.
* Current state or baseline.
* Scope and time horizon.
* Success measures.
* Constraints and non-negotiable requirements.
* Consequence of maintaining the current state.

## Alternative profiles

For each alternative, provide:

* Option name and description.
* Intended outcome.
* Scope.
* Cost and resource requirements.
* Expected benefits.
* Timeline and time to value.
* Dependencies.
* Risks.
* Stakeholders affected.
* Reversibility.
* Assumptions.
* Evidence and source.
* Confidence level: high, medium, or low.

## Trade-off comparison matrix

Compare each alternative using the approved dimensions:

* Strategic alignment.
* Organizational value.
* Financial cost and return.
* Clinical or operational impact.
* Quality and service effects.
* Implementation effort.
* Time to value.
* Feasibility and readiness.
* Security, privacy, legal, regulatory, and policy implications.
* Workforce and change-management effects.
* Stakeholder impact and equity.
* Technical complexity.
* Scalability and sustainability.
* Risk and uncertainty.
* Flexibility and reversibility.
* Opportunity cost.
* Resource displacement.

For each assessment, provide supporting evidence, assumptions, limitations, and source.

## Gains and sacrifices

For each alternative, state:

* What leadership gains.
* What leadership gives up.
* What work is delayed, reduced, or displaced.
* Which risks are reduced.
* Which risks are introduced or retained.
* Which stakeholders benefit.
* Which stakeholders experience burden or disruption.
* Which future options remain available.
* Which decisions may become difficult or costly to reverse.

## Resource and opportunity-cost analysis

Identify:

* Funding required.
* Staffing and expertise required.
* Technology and integration capacity consumed.
* Leadership attention required.
* Change-management effort.
* Existing commitments affected.
* Sunk costs.
* Future operating costs.
* Exit or transition costs.
* Alternative uses of the same resources.

## Risk and uncertainty analysis

For each alternative, provide:

* Key risks.
* Likelihood and impact, when supported.
* Existing or proposed mitigation.
* Residual risk.
* Best-case, expected, and worst-case outcomes.
* Critical assumptions.
* Evidence quality.
* Reconsideration triggers.
* Potential unintended consequences.

## Stakeholder impact analysis

For each relevant stakeholder group, provide:

* Expected benefits.
* Expected burdens.
* Service, access, workload, or equity effects.
* Documented concerns or preferences.
* Required consultation or approval.
* Ability to adapt if the option changes.

Do not infer stakeholder positions without evidence.

## Sensitivity analysis

Explain how the comparison changes if:

* Costs are higher than expected.
* Benefits are delayed or lower than expected.
* Required resources are unavailable.
* Timelines extend.
* Risk likelihood or impact increases.
* Evaluation weights change within reasonable approved ranges.
* A key dependency fails.

Identify which assumptions most strongly affect the outcome.

## Leadership judgment required

Identify trade-offs that cannot be resolved analytically, such as:

* Short-term benefit versus long-term capability.
* Speed versus assurance.
* Cost reduction versus service quality.
* Centralization versus local flexibility.
* Innovation versus operational risk.
* Strategic investment versus immediate operational needs.

For each, state the options and consequences without presenting value judgments as facts.

## Information gaps and confidence

Identify:

* Missing or incomparable cost estimates.
* Unverified benefit assumptions.
* Unknown stakeholder effects.
* Incomplete risk information.
* Unclear opportunity costs.
* Conflicting evidence.
* Alternatives that cannot yet be fairly compared.
* Confidence level for each major finding.

## Human-review requirement

Authorized leadership must review the assumptions, evidence, trade-offs, risks, opportunity costs, and stakeholder effects before selecting an alternative or committing resources.
