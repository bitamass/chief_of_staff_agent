# Inputs — Trade-Off Analysis

## Required decision context

* Clear decision question.
* Decision owner and approval authority.
* Decision deadline.
* Desired outcome and success measures.
* Strategic goals and priorities affected.
* Current state or baseline.
* Scope and boundaries of the analysis.
* Reason the trade-off analysis is required.
* Consequence of delay or no decision.

## Alternatives to compare

For each alternative, collect:

* Option name and description.
* Intended outcome.
* Scope.
* Required resources.
* Estimated cost.
* Expected benefits.
* Implementation effort.
* Timeline and time to value.
* Dependencies.
* Risks and constraints.
* Stakeholders affected.
* Reversibility.
* Supporting evidence.
* Key assumptions.
* Confidence in the estimates.

Include the current-state or no-action baseline when relevant.

## Trade-off dimensions

Collect approved evaluation dimensions, including as applicable:

* Strategic alignment.
* Expected organizational value.
* Financial cost and return.
* Clinical or operational impact.
* Quality and service effects.
* Implementation effort.
* Time to value.
* Feasibility and readiness.
* Security and privacy.
* Regulatory, legal, policy, or compliance impact.
* Workforce and change-management effects.
* Stakeholder impact and equity.
* Technical complexity.
* Scalability and sustainability.
* Risk and uncertainty.
* Flexibility and reversibility.
* Opportunity cost.
* Resource displacement.

## Constraints and thresholds

Document:

* Budget ceiling.
* Staffing and specialized-expertise limits.
* Required deadline.
* Minimum acceptable outcomes.
* Risk-tolerance thresholds.
* Security, privacy, clinical, legal, regulatory, or policy requirements.
* Architecture or technology standards.
* Procurement and contracting constraints.
* Governance and approval requirements.
* Required dependencies.
* Prohibited or nonviable approaches.
* Conditions that would eliminate an alternative.

## Resource and opportunity-cost inputs

For each alternative, collect:

* Funding required.
* Staffing and skill requirements.
* Technology and integration capacity.
* Leadership attention.
* Change-management effort.
* Work that would be delayed, reduced, or displaced.
* Existing commitments affected.
* Sunk costs.
* Ongoing operating costs.
* Exit or transition costs.
* Capacity restored or consumed.

## Risk and uncertainty inputs

For each alternative, collect:

* Key risks.
* Likelihood and impact, when supported.
* Residual risk after mitigation.
* Dependencies and failure points.
* Uncertain assumptions.
* Range of cost, benefit, and timeline estimates.
* Best-case, expected, and worst-case outcomes.
* Reconsideration triggers.
* Evidence quality and confidence level.
* Potential unintended consequences.

## Stakeholder inputs

For each affected stakeholder or group, collect:

* Role and interest.
* Expected benefits.
* Expected burdens or adverse effects.
* Documented preferences or concerns.
* Decision or approval authority.
* Required consultation.
* Equity, access, service, or workload implications.
* Ability to adapt to or reverse the selected option.

## Supporting evidence

* Business cases and financial models.
* Operational or clinical analyses.
* Technical and architecture assessments.
* Security, privacy, legal, and compliance reviews.
* Workforce and capacity plans.
* Risk and issue registers.
* Stakeholder research.
* Pilot or prototype results.
* Historical performance.
* Comparable initiatives and benchmarks.
* Source owners, dates, versions, and locations.

## Input validation

Before conducting the trade-off analysis:

* Confirm that the decision question and alternatives are clearly defined.
* Verify that alternatives are materially distinct and evaluated over the same time horizon.
* Confirm that evaluation dimensions, thresholds, and weights are approved.
* Ensure cost, benefit, risk, and resource estimates use comparable assumptions.
* Identify missing or inconsistent evidence.
* Separate sunk costs from future costs.
* Identify double-counted benefits or risks.
* Distinguish documented stakeholder impacts from assumptions.
* Do not exclude disadvantages or residual risks from a favored option.
* Do not invent weights, estimates, stakeholder preferences, or approvals.
