# Skill: Generate Status Reports

## Purpose

This skill converts approved initiative, program, project, portfolio, or operational information into a concise, evidence-based status report.

It helps leadership understand:

- What was completed.
- What changed during the reporting period.
- Whether delivery remains on track.
- Which decisions, actions, milestones, risks, and dependencies require attention.
- What is expected next.
- Where leadership intervention is needed.
- How reliable and current the underlying information is.

The skill is designed to improve reporting consistency and decision readiness. It does not replace source systems, project-owner validation, financial controls, formal governance reporting, or human approval.

## Core activities

### 1. Establish the reporting frame

- Confirm the reporting period.
- Identify the initiative, program, portfolio, project, or operational scope.
- Confirm the intended audience and decisions the report should support.
- Identify the approved status definitions and reporting template.
- Establish the source-data cutoff date.
- Retrieve the previous report for comparison.

### 2. Collect and organize evidence

Organize current information by:

- Objectives and expected outcomes.
- Workstreams.
- Deliverables.
- Milestones.
- Decisions.
- Actions and commitments.
- Risks and issues.
- Dependencies.
- Resources and financials.
- Stakeholder and governance matters.

Every material status statement should connect to an identifiable source, owner, or dated update.

### 3. Determine what changed

Compare the current period with the previous report:

- Newly completed work.
- Changed milestone dates.
- New or closed risks.
- Changed risk severity or trend.
- New decisions.
- Overdue or completed actions.
- Scope, budget, resource, or ownership changes.
- Emerging dependencies.
- New leadership requests.

Avoid repeating unchanged information unless it remains material to executive understanding.

### 4. Evaluate status

Assess status across relevant dimensions:

- Scope.
- Schedule.
- Budget.
- Resources.
- Risk.
- Quality.
- Dependencies.
- Outcomes or benefits.
- Governance and assurance readiness.

Use approved organizational definitions when provided. Otherwise:

#### On track

- Key milestones and deliverables remain achievable.
- No material unresolved issue threatens the expected outcome.
- Risks have credible mitigation plans.

#### Watch

- A concern requires monitoring or management attention.
- Delivery remains achievable without significant intervention.
- Recovery actions are defined.

#### At risk

- A material issue threatens a milestone, outcome, budget, scope, or obligation.
- Leadership action, additional resources, or a significant recovery plan may be needed.

#### Off track

- A major commitment, outcome, milestone, or obligation is unlikely to be achieved under the current plan.
- A revised plan or executive decision is required.

A single severe issue may determine the overall status, but the report must explain why.

### 5. Validate accomplishments

Before reporting work as completed:

- Confirm the deliverable exists.
- Confirm applicable acceptance criteria are met.
- Confirm the authorized owner or approver has accepted it when required.
- Distinguish draft, submitted, approved, implemented, and completed.
- Avoid using percentage-complete values without a documented basis.

Activity does not equal accomplishment. The report should emphasize outcomes and completed commitments.

### 6. Analyze decisions and actions

- Separate decisions made from recommendations and discussions.
- Identify pending decisions and required decision dates.
- Identify overdue, blocked, or unowned actions.
- Connect each action to an owner and due date.
- Explain the effect of delayed decisions or actions.
- Recommend escalation only when supported by impact and prior follow-up.

### 7. Analyze risks and issues

Distinguish:

- **Risk:** a possible future event.
- **Issue:** a condition that has already occurred.
- **Dependency:** an external input or condition required for progress.
- **Assumption:** something treated as true but not yet fully verified.

For each material item:

- State the cause.
- Describe the event or condition.
- Explain the consequence.
- Identify the owner.
- State the mitigation or resolution.
- Indicate trend and target date.
- Identify decisions or assistance required.

### 8. Prepare the forward view

Identify:

- Milestones due next.
- Upcoming decisions.
- Critical meetings and reviews.
- Actions approaching their due dates.
- Dependencies that must be resolved.
- Expected deliverables.
- Leadership participation required.
- Conditions that could change the forecast.

The forward view should explain what must happen—not merely list calendar events.

### 9. Surface leadership attention

Only elevate items that require:

- A decision.
- Priority resolution.
- Resource allocation.
- Risk acceptance.
- Escalation.
- Cross-organizational intervention.
- Governance approval.
- Executive communication.

Each request should specify the requested action, responsible decision-maker, and required date.

## Decision rules

- Do not report an item as completed without evidence.
- Do not equate effort or activity with progress.
- Do not carry forward an old status without checking for current evidence.
- Do not average statuses mechanically to determine overall status.
- Do not use green status when a material commitment is unlikely to be met.
- Do not use red status solely because one low-impact action is overdue.
- Separate factual status from management recommendation.
- Separate risks, issues, dependencies, and assumptions.
- Report changes from the prior period explicitly.
- Use the reporting cutoff date consistently.
- Label stale, missing, conflicting, or unverified data.
- Preserve original owner-provided status when presenting an alternative assessment.

## Quality standards

A strong status report must be:

- Current: based on a defined reporting cutoff date.
- Evidence-based: material claims link to sources or accountable owners.
- Comparative: clearly explains changes from the previous period.
- Outcome-focused: emphasizes completed results and expected outcomes.
- Decision-oriented: highlights items requiring leadership action.
- Consistent: applies common status definitions.
- Concise: prioritizes material information.
- Transparent: identifies uncertainty and disagreement.
- Forward-looking: shows upcoming decisions, milestones, and dependencies.
- Traceable: decisions, actions, risks, and accomplishments can be verified.

## Safeguards and limitations

- Do not fabricate progress, completion, metrics, dates, ownership, or approvals.
- Do not alter owner-provided status without showing the basis for a different assessment.
- Do not conceal adverse status to make reporting appear favorable.
- Do not expose restricted financial, personnel, clinical, legal, privacy, or security information to unauthorized audiences.
- Do not automatically send, publish, approve, or certify a report.
- Do not treat the generated report as an official record until reviewed.
- Flag incomplete, stale, or conflicting inputs.
- Require the appropriate owner to validate consequential status statements.

## Human role

The program owner, initiative lead, executive sponsor, or designated report owner must:

- Confirm the reporting scope and cutoff date.
- Validate accomplishments and milestone status.
- Confirm risk, issue, and action ownership.
- Resolve conflicting source information.
- Approve status classifications.
- Confirm leadership requests and recommendations.
- Review sensitive or restricted content.
- Approve the final report before distribution.
