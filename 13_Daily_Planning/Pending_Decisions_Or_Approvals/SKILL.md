# Skill: Pending Decisions Or Approvals

## Purpose

This skill identifies, organizes, and prioritizes decisions and approvals awaiting executive, managerial, or governance action. It evaluates authority, deadlines, options, evidence, risks, dependencies, stakeholder input, and readiness so the appropriate decision-maker can act with a clear understanding of the request and its consequences.

The skill prepares and tracks decision requests. It does not approve, reject, authorize, endorse, accept risk, or communicate a final decision without authorized human action.

## Core concepts

### Decision versus approval

- A **decision** selects a direction or resolves a defined question.
- An **approval** authorizes a proposed action, expenditure, commitment, exception, or deliverable.
- A request may require both a substantive decision and a subsequent formal approval.

### Recommendation versus authorization

- A **recommendation** expresses a proposed course of action.
- An **authorization** grants permission to proceed.
- A recommendation must never be represented as approval.

### Decision owner versus preparer

- The **preparer** assembles the request and supporting evidence.
- The **requestor** asks for the decision or approval.
- The **decision owner** has authority and accountability for the decision.
- Reviewers and endorsers may advise but do not necessarily have approval authority.

### Decision readiness

- **Ready:** the decision statement, authority, options, evidence, risks, and required reviews are sufficient.
- **Partially ready:** material gaps exist, but the decision-maker can provide direction or conditional approval.
- **Not ready:** the decision cannot responsibly be made with the available information or authority.
- **Awaiting information:** a named input, participant, or review is outstanding.

### Conditional approval

A conditional approval permits progress only when specified conditions are satisfied. Conditions must have:

- A clear requirement.
- An owner.
- A deadline.
- Evidence of completion.
- A person authorized to confirm satisfaction.

### Decision latency

Decision latency is the time between a sufficiently prepared request and an authorized response. The skill should distinguish delays caused by incomplete preparation from delays caused by unavailable authority or unresolved judgment.

## Core activities

### 1. Build the pending-item inventory

Review available records for:

- Explicit decision requests.
- Approval requests.
- Recommendations awaiting authorization.
- Exception requests.
- Risk-acceptance requests.
- Deliverables awaiting acceptance.
- Decisions created by meetings, milestones, issues, or governance processes.

Exclude items that have already been decided, withdrawn, superseded, or canceled unless follow-through remains open.

### 2. Clarify the decision statement

For each item:

- State exactly what must be decided or approved.
- Identify the requested action.
- Explain why the decision is needed now.
- Define the scope and affected parties.
- Identify the consequence of approval, rejection, delay, and no action.
- Separate multiple independent decisions into distinct records when appropriate.

### 3. Confirm authority and governance

- Identify the authorized decision-maker or approving body.
- Verify authority thresholds and delegated limits.
- Identify required reviewers, endorsers, or co-approvers.
- Check for quorum, conflict-of-interest, separation-of-duty, or formal meeting requirements.
- Flag requests lacking a valid approval pathway.
- Do not infer authority from title alone.

### 4. Gather options and evidence

- Identify available options, including maintaining the current state when relevant.
- Summarize benefits, costs, risks, trade-offs, and implementation implications.
- Identify the documented recommendation and its author.
- Retrieve supporting evidence and specialist reviews.
- Separate facts, estimates, assumptions, and interpretations.
- Identify material alternatives that were excluded without sufficient rationale.

### 5. Evaluate decision readiness

Check whether:

- The decision statement is clear.
- Authority is confirmed.
- Options are sufficiently defined.
- Evaluation criteria are explicit.
- Material evidence is current.
- Risks and dependencies are visible.
- Required stakeholders and specialists have been consulted.
- Ownership and implementation responsibilities are known.
- The deadline and consequence of delay are documented.

Assign a readiness status and explain the basis.

### 6. Prioritize the decision queue

Evaluate each pending item based on:

- Decision deadline.
- Consequence of delay.
- Strategic or operational value.
- Risk exposure.
- Number and importance of downstream dependencies.
- Executive-specific authority.
- Stakeholder commitments.
- Readiness.
- Reversibility and time sensitivity.

Do not classify an item as high priority solely because the requestor is senior.

### 7. Identify readiness gaps

For each unresolved gap:

- State what is missing.
- Explain why it matters.
- Assign or propose a responsible owner.
- Set or recommend a completion date.
- Identify whether the decision can proceed conditionally.
- Avoid requesting information that would not materially affect the decision.

### 8. Prepare the decision brief

- Lead with the decision required and its deadline.
- Present options and material trade-offs neutrally.
- Identify the documented recommendation without presenting it as predetermined.
- Surface risks, dependencies, dissenting views, and uncertainty.
- State the readiness status.
- Identify the exact action requested from the decision-maker.

### 9. Recommend the appropriate disposition

Recommend one of the following:

- Decide or approve.
- Approve with explicit conditions.
- Request targeted additional information.
- Seek required specialist review.
- Return for revision.
- Defer to a specific date or triggering condition.
- Escalate to the correct authority.
- Decline or withdraw.
- Separate the request into multiple decisions.

The recommendation must include rationale and required next steps.

### 10. Prepare decision recording and follow-through

After authorized human action:

- Record the decision, decision-maker, date, and rationale.
- Capture conditions and limitations.
- Identify follow-up actions, owners, and deadlines.
- Link the decision to affected initiatives, tasks, risks, and meetings.
- Identify communication and distribution requirements.
- Preserve the evidence reviewed and any dissenting views.

## Decision rules

- Do not represent a recommendation, endorsement, or consensus as formal approval.
- Do not mark an item ready when required authority or material evidence is missing.
- Do not delay a decision for immaterial information.
- Do not recommend conditional approval without measurable conditions, owners, and deadlines.
- Do not infer approval from silence, attendance, prior discussion, or absence of objection.
- Do not treat a decision deadline as confirmed unless supported by a source.
- Preserve rejected, deferred, and withdrawn outcomes in the decision history.
- Flag inconsistency with prior decisions or policy rather than silently replacing them.
- Separate substantive decisions from implementation actions.
- Escalate only through documented authority pathways.
- Require human confirmation before any decision status is changed.

## Quality standards

A high-quality pending-decision review must be:

- **Clear:** the requested decision is explicit.
- **Authorized:** the correct decision-maker and process are identified.
- **Balanced:** options and trade-offs are represented fairly.
- **Evidence-based:** material claims connect to current sources.
- **Risk-aware:** consequences and dependencies are visible.
- **Time-sensitive:** deadlines and delay effects are clear.
- **Decision-ready:** gaps and required actions are explicit.
- **Traceable:** recommendations, approvals, rationale, and follow-through are recorded.
- **Concise:** executives can quickly understand what action is required.
- **Human-controlled:** no approval or authorization occurs automatically.

## Safeguards and limitations

- Do not independently approve, reject, authorize, endorse, defer, or withdraw a request.
- Do not fabricate decision authority, deadlines, recommendations, evidence, or stakeholder positions.
- Do not accept financial, legal, clinical, security, privacy, compliance, personnel, procurement, or operational risk.
- Do not imply institutional approval based on draft or incomplete records.
- Do not send decision communications or update external systems without authorization.
- Do not expose confidential decision materials to unauthorized audiences.
- Do not suppress dissenting views, material uncertainty, or conflicting evidence.
- Do not make specialist determinations requiring qualified review.
- Clearly distinguish proposed, pending, conditional, and final statuses.

## Human role

The authorized decision-maker or approving body must:

- Confirm the decision statement and scope.
- Validate authority and required governance steps.
- Review options, evidence, risks, and stakeholder input.
- Decide, approve, condition, defer, reject, or escalate the request.
- Approve the rationale and any conditions.
- Authorize communication and implementation.
- Confirm that the decision is accurately recorded.
