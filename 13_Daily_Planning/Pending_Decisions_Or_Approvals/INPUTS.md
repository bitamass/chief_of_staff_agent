# Inputs

## Planning and review context

- Review date, time zone, and planning horizon.
- Executive or governance role responsible for the review.
- Organizational unit, initiative, program, or portfolio in scope.
- Current strategic and operational priorities.
- Relevant approval policies, decision rights, and delegated authorities.
- Review cadence and intended audience.
- Calendar events, milestones, or deadlines affected by pending decisions.

## Decision or approval record

For each pending item:

- Unique identifier and concise title.
- Clear decision or approval statement.
- Request type: decision, approval, endorsement, authorization, acceptance, exception, or recommendation.
- Date submitted and source.
- Requestor, sponsor, preparer, and accountable owner.
- Decision-maker or approving authority.
- Current status: drafting, submitted, under review, awaiting information, ready, deferred, escalated, approved, rejected, or withdrawn.
- Required decision or response date.
- Related initiative, deliverable, contract, policy, risk, issue, or commitment.

## Business context and objective

- Purpose of the request.
- Problem, opportunity, or obligation being addressed.
- Desired outcome.
- Strategic or operational alignment.
- Scope and affected organizational areas.
- Expected value, benefit, or required compliance outcome.
- Consequence of approval, rejection, delay, or no action.
- Relevant prior decisions and commitments.

## Options and recommendation

- Options available to the decision-maker.
- Description of each option.
- Documented recommendation and recommending party.
- Evaluation criteria.
- Benefits, disadvantages, costs, risks, and trade-offs.
- Reversibility and implementation implications.
- Assumptions supporting the options or recommendation.
- Alternatives considered and excluded, with rationale.

## Evidence and supporting materials

- Decision memo, business case, proposal, charter, contract, budget, analysis, or supporting report.
- Financial, operational, technical, security, privacy, legal, compliance, workforce, clinical, procurement, or reputational evidence.
- Data sources, analysis dates, and relevant measures.
- Specialist assessments or reviews.
- Stakeholder feedback.
- Prior meeting notes, decisions, and unresolved questions.
- Confidence level and limitations of the available evidence.

## Authority and governance

- Named decision-maker or approving body.
- Applicable authority level and approval threshold.
- Required reviewers, endorsers, or co-approvers.
- Quorum or formal meeting requirements.
- Delegated authority and any limitations.
- Separation-of-duty or conflict-of-interest requirements.
- Required legal, clinical, security, privacy, finance, HR, compliance, procurement, or governance review.
- Escalation pathway when authority or consensus is unavailable.

## Readiness requirements

- Required documents and evidence.
- Required stakeholder consultation.
- Required specialist reviews.
- Required financial or resource validation.
- Required risk assessment.
- Required implementation or change-management plan.
- Required owner and accountability structure.
- Unresolved conditions preventing a decision.
- Person responsible for closing each readiness gap.

## Risks and dependencies

- Risks associated with each option.
- Risks associated with delay or inaction.
- Dependencies on other decisions, approvals, resources, systems, vendors, or milestones.
- Downstream work that cannot begin until the decision is made.
- Time-sensitive opportunities or nonrecoverable deadlines.
- Mitigation or contingency actions.
- Conditions requiring escalation.

## Stakeholder context

- People or groups affected by the decision.
- Stakeholder roles, interests, concerns, and documented positions.
- Required consultation or notification.
- Areas of agreement or disagreement.
- Commitments already made.
- Sensitive relationships or communication considerations.
- Stakeholders absent from the current review.

## Source and data quality

- Source and date for the decision statement, deadline, authority, and recommendation.
- Distinction between confirmed facts, estimates, assumptions, and interpretations.
- Missing, outdated, inconsistent, or unverified information.
- Conflicting recommendations or evidence.
- Confidence in cost, benefit, timing, and risk estimates.
- Items requiring human or specialist validation.
