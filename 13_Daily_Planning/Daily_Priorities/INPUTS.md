# Inputs

## Daily planning context

- Planning date, time zone, and available working hours.
- Executive role, responsibilities, and current decision authority.
- Current strategic priorities and leadership objectives.
- Known deadlines, constraints, travel, and protected focus periods.
- Planning horizon: today only or today with near-term consequences.
- Priorities carried forward from the previous day.

## Candidate priorities

For each potential priority:

- Clear description of the required outcome.
- Related strategic goal, initiative, program, or operational responsibility.
- Requestor, sponsor, accountable owner, and supporting participants.
- Due date, decision deadline, or milestone date.
- Current status: not started, in progress, blocked, awaiting input, or ready for review.
- Estimated time and effort required from the executive.
- Whether executive attention, decision, approval, communication, or sponsorship is specifically required.
- Consequence of completion, delay, delegation, or non-completion.
- Source and date of the request or commitment.

## Calendar and meeting demands

- Meetings scheduled for the day.
- Objective, expected outcome, and executive role for each meeting.
- Preparation and follow-up time required.
- Decisions or commitments expected during each meeting.
- Fixed and flexible calendar commitments.
- Meeting conflicts and available focus periods.
- Items that can be addressed during existing meetings rather than as separate tasks.

## Decisions and approvals

- Decisions that must be made or advanced today.
- Approval requests and their deadlines.
- Available options, recommendations, and trade-offs.
- Required evidence, participants, and specialist reviews.
- Consequence of delay or no decision.
- Decision owner and approving authority.
- Current readiness of the decision materials.

## Actions and commitments

- Open actions assigned to the executive.
- Commitments made by or to the executive.
- Owner, due date, status, and evidence of progress.
- Overdue actions and unresolved follow-ups.
- Commitments affecting other people, initiatives, or upcoming meetings.
- Actions that may be delegated, rescheduled, combined, or closed.

## Risks, issues, and blockers

- Active risks and issues requiring attention.
- Severity, likelihood, impact, and trend.
- Risk, issue, or blocker owner.
- Mitigation, containment, or resolution status.
- Escalation threshold and response deadline.
- Decisions, resources, or authority needed from the executive.
- Consequence of not addressing the matter today.

## Stakeholder considerations

- People requiring a response, decision, update, or engagement.
- Stakeholder role, interest, influence, and urgency.
- Existing commitments or service-level expectations.
- Relationship or reputational consequences of delay.
- Sensitive communications requiring executive review.
- Items that can be handled by another authorized person.

## Dependencies and resources

- Information, people, approvals, systems, or resources required.
- Predecessor and successor tasks.
- Cross-initiative dependencies.
- Capacity or resource constraints.
- Items waiting on another person or team.
- Work that will unblock multiple downstream activities.

## Priority criteria and constraints

- Approved organizational or executive prioritization criteria.
- Strategic value.
- Urgency and deadline.
- Consequence of delay.
- Risk reduction.
- Stakeholder impact.
- Executive-specific contribution.
- Effort and available capacity.
- Ability to delegate or defer.
- Any required weighting or scoring method.

## Source and data quality

- Source and date for each candidate priority.
- Distinction between confirmed commitments, requests, proposals, and assumptions.
- Missing, conflicting, outdated, or unverified information.
- Confidence in effort, urgency, and impact estimates.
- Items requiring executive or subject-matter validation.
