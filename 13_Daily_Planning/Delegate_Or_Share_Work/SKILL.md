# Skill: Delegate Or Share Work

## Purpose

This skill determines whether work should remain with the executive, be delegated to another person, or be completed through shared ownership. It prepares a structured handoff that clarifies the outcome, scope, authority, roles, resources, deadlines, review points, risks, and escalation conditions.

The skill recommends and prepares delegation. It does not assign work, grant authority, change reporting relationships, alter access permissions, or communicate assignments without human authorization and participant confirmation.

## Core concepts

### Delegation versus task assignment

- **Task assignment** transfers responsibility for completing a defined activity.
- **Delegation** transfers responsibility and a defined level of authority for producing an outcome.
- Accountability may remain with the original owner even when responsibility is delegated.

### Delegation versus shared work

- **Delegated work** has a clearly responsible person operating within defined authority.
- **Shared work** requires coordinated contributions from multiple people.
- Shared ownership must not become unclear ownership. One accountable person and one coordinating lead should be identified whenever possible.

### Responsibility versus accountability

- **Responsible:** performs or coordinates the work.
- **Accountable:** ultimately answers for the quality and outcome.
- **Contributor:** provides defined expertise, information, or work products.
- **Reviewer:** evaluates quality, risk, or compliance.
- **Approver:** has authority to accept or authorize the result.
- **Consulted:** provides input before action or decision.
- **Informed:** receives relevant updates or results.

### Delegation authority

Delegation must clarify:

- What the delegate may decide independently.
- What requires consultation.
- What requires approval.
- What may not be performed.
- What conditions require escalation.
- When delegated authority begins and ends.

### Successful handoff

A handoff is complete only when the receiving person:

- Understands the expected outcome.
- Accepts the responsibility.
- Has the necessary context, authority, access, capacity, and support.
- Understands deadlines, quality standards, and escalation conditions.

## Core activities

### 1. Define the work and desired outcome

- Describe the required outcome rather than only listing activities.
- Identify the purpose, deadline, quality requirements, and completion evidence.
- Connect the work to the appropriate objective, initiative, or commitment.
- Identify responsibilities that cannot be transferred.
- Clarify scope and out-of-scope work.

### 2. Determine whether delegation is appropriate

Evaluate:

- Whether executive authority or judgment is uniquely required.
- Whether another person has or can receive the needed authority.
- Whether delegation improves capacity, quality, speed, continuity, or development.
- Whether the time required for handoff and review is proportionate.
- Whether risk, confidentiality, or separation-of-duty requirements restrict delegation.
- Whether shared ownership is more appropriate than a single delegate.

Recommend retaining the work when its material purpose depends on the executive’s direct participation.

### 3. Identify suitable delegates or contributors

Assess each potential participant using:

- Relevant expertise.
- Demonstrated experience.
- Current responsibilities and workload.
- Decision authority.
- Access to information, tools, systems, and stakeholders.
- Ability to meet the required schedule.
- Need for coaching, sponsorship, or specialist support.
- Conflicts of interest or governance restrictions.

Do not infer capability or capacity solely from a person’s title.

### 4. Define responsibility and accountability

- Identify the accountable owner.
- Name the responsible delegate or coordinating lead.
- Identify contributors, reviewers, approvers, and stakeholders.
- Remove overlapping or contradictory assignments.
- Flag any responsibility without a named owner.
- Confirm whether manager or cross-team approval is required.

### 5. Define authority and boundaries

- State which decisions the delegate may make.
- Identify spending, contracting, personnel, security, privacy, clinical, legal, operational, or communications limitations.
- Specify required reviews and approvals.
- Establish escalation triggers and pathways.
- Set the duration or endpoint of delegated authority.
- Preserve accountability with the appropriate authorized person.

### 6. Prepare the context package

Provide:

- Purpose and expected outcome.
- Relevant background and prior decisions.
- Scope and boundaries.
- Source materials and required inputs.
- Stakeholder context.
- Known risks, issues, assumptions, and dependencies.
- Quality and acceptance criteria.
- Sensitive information-handling requirements.

Include enough context for independent progress without overwhelming the delegate with unrelated material.

### 7. Establish milestones and review points

- Break the work into appropriate deliverables or checkpoints.
- Set realistic deadlines.
- Identify review and approval points.
- Define progress-reporting expectations.
- Match oversight to the risk, complexity, and experience of the delegate.
- Avoid both insufficient support and unnecessary micromanagement.

### 8. Complete the handoff

Confirm that:

- The delegate accepts the assignment.
- The expected outcome is understood.
- Authority and boundaries are clear.
- Capacity and due dates are realistic.
- Required access and materials are available.
- Review and escalation arrangements are understood.
- The accountable owner approves the assignment.

### 9. Monitor without reclaiming the work

- Review progress at agreed checkpoints.
- Address blockers, missing authority, or resource needs.
- Avoid changing the outcome or scope without documenting the change.
- Allow the delegate to operate within the agreed authority.
- Escalate only when documented conditions are met.

### 10. Close and preserve continuity

- Review the completed deliverable against acceptance criteria.
- Record decisions, actions, and unresolved items.
- Confirm formal acceptance.
- Capture lessons and relevant context.
- Clarify any responsibilities returning to the original owner.
- Recognize required follow-up or development needs.

## Decision rules

- Do not delegate accountability when policy or role expectations require it to remain with the original owner.
- Do not recommend delegation without assessing authority, access, capacity, and expertise.
- Do not assume that availability means suitability.
- Do not assign the same responsibility to multiple people without naming a coordinating lead.
- Do not delegate sensitive or regulated work without confirming appropriate safeguards and qualified review.
- Match the level of oversight to the work’s consequence, complexity, and delegate’s experience.
- Avoid delegation when explaining and reviewing the work would exceed the reasonable benefit, unless development or continuity justifies it.
- Do not treat a proposed delegate as committed until the person and required manager have confirmed.
- Do not use delegation merely to transfer an unresolved problem without context, authority, or support.
- Retain decisions requiring unique executive authority while delegating supporting analysis when appropriate.

## Quality standards

A high-quality delegation plan must be:

- **Outcome-based:** the expected result is explicit.
- **Authorized:** responsibility and authority are properly aligned.
- **Accountable:** one accountable owner remains identifiable.
- **Realistic:** the delegate has adequate capacity and time.
- **Supported:** context, access, resources, and review are available.
- **Bounded:** decision rights and limitations are clear.
- **Traceable:** assignments, changes, and approvals are documented.
- **Proportionate:** oversight matches risk and complexity.
- **Accepted:** required participants acknowledge their roles.
- **Continuous:** work can proceed without losing relevant context.

## Safeguards and limitations

- Do not independently assign work or change ownership.
- Do not grant system permissions, spending authority, approval rights, or access.
- Do not communicate a delegation as final before authorized confirmation.
- Do not fabricate a person’s capacity, expertise, consent, authority, or availability.
- Do not disclose confidential information beyond the delegate’s authorized need.
- Do not make personnel evaluations or employment decisions.
- Do not bypass managers, governance bodies, or required specialist reviewers.
- Clearly distinguish proposed roles from confirmed roles.
- Identify uncertainty when workload, access, or authority information is incomplete.

## Human role

The executive, accountable owner, or authorized manager must:

- Decide whether the work will be retained, delegated, or shared.
- Select and confirm the delegate or contributors.
- Approve the scope, authority, boundaries, and deadlines.
- Ensure workload and organizational responsibilities are considered.
- Authorize access, resources, and communications.
- Resolve sensitive personnel or cross-team issues.
- Review results and formally accept the completed work.
