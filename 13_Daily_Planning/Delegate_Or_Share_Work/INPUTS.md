# Inputs

## Work item context

For each work item being considered for delegation or shared ownership:

- Clear description of the required outcome.
- Related strategic goal, initiative, project, meeting, or operational responsibility.
- Requestor, sponsor, current owner, and accountable leader.
- Due date, milestone date, or response deadline.
- Current status and work already completed.
- Estimated effort, complexity, and urgency.
- Consequence of delay, incomplete work, or unsuccessful delivery.
- Source and date of the request or commitment.

## Delegation objective

- Reason delegation or shared ownership is being considered.
- Expected benefit, such as capacity, expertise, development, continuity, or faster delivery.
- Portion of the work proposed for delegation.
- Responsibilities that must remain with the executive or current owner.
- Expected deliverables and completion criteria.
- Required level of autonomy.
- Required review, approval, or check-in points.

## Potential delegates and contributors

For each potential participant:

- Name, title, role, and organizational unit.
- Relevant expertise, experience, and current responsibilities.
- Available capacity and competing commitments.
- Existing authority and decision rights.
- Access to required information, systems, tools, and stakeholders.
- Relationship to the initiative or work item.
- Development goals or learning opportunities.
- Known constraints, conflicts of interest, or separation-of-duty requirements.
- Manager approval or cross-team agreement required.

## Authority and accountability

- Accountable owner who retains responsibility for the outcome.
- Decisions the delegate may make independently.
- Decisions requiring consultation or approval.
- Financial, operational, contractual, security, privacy, clinical, legal, or personnel limitations.
- Escalation pathway and response expectations.
- Required approvers and reviewers.
- Conditions under which authority returns to the original owner.

## Required context and materials

- Background information and purpose of the work.
- Relevant prior decisions and commitments.
- Scope, boundaries, and out-of-scope items.
- Required inputs, reference materials, templates, and source documents.
- Stakeholder map and communication expectations.
- Known risks, issues, assumptions, and dependencies.
- Quality standards and acceptance criteria.
- Confidentiality, data classification, and distribution restrictions.

## Work plan and coordination

- Required tasks and proposed sequence.
- Deliverables, milestones, and target dates.
- Dependencies on other people or teams.
- Required meetings or collaboration points.
- Review and feedback cadence.
- Progress-reporting method.
- Contingency plan if the delegate becomes unavailable.
- Handoff or reintegration requirements.

## Capacity and workload information

- Executive or current-owner workload.
- Potential delegate’s existing priorities.
- Time required to explain, support, and review the delegated work.
- Availability of backup resources.
- Work that may be postponed, combined, or reassigned.
- Effect of the delegation on other commitments.
- Evidence that the proposed workload is realistic.

## Source and data quality

- Source for role, authority, availability, and expertise information.
- Date the capacity information was last confirmed.
- Distinction between confirmed assignments and proposed assignments.
- Missing, outdated, conflicting, or unverified information.
- Assumptions about skills, capacity, access, or authority.
- Items requiring confirmation from the individual, manager, or accountable owner.
