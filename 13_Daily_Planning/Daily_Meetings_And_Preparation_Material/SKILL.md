# Skill: Daily Meetings And Preparation Material

## Purpose

This skill reviews the executive’s daily calendar and supporting information to prepare concise, source-linked materials for each important meeting. It connects agendas, prior discussions, decisions, actions, risks, participants, and initiative context so the executive can enter each meeting prepared to contribute, decide, question, or align.

The skill prepares meeting intelligence. It does not make decisions, approve recommendations, communicate executive positions, or distribute materials without human review.

## Core concepts

### Meeting preparation versus meeting summary

- **Meeting preparation** is forward-looking and helps the executive participate effectively in an upcoming meeting.
- **Meeting summary** is retrospective and records what occurred.
- Prior summaries may inform preparation, but they should not replace a clear statement of what must happen next.

### Meeting objective versus agenda

- The **objective** explains why the meeting is occurring and what outcome is needed.
- The **agenda** identifies the topics and sequence.
- A meeting may have an agenda but still lack a clear decision or outcome.

### Decision, alignment, and information

Each meeting should be classified according to its primary purpose:

- **Decision:** authorized participants must choose or approve a course of action.
- **Alignment:** participants must establish a shared understanding or direction.
- **Information:** participants receive or clarify material information.
- **Working session:** participants jointly develop or resolve something.
- **Relationship or stakeholder engagement:** the primary objective is communication, trust, or coordination.

### Readiness

- **Ready:** required materials, authority, participants, and evidence are available.
- **Partially ready:** some preparation is available, but material gaps remain.
- **Not ready:** the meeting cannot reasonably achieve its intended outcome.
- **Awaiting information:** readiness depends on a named input not yet available.

### Source-linked preparation

Material claims, prior decisions, commitments, risks, and recommendations should identify their supporting source. Unsupported interpretations must be labeled clearly.

## Core activities

### 1. Build the daily meeting inventory

- Review all meetings scheduled for the planning date.
- Confirm time, location, objective, attendees, and executive role.
- Identify overlapping meetings and insufficient preparation time.
- Distinguish confirmed meetings from tentative or incomplete events.

### 2. Determine preparation priority

Evaluate each meeting according to:

- Decision significance.
- Strategic or operational importance.
- Executive-specific authority or contribution.
- Consequence of inadequate preparation.
- Risk, issue, or stakeholder sensitivity.
- Complexity of the material.
- Proximity to a deadline or milestone.
- Availability and quality of supporting information.

Assign a preparation priority and explain the basis for it.

### 3. Collect relevant context

For each meeting:

- Review the agenda and available pre-reads.
- Retrieve relevant prior discussions and decisions.
- Identify open actions and commitments.
- Connect the meeting to its initiative, charter, deliverables, and milestones.
- Identify relevant risks, issues, dependencies, and stakeholder concerns.
- Exclude unrelated context that does not help achieve the meeting objective.

### 4. Establish continuity

- Explain what led to the meeting.
- Identify decisions and commitments that remain in effect.
- Show what has changed since the previous discussion.
- Connect today’s expected outcome to subsequent meetings, actions, and milestones.
- Flag contradictions between current proposals and prior decisions.

### 5. Identify decisions and alignment needs

- State each decision or alignment question clearly.
- Confirm the decision owner and required authority.
- Present available options and material trade-offs.
- Identify required evidence, reviewers, and dependencies.
- Distinguish a recommendation from an approved direction.
- Explain the consequence of delay or no decision.

### 6. Prepare executive questions

Develop questions that help the executive:

- Test important assumptions.
- Confirm facts, evidence, and confidence.
- Clarify ownership, authority, and accountability.
- Understand cost, timing, value, and risk.
- Surface missing stakeholder perspectives.
- Identify dependencies and implementation constraints.
- Establish measurable next steps.

Questions should be specific to the meeting rather than generic leadership prompts.

### 7. Develop talking points

- Create concise, evidence-supported messages.
- Connect the message to the meeting objective.
- Distinguish confirmed institutional positions from suggested language.
- Identify sensitive issues or claims requiring caution.
- Avoid scripting commitments the executive has not approved.

### 8. Assess meeting readiness

Check whether:

- The objective and expected outcome are clear.
- Required participants will attend.
- Decision authority is present.
- Materials are current and complete.
- Options and trade-offs are sufficiently developed.
- Risks and dependencies are visible.
- Prior decisions and actions are accurately represented.
- Preparation time is sufficient.

Flag deficiencies and recommend corrective action.

### 9. Prepare follow-through

- Anticipate likely actions, decisions, and communications.
- Identify appropriate owners and deadlines for confirmation.
- Include a method for recording decisions and commitments.
- Show how the meeting outcome affects subsequent work.
- Do not record anticipated actions as actual commitments before the meeting occurs.

## Decision rules

- Prioritize preparation for meetings involving high-consequence decisions, material risks, or unique executive authority.
- Do not treat a recurring meeting as low priority without evaluating its current agenda.
- Do not present an old decision as current if later evidence may have superseded it.
- Do not infer agreement from silence, attendance, or the absence of documented objections.
- Label unsupported recommendations and assumptions.
- If required authority or evidence is missing, classify the meeting as partially ready or not ready.
- Recommend postponement only when the readiness gap materially prevents the intended outcome.
- Preserve distinctions among decisions, recommendations, proposals, commitments, and discussion points.
- Use only information relevant to the selected meeting and authorized audience.

## Quality standards

A high-quality preparation package must be:

- **Relevant:** focused on the meeting’s actual objective.
- **Concise:** optimized for rapid executive review.
- **Complete:** includes prior context, current state, decisions, risks, questions, and follow-up.
- **Traceable:** material claims connect to identifiable sources.
- **Current:** reflects the latest available information.
- **Decision-ready:** makes authority, options, trade-offs, and consequences clear.
- **Consistent:** aligns with prior decisions and commitments or explains deviations.
- **Actionable:** identifies what the executive should know, ask, decide, or confirm.

## Safeguards and limitations

- Do not fabricate meeting objectives, participant positions, decisions, actions, risks, or evidence.
- Do not portray a proposed recommendation as an approved executive position.
- Do not send or distribute briefing materials without authorization.
- Do not expose restricted meeting information to an unauthorized audience.
- Do not make clinical, legal, security, privacy, compliance, or financial determinations requiring qualified review.
- Do not infer decision authority solely from job titles.
- Do not automatically modify the calendar or meeting invitation.
- Clearly identify missing information, conflicting sources, and low-confidence conclusions.

## Human role

The executive or authorized reviewer must:

- Confirm the meeting objective and desired outcome.
- Validate sensitive context, recommendations, and talking points.
- Confirm decision rights and organizational positions.
- Resolve conflicting or incomplete information.
- Approve any communication, commitment, or distribution.
- Correct the preparation package before it is used in an executive setting.
