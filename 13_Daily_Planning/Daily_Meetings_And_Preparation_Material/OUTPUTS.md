# Outputs

## Daily meeting preparation overview

- Chronological list of the day’s meetings.
- Executive role and expected outcome for each meeting.
- Preparation priority: critical, high, standard, or minimal.
- Estimated preparation time.
- Current readiness: ready, partially ready, not ready, or awaiting information.
- Immediate preparation actions required.

## Meeting preparation brief

For each meeting:

### Meeting at a glance

- Title, time, location, and duration.
- Objective and desired outcome.
- Executive’s role.
- Key participants and their roles.
- Related initiative, priority, or operational area.

### Why this meeting matters

- Strategic or operational importance.
- Relevant deadline, milestone, risk, or commitment.
- Consequence if the intended outcome is not achieved.
- Connection to prior and upcoming meetings.

### Prior context

- Relevant prior discussions.
- Decisions already made.
- Commitments and actions still open.
- Previous areas of agreement or disagreement.
- Material changes since the previous discussion.

### Decisions and alignment needed

- Decisions expected during the meeting.
- Matters requiring alignment rather than formal approval.
- Options and significant trade-offs.
- Recommended executive position, when supported by evidence.
- Authority or specialist-review limitations.

### Key facts and evidence

- Concise facts needed for informed participation.
- Current status, milestones, measures, and trends.
- Relevant financial, operational, technical, governance, or stakeholder information.
- Source and date for each material claim.
- Confidence level and important data limitations.

### Risks, issues, and dependencies

- Material risks and issues.
- Current mitigation or resolution status.
- Dependencies that could affect the decision or outcome.
- Warning or escalation conditions.
- Consequence of delay or inaction.

### Recommended questions

- Questions to validate assumptions.
- Questions to clarify ownership and authority.
- Questions about evidence, risk, cost, timing, and dependencies.
- Questions that surface disagreements or missing stakeholder perspectives.
- Questions that establish next steps and accountability.

### Talking points

- Suggested opening context.
- Key messages the executive may wish to communicate.
- Evidence supporting each message.
- Sensitive language or unsupported claims to avoid.
- Closing summary and requested outcome.

### Actions and follow-up

- Anticipated actions and owners.
- Required deadlines and review points.
- Decisions that must be recorded.
- Communications that may be needed after the meeting.
- Upcoming meetings or milestones affected by the outcome.

## Preparation queue

- Meetings ordered by urgency and preparation requirement.
- Materials to review for each meeting.
- Estimated preparation time.
- Missing inputs and person responsible for providing them.
- Recommended preparation deadline.
- Items requiring specialist or executive confirmation.

## Cross-meeting continuity report

- Topics appearing in multiple meetings.
- Decisions in one meeting that affect another.
- Repeated or conflicting commitments.
- Shared risks, dependencies, participants, or deliverables.
- Information that should be carried forward.
- Potential sequencing conflicts or inconsistent messages.

## Readiness and exception report

- Meetings without a clear objective or agenda.
- Missing or outdated preparation materials.
- Decisions lacking required evidence or authority.
- Open actions without an owner or due date.
- Conflicting information across sources.
- Insufficient preparation time.
- Items requiring escalation, rescheduling, or human judgment.

## Source and confidence record

- Documents and records reviewed for each meeting.
- Date and freshness of each source.
- Confirmed facts, assumptions, and interpretations.
- Missing or conflicting evidence.
- Items requiring human validation before use.
