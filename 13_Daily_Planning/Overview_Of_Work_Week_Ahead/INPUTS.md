# Inputs

## Weekly planning context

- Week start and end dates, time zone, and working-day assumptions.
- Executive role, responsibilities, and decision authority.
- Current strategic priorities and leadership objectives.
- Major organizational, operational, or external conditions affecting the week.
- Planned leave, travel, reduced availability, holidays, or protected focus periods.
- Priorities, decisions, actions, and unresolved items carried forward from the previous week.

## Weekly calendar

For each scheduled event:

- Meeting title, date, start time, end time, and location or virtual platform.
- Organizer, attendees, and participant roles.
- Meeting objective and desired outcome.
- Executive’s expected role: decision-maker, sponsor, presenter, contributor, reviewer, observer, or optional attendee.
- Related initiative, strategic objective, milestone, or operational responsibility.
- Required preparation and follow-up time.
- Agenda, pre-read, or briefing materials.
- Whether the event is fixed, flexible, tentative, recurring, delegable, or optional.
- Travel, transition, and setup requirements.
- Confidentiality or distribution restrictions.

## Weekly outcomes and priorities

For each proposed weekly priority:

- Required outcome.
- Related strategic goal, initiative, program, commitment, or responsibility.
- Business or operational value.
- Sponsor, accountable owner, and supporting participants.
- Due date or relevant milestone.
- Executive action required: decide, approve, review, communicate, sponsor, prepare, or unblock.
- Current status and readiness.
- Estimated executive effort.
- Consequence of delay or non-completion.
- Source and date of the priority.

## Decisions and approvals

- Decisions expected during the week.
- Decision owner and approving authority.
- Decision deadline and related meeting.
- Options, recommendations, and trade-offs.
- Required evidence and specialist review.
- Current readiness: ready, partially ready, not ready, or awaiting information.
- Consequence of delay or no decision.
- Dependencies and affected stakeholders.

## Deliverables and milestones

- Deliverables due during or shortly after the week.
- Initiative or program association.
- Owner, status, due date, and completion criteria.
- Required executive review, approval, or sponsorship.
- Predecessor and successor milestones.
- Resource, schedule, or quality concerns.
- Evidence supporting the reported status.
- Upcoming checkpoints affected by this week’s work.

## Actions and commitments

- Open actions and commitments assigned to the executive or leadership team.
- Source meeting, decision, or request.
- Owner, due date, and status.
- Overdue or blocked actions.
- Dependencies and required support.
- Commitments to external or high-impact stakeholders.
- Items that may be delegated, deferred, combined, or completed asynchronously.

## Risks, issues, and blockers

- Material risks and issues likely to affect the week.
- Owner, current rating, status, and trend.
- Mitigation, containment, or resolution actions.
- Warning and escalation thresholds.
- Upcoming dates or events that may increase exposure.
- Executive decision, authority, or resource required.
- Consequence if the matter is not addressed during the week.

## Stakeholder engagements

- Stakeholders requiring a meeting, response, update, decision, or follow-up.
- Stakeholder role, interest, influence, and current concerns.
- Existing commitments and communication deadlines.
- Sensitive relationships or messages requiring executive participation.
- Engagements that may be delegated.
- Required preparation and desired outcome.

## Capacity and workload

- Available working hours for each day.
- Existing calendar utilization.
- Estimated preparation, meeting, focus-work, and follow-up time.
- Current team capacity and key participant availability.
- Known workload conflicts.
- Work that can be delegated, deferred, shortened, or combined.
- Contingency capacity for emerging requests or incidents.

## Cross-week continuity

- Prior decisions affecting the upcoming week.
- Work unfinished from the previous week.
- Upcoming meetings or milestones beyond the week that require early preparation.
- Dependencies spanning multiple weeks.
- Decisions or commitments that must carry into the following week.
- Recurring patterns or unresolved items requiring leadership attention.

## Source and data quality

- Calendar source and last synchronization date.
- Source for every priority, deadline, decision, risk, and commitment.
- Distinction between confirmed events and tentative plans.
- Missing, outdated, conflicting, or unverified information.
- Assumptions used for effort, timing, priority, or readiness.
- Items requiring executive, owner, or specialist confirmation.
