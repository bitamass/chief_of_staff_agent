# Inputs

## Planning context

- Planning date, time zone, and available working hours.
- Executive role, current responsibilities, and decision authority.
- Daily objectives and expected outcomes.
- Preferred working patterns, focus periods, and meeting constraints.
- Required breaks, travel time, and personal or accessibility considerations.

## Calendar information

For each calendar event:

- Meeting title, date, start time, end time, and location or virtual platform.
- Meeting objective and expected outcome.
- Organizer, attendees, and participant roles.
- Executive’s role: decision-maker, contributor, presenter, sponsor, or optional attendee.
- Required preparation materials and preparation time.
- Agenda, pre-read, prior decisions, and related action items.
- Whether attendance may be delegated, shortened, combined, moved, or declined.
- Confidentiality, sensitivity, or distribution restrictions.

## Priorities and commitments

- Strategic goals and current executive priorities.
- Initiative milestones, deliverables, and deadlines.
- Decisions that must be made or advanced during the day.
- Open commitments and action items assigned to the executive.
- Requests awaiting review, approval, response, or escalation.
- Time-sensitive risks, issues, and stakeholder needs.
- Commitments carried forward from previous days.

## Work requirements

- Estimated duration for each task.
- Due date and consequence of delay.
- Required level of concentration.
- People, information, or approvals needed.
- Dependencies and sequencing requirements.
- Whether the work must be performed by the executive or can be delegated.
- Status: not started, in progress, blocked, awaiting response, or complete.

## Preparation and follow-up requirements

- Meetings requiring briefing materials, talking points, or decisions.
- Questions the executive should ask.
- Required reviews before each meeting.
- Follow-up time needed after meetings.
- Decisions, commitments, and action items likely to result.
- Owners and deadlines associated with expected follow-up.

## Scheduling constraints

- Fixed versus flexible calendar events.
- Protected focus time and no-meeting periods.
- Travel, transition, and setup time.
- Scheduling conflicts and double bookings.
- Working-hour limitations and participant availability.
- Deadlines that cannot be moved.
- Events that require specialist, leadership, or governance participation.

## Source and data quality

- Calendar source and last synchronization time.
- Source for each priority, deadline, commitment, and meeting requirement.
- Identification of tentative, outdated, conflicting, or incomplete calendar information.
- Assumptions used to estimate preparation or task duration.
- Items requiring confirmation from the executive or meeting organizer.
