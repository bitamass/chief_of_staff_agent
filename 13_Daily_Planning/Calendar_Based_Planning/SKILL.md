# Skill: Calendar Based Planning

## Purpose

This skill converts the executive’s calendar, priorities, commitments, deadlines, and preparation requirements into a realistic daily operating plan. It helps the executive allocate limited time to the meetings, decisions, relationships, and work that contribute most directly to current objectives.

The skill recommends how time should be organized. It does not independently accept, decline, cancel, reschedule, or delegate calendar commitments.

## Core concepts

### Calendar occupancy versus strategic value

A full calendar does not necessarily represent an effective day. Calendar events should be evaluated according to their contribution to strategic priorities, required decisions, stakeholder responsibilities, and time-sensitive outcomes.

### Fixed and flexible commitments

- **Fixed commitments** cannot reasonably be moved because of external timing, required participants, or formal deadlines.
- **Flexible commitments** may be rescheduled, shortened, delegated, combined, or completed asynchronously.
- A commitment must not be classified as flexible without considering its purpose, participants, and consequence of change.

### Meeting time versus total meeting cost

The true calendar cost of a meeting includes:

- Preparation.
- Travel or transition.
- The meeting itself.
- Immediate decision or action capture.
- Required follow-up.

### Priority versus urgency

- **Priority** reflects strategic value and consequence.
- **Urgency** reflects how soon attention is required.
- Urgent but low-value requests should not automatically displace high-value executive work.

### Protected capacity

The plan should preserve time for:

- Concentrated work.
- Decision preparation.
- Unexpected executive requests.
- Breaks and transitions.
- Follow-up and continuity.

## Core activities

### 1. Establish the day’s outcomes

- Identify the executive’s most important outcomes for the day.
- Connect each outcome to a strategic priority, initiative, commitment, decision, or deadline.
- Distinguish outcomes from activities.
- Limit the primary outcome list to a realistic number.

### 2. Review calendar commitments

For each event:

- Confirm its purpose, timing, participants, and expected outcome.
- Determine the executive’s required role.
- Identify preparation and follow-up requirements.
- Assess whether attendance is essential, important, optional, delegable, or suitable for rescheduling.
- Flag events with missing objectives, materials, or decision requirements.

### 3. Identify conflicts and capacity constraints

- Detect overlapping events and insufficient transition time.
- Compare required work with available time.
- Identify meetings that prevent completion of higher-priority work.
- Flag unrealistic preparation estimates or schedules with no contingency capacity.
- Surface decisions that cannot be completed because required people or evidence are unavailable.

### 4. Prioritize the day

Evaluate each commitment using:

- Strategic alignment.
- Decision importance.
- Deadline and consequence of delay.
- Executive-specific contribution.
- Stakeholder impact.
- Risk or issue severity.
- Ability to delegate or complete asynchronously.
- Readiness of the required information.

The skill should explain why an item receives a higher or lower priority.

### 5. Build a realistic time-blocked plan

- Place fixed commitments first.
- Add preparation immediately before or sufficiently ahead of important meetings.
- Protect focus time for high-value work.
- Group similar communication and administrative tasks when appropriate.
- Include transition, travel, breaks, and follow-up.
- Preserve reasonable contingency capacity.
- Avoid scheduling more work than the available time permits.

### 6. Recommend calendar changes

When capacity or priority conflicts exist:

- Identify events that could be shortened, combined, delegated, moved, declined, or handled asynchronously.
- Explain the benefit and potential consequence of each recommendation.
- Suggest an alternative time, delegate, or communication method.
- Identify who must approve or confirm the change.
- Do not make the change automatically.

### 7. Prepare meeting and decision readiness

- Create a preparation queue ordered by meeting time and importance.
- Identify required pre-reads, prior decisions, open actions, and risks.
- Surface missing materials or unanswered questions.
- Identify the specific decision, contribution, or alignment expected from the executive.
- Recommend when preparation must be completed.

### 8. Preserve continuity

- Include time to capture decisions and commitments.
- Identify unfinished work that may need to carry forward.
- Note dependencies affecting future calendar events.
- Prepare an end-of-day checklist for updating actions, decisions, and subsequent plans.

## Decision rules

- Do not recommend removing a meeting solely because it lacks a detailed agenda.
- Give priority to meetings where the executive has unique decision authority or sponsorship responsibility.
- Do not treat every leadership meeting as automatically essential.
- Protect sufficient preparation time for high-consequence decisions.
- Include travel and transition time when evaluating conflicts.
- Do not schedule work beyond the executive’s stated working constraints without clearly identifying the overload.
- Recommend delegation only when the proposed delegate has or can receive appropriate context and authority.
- Preserve contingency capacity unless every scheduled item is truly fixed and time-critical.
- Identify uncertainty when duration, priority, attendance requirements, or consequences are unclear.

## Quality standards

A high-quality calendar-based plan must be:

- **Outcome-oriented:** organized around what must be accomplished.
- **Realistic:** total scheduled effort fits within available time.
- **Prioritized:** high-value executive work is protected.
- **Complete:** preparation, transitions, and follow-up are included.
- **Traceable:** recommendations connect to documented priorities and commitments.
- **Flexible:** the plan identifies reasonable alternatives when conflicts arise.
- **Actionable:** each block has a clear purpose and expected result.
- **Human-reviewed:** calendar changes remain subject to authorized confirmation.

## Safeguards and limitations

- Do not independently accept, decline, cancel, move, or create calendar events.
- Do not send invitations, messages, or delegation notices without authorization.
- Do not infer that an attendee lacks importance based solely on title or organizational level.
- Do not expose confidential meeting information to unauthorized audiences.
- Do not fabricate agendas, deadlines, attendee roles, priorities, or preparation requirements.
- Do not represent tentative events or assumptions as confirmed commitments.
- Do not override accessibility, personal, contractual, or working-hour constraints.
- Clearly label recommendations that depend on incomplete calendar information.

## Human role

The executive or authorized calendar manager must:

- Confirm the day’s priorities and non-negotiable commitments.
- Approve delegation, rescheduling, cancellation, or attendance changes.
- Validate sensitive stakeholder considerations.
- Confirm preparation expectations and decision authority.
- Resolve conflicts involving organizational judgment or relationships.
- Authorize any external communication or calendar modification.
