# Inputs

## Task origin and context

- Source of the proposed task, such as a meeting, decision, request, commitment, email, risk, issue, initiative, or planning session.
- Source date and reference.
- Relevant strategic goal, initiative, program, deliverable, or operational responsibility.
- Requestor, sponsor, accountable owner, and affected stakeholders.
- Background information needed to understand why the task is required.
- Related prior decisions, commitments, assumptions, risks, and dependencies.

## Required outcome

- Clear description of the result the task should produce.
- Business or operational reason for the task.
- Observable completion or acceptance criteria.
- Required format, quality standard, or evidence of completion.
- Consequence of delay, non-completion, or inadequate completion.
- Whether the task supports a decision, meeting, milestone, deliverable, risk response, or stakeholder commitment.

## Ownership and participation

- Proposed task owner.
- Accountable person for the resulting outcome.
- Contributors, reviewers, approvers, and people to consult or inform.
- Required expertise, role, or authority.
- Current owner capacity and competing priorities.
- Whether the work can be delegated or shared.
- Manager or cross-functional authorization required.
- Any separation-of-duty or conflict-of-interest considerations.

## Timing and priority

- Proposed start date.
- Due date or completion window.
- Relevant milestone, meeting, or decision deadline.
- Estimated duration and level of effort.
- Urgency and consequence of delay.
- Priority relative to existing work.
- Whether the date is confirmed, requested, estimated, or proposed.
- Required review or approval time before final completion.

## Scope and boundaries

- Work included in the task.
- Activities or decisions explicitly out of scope.
- Required level of detail.
- Geographic, organizational, technical, operational, or data boundaries.
- Decisions the task owner may make.
- Decisions requiring consultation or approval.
- Prohibited actions or authority limitations.

## Dependencies and prerequisites

- Predecessor tasks or decisions.
- Required information, documents, systems, tools, or access.
- Required participants or approvals.
- Related tasks and downstream deliverables.
- Known blockers and unresolved assumptions.
- External vendor or partner dependencies.
- Conditions that must be met before the task can begin or finish.

## Risks and sensitivity

- Risks associated with performing or not performing the task.
- Security, privacy, legal, compliance, clinical, financial, personnel, procurement, or reputational considerations.
- Data classification and handling requirements.
- Required specialist review.
- Escalation conditions.
- Confidentiality and distribution limitations.

## Existing task comparison

- Current open tasks with similar wording, outcome, owner, or source.
- Prior tasks that were completed, canceled, superseded, or deferred.
- Related action items or commitments.
- Potential duplicate, conflicting, or overlapping work.
- Existing task identifier, if this is an update rather than a new task.

## Source and data quality

- Source for the outcome, owner, deadline, and priority.
- Distinction between confirmed assignments and proposed task attributes.
- Missing, outdated, conflicting, or unverified information.
- Assumptions used to draft the task.
- Confidence level for effort and completion estimates.
- Items requiring confirmation before the task is activated.
