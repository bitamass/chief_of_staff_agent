# Skill: Update Existing Tasks

## Purpose

This skill reviews new information about an existing task and prepares a controlled, traceable update to its status, ownership, scope, priority, timing, dependencies, risks, completion criteria, or disposition. It preserves the task’s history while showing what changed, why it changed, who must approve the change, and how the change affects related work.

The skill prepares proposed updates. It does not modify official task records, reassign work, change commitments, close tasks, or communicate changes without authorized human approval.

## Core concepts

### Progress update versus task change

- A **progress update** records what has occurred without changing the approved outcome, owner, scope, priority, or deadline.
- A **task change** modifies one or more approved task attributes.
- Progress should not be used to conceal a material change in scope or timing.

### Update versus new task

An existing task should usually be updated when:

- The required outcome remains substantially the same.
- The change clarifies or adjusts how the same outcome will be achieved.
- Ownership, timing, priority, or status changes without creating a separate deliverable.

A new task should usually be created when:

- The required outcome materially changes.
- New work can be completed and tracked independently.
- A scope addition has a different owner, deadline, or acceptance criterion.
- Combining the work would obscure responsibility or completion.

### Current state versus proposed state

- The **current state** is the last authorized task record.
- The **proposed state** reflects a requested but not yet approved change.
- The proposed state must not overwrite the current state until authorization is confirmed.

### Status versus evidence

A status label is a claim that should be supported by evidence:

- **Not started:** no substantive work has begun.
- **In progress:** work has begun and evidence of activity exists.
- **Blocked:** progress cannot continue because of a named constraint.
- **Awaiting information:** progress depends on a specified input.
- **Ready for review:** the deliverable is complete enough for review.
- **Complete:** acceptance criteria are satisfied and required acceptance is documented.
- **Deferred:** an authorized decision moved work to a later date or condition.
- **Canceled:** an authorized decision ended the work.
- **Reopened:** previously closed work requires additional authorized action.

### Change traceability

Every material update should preserve:

- The prior value.
- The proposed or approved new value.
- The reason for the change.
- The source.
- The requestor.
- The approving authority.
- The effective date.

## Core activities

### 1. Locate and validate the task

- Confirm the task identifier and official source record.
- Review its current title, outcome, owner, status, priority, dates, and completion criteria.
- Identify linked initiatives, decisions, commitments, risks, and tasks.
- Confirm that the proposed update applies to the correct task and version.

### 2. Interpret the update request

- Identify exactly what the request proposes to change.
- Determine whether the source explicitly requested the change or merely provided new information.
- Separate confirmed facts from suggested interpretations.
- Identify who requested the update and whether that person has appropriate authority.
- Ask for clarification when the proposed change is ambiguous.

### 3. Assess current progress

- Review evidence of work completed.
- Compare progress with milestones and completion criteria.
- Identify remaining work.
- Assess variance from the prior schedule, scope, or effort estimate.
- Avoid assigning a percentage complete without a defined measurement basis.
- Distinguish active progress from waiting, review, or blocked time.

### 4. Determine update type

Classify the proposal as:

- Status update.
- Ownership change.
- Priority change.
- Schedule change.
- Scope clarification.
- Scope expansion or reduction.
- Outcome change.
- Dependency update.
- Risk or blocker update.
- Completion-criteria change.
- Closure, cancellation, deferral, or reopening.
- Supporting-context update.

Multiple change types may apply to one request.

### 5. Determine whether to update or create a new task

- Compare the current and proposed outcomes.
- Assess whether the new work has distinct ownership, timing, or completion criteria.
- Identify whether the update would make the original task too broad.
- Recommend separating materially different work.
- Preserve links between the original task and any new task.

### 6. Validate ownership and authority

- Confirm the current owner and accountable person.
- Assess any proposed ownership change.
- Verify the proposed owner’s authority, capacity, access, and acknowledgment.
- Identify required manager or cross-team approval.
- Do not infer acceptance from meeting attendance or copied communication.
- Preserve the current owner until the change is authorized.

### 7. Assess schedule and priority changes

- Compare current and proposed dates.
- Identify the reason and consequence of the change.
- Evaluate dependencies and downstream impact.
- Confirm whether the proposed date is realistic.
- Assess whether the priority change aligns with documented criteria.
- Identify stakeholder commitments affected by the revision.

### 8. Assess scope and outcome changes

- Identify exactly what is added, removed, or clarified.
- Evaluate effect on effort, resources, quality, risk, and timing.
- Update completion criteria as needed.
- Confirm that removed work is assigned elsewhere, intentionally canceled, or no longer required.
- Recommend a new task when a distinct outcome emerges.

### 9. Synchronize related records

Identify updates needed in:

- Initiative or program plans.
- Milestones and deliverables.
- Decision and approval records.
- Risk and issue registers.
- Meeting preparation materials.
- Related predecessor and successor tasks.
- Stakeholder commitments.
- Daily or weekly plans.

Do not update those records automatically without authorization.

### 10. Validate closure, cancellation, or reopening

For proposed closure:

- Confirm that completion criteria are satisfied.
- Verify acceptance evidence.
- Identify unresolved exceptions.
- Confirm the authorized closer.

For cancellation or deferral:

- Confirm the decision and rationale.
- Assess downstream impact.
- Preserve completed work and context.

For reopening:

- Identify the new evidence or requirement.
- Define remaining work and revised acceptance criteria.
- Confirm authorization.

### 11. Produce a traceable change proposal

- Show current and proposed values.
- Explain the reason and impact.
- Identify unresolved questions.
- State the required approver.
- Recommend approval, revision, separation, merging, or rejection.
- Prepare a change-history entry for use after human authorization.

## Decision rules

- Do not overwrite the current task state with an unapproved proposed change.
- Do not mark a task complete without evidence that its acceptance criteria are satisfied.
- Do not interpret “work was sent” as final acceptance.
- Do not infer a new owner without explicit acknowledgment and required authorization.
- Do not change a confirmed deadline solely because the task is behind schedule.
- Do not hide scope expansion inside a progress update.
- Create or recommend a separate task when the proposed work has a materially distinct outcome.
- Preserve the task history when correcting inaccurate information.
- Do not delete prior values or source references.
- Flag conflicts between the task update and existing decisions, commitments, or milestones.
- Clearly label dates, status, ownership, and priority as proposed until approved.

## Quality standards

A high-quality task update must be:

- **Accurate:** based on current evidence.
- **Specific:** clearly identifies each changed attribute.
- **Traceable:** preserves prior values, source, rationale, and authorization.
- **Authorized:** identifies the person who may approve the change.
- **Consistent:** aligns linked tasks, decisions, milestones, and commitments.
- **Evidence-based:** status and completion claims have support.
- **Impact-aware:** downstream effects are visible.
- **Non-destructive:** historical context is preserved.
- **Actionable:** required confirmation and next steps are clear.
- **Human-reviewed:** no official change occurs automatically.

## Safeguards and limitations

- Do not independently modify, reassign, close, cancel, reopen, merge, or delete tasks.
- Do not fabricate progress, completion evidence, owners, dates, priorities, approvals, or rationales.
- Do not communicate a proposed update as final.
- Do not infer acceptance from silence or the absence of objections.
- Do not conceal overdue work by changing dates without documenting the original commitment.
- Do not remove unresolved risks, blockers, dependencies, or exceptions.
- Do not disclose sensitive task information to unauthorized audiences.
- Do not make legal, clinical, security, privacy, compliance, financial, procurement, or personnel determinations.
- Clearly distinguish confirmed information from proposed changes and assumptions.

## Human role

The authorized task owner, accountable leader, or manager must:

- Validate the reported progress.
- Approve changes to scope, outcome, ownership, priority, or dates.
- Confirm completion, cancellation, deferral, or reopening.
- Resolve conflicts with related commitments or tasks.
- Authorize updates to the official tracking system.
- Communicate material changes to affected stakeholders.
- Confirm that the change history accurately records what occurred.
