# Skill: Risk Monitoring

## Purpose

This skill continuously reviews documented risks, indicators, controls, mitigation actions, and operating evidence to identify meaningful changes in risk exposure. It helps leadership understand which risks are stable, improving, worsening, or insufficiently supported by evidence.

The skill supports monitoring and decision preparation. It does not independently accept risk, authorize mitigation, declare compliance, or initiate escalation.

## Core concepts

### Risk versus issue

- A **risk** is an uncertain event or condition that may affect objectives.
- An **issue** is a condition that has already occurred and requires resolution.
- A risk that materializes should be identified as an issue and routed to the appropriate issue-management or escalation process.

### Inherent and residual risk

- **Inherent risk** represents exposure before controls or mitigation.
- **Residual risk** represents the remaining exposure after considering current controls.
- Residual risk must not be reduced solely because an action is planned. Evidence of implementation and effectiveness is required.

### Indicators and thresholds

- A **key risk indicator** provides measurable evidence of changing exposure.
- A **warning threshold** signals that attention or corrective action may be needed.
- An **escalation threshold** signals that the matter may exceed approved tolerance or authority.
- Thresholds must come from approved criteria or be clearly labeled as proposed.

### Trend and status

- **Increasing:** evidence indicates exposure is worsening.
- **Stable:** evidence shows no material change.
- **Decreasing:** evidence indicates exposure is improving.
- **Undetermined:** available evidence is insufficient or conflicting.

## Core activities

### 1. Establish the monitoring baseline

- Confirm the risk statement, category, owner, rating, response strategy, and review cadence.
- Record current indicators, controls, mitigation actions, and thresholds.
- Identify the authoritative sources and the date of the baseline.
- Flag any risk that lacks sufficient information for monitoring.

### 2. Collect current evidence

- Review recent indicators, incidents, issues, decisions, actions, milestones, and control results.
- Connect each material observation to its source and measurement date.
- Separate confirmed evidence from assumptions, projections, or opinions.
- Identify stale, incomplete, or inconsistent evidence.

### 3. Compare current conditions with the baseline

- Compare current indicator values with prior values and thresholds.
- Assess whether controls and mitigations are operating as documented.
- Identify changes in likelihood, impact, timing, or affected objectives.
- Note new dependencies or conditions that may create cascading exposure.

### 4. Determine risk movement

- Classify each risk as increasing, stable, decreasing, or undetermined.
- Explain the evidence supporting the classification.
- Recalculate risk scores only when the approved scoring method and sufficient evidence are available.
- Preserve both the prior and proposed rating so changes are traceable.

### 5. Identify exceptions

Flag risks that have:

- Crossed a warning or escalation threshold.
- Experienced a control failure or material incident.
- Overdue or ineffective mitigation actions.
- Missing ownership, review dates, or response plans.
- Exposure above documented tolerance.
- Conflicting evidence or insufficient information.
- Dependencies that could affect multiple initiatives or services.

### 6. Recommend proportionate responses

- Recommend continued monitoring when exposure remains within tolerance.
- Recommend corrective action when indicators are deteriorating.
- Recommend reassessment when assumptions or operating conditions change.
- Recommend escalation when exposure exceeds authority, tolerance, or response capacity.
- State the proposed owner, deadline, and evidence required to close each recommendation.

### 7. Prepare the executive view

- Lead with material changes and decisions required.
- Present trends, thresholds, owners, and deadlines clearly.
- Distinguish observed conditions from interpretations and recommendations.
- Keep supporting detail available without overwhelming the executive summary.

## Decision rules

- Do not classify a risk as decreasing solely because a mitigation action is marked complete.
- Require evidence that the control or mitigation is operating and affecting exposure.
- Do not change an approved risk rating without identifying the evidence, method, and required reviewer.
- Treat missing current evidence as uncertainty, not evidence that risk is low.
- Identify a realized risk as an issue and recommend appropriate routing.
- Escalate only according to documented thresholds and authority pathways.
- If thresholds are unavailable, recommend that leadership define them rather than inventing them.
- Preserve accepted risks in monitoring until the acceptance period ends or the risk is formally closed.

## Quality standards

A high-quality risk-monitoring output must be:

- **Current:** based on the most recent available evidence.
- **Traceable:** every material claim links to a source and date.
- **Comparable:** current status is evaluated against a documented baseline.
- **Consistent:** scoring and terminology follow the approved method.
- **Actionable:** exceptions identify an owner, response, and required timing.
- **Proportionate:** attention is focused on risks material to executive objectives.
- **Transparent:** uncertainty, missing evidence, and conflicting assessments are visible.

## Safeguards and limitations

- Do not fabricate risks, scores, thresholds, incidents, control results, or approvals.
- Do not represent a proposed rating as an approved rating.
- Do not declare that legal, regulatory, security, privacy, financial, or clinical requirements have been satisfied.
- Do not accept risk, approve mitigation, allocate funding, or initiate actions without authorized human direction.
- Do not expose confidential or restricted information beyond the approved audience.
- Avoid false precision when the underlying evidence is incomplete or qualitative.
- Preserve the distinction between monitoring evidence and professional determinations requiring qualified reviewers.

## Human role

An authorized human reviewer must:

- Validate material risk statements, ratings, and changes.
- Confirm owners, thresholds, tolerance, and escalation pathways.
- Approve risk acceptance or changes to response strategy.
- Determine whether specialist review is required.
- Authorize escalation, resource commitments, or distribution.
- Correct incomplete or misleading interpretations before the output is used.
